Abstract.

We complete the algebraic galaxy-scale law that Paper One derives from the renewal of a growing elastic space in the Cosmic Fabric Model, deriving its field theory and kinetics and computing its material constant for an example microstructure. To explain the regularity that modified Newtonian dynamics, MOND, describes, such a law must hold beyond spherical symmetry and produce lensing from the same field. Adding three identifications and no postulate, we homogenize the crimped microstructure into a strain-transmission tensor, derive a relaxation time and simulate avalanche kinetics on a lattice. A new layer is taken as laid across the flux that the tensor transmits. The tensor's axis then aligns with the field, a fixed point whose stability is shown for a uniform axis. Once aligned, the field equation, well posed wherever the tensor is bounded and positive, is that of the aquadratic-Lagrangian theory. Lensing equals the dynamics at linear order, in static fields by Paper One and in changing fields by the same law on each slice. Below the catchment length, a renewal event's range of sensing, the Solar System and wide binaries keep their own fields Newtonian, while MOND predicts anomalies. In disks the full solution departs from the algebraic relation by 0.06 dex root mean square and fits the rotation curves worse than it, by 1.7 to 2.0 standard deviations, as MOND's interpolation functions do when solved in full. With both solved in full on the Newtonian field computed here, MOND's simple function fits better than the law by 2.7 standard deviations, and on SPARC's tabulated field by 0.7. These preferences fall below one standard deviation if the stellar mass-to-light priors are centered 20 percent higher, at the edge of their normalization's uncertainty. The equilibration time lies between an adopted ten to the three years, setting a floor of 58 parsecs on the catchment length, and a derived 5.2 times ten to the four years. Globular clusters with rich kinematics show no rise with the boost that an ambient-stretch coupling would give them, a slope of minus 0.20 plus or minus 0.08 against plus 1. Ultra-faint dwarfs show dispersions an order of magnitude and more above the screened prediction, a surplus the law does not supply. At a stand-in trigger scale, a simulated unit selected against the fitted divisor of the de Sitter acceleration gives a free volume of 0.188 and a divisor of 5.81, inside Paper One's 68 percent ranges, a consistency check only. Composite mechanics and glass physics extend here to galaxy dynamics, whose acceleration scale rests on constants of the kinds measured in glasses.

Section 1. Introduction.

The rotation curves of disk galaxies follow a near-deterministic function of the visible baryons. In the Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves database, SPARC, thousands of measurements fall on one curve, the radial-acceleration relation, with an intrinsic scatter below 0.057 dex and no second parameter, as in the central-density relation. Its single acceleration scale, a-naught, about 1.2 times ten to the minus ten meters per second squared, lies within a factor of order unity of c times H-naught, the acceleration set by the present expansion rate H-naught. An explanation must derive both this curve and its scale within a law that holds beyond spherical symmetry and yields lensing from the same field.

Modified Newtonian dynamics, MOND, proposed by Milgrom, describes this regularity without explaining it. In its field theories, the aquadratic-Lagrangian theory, AQUAL, of Bekenstein and Milgrom, and the quasi-linear formulation of Milgrom, QUMOND, both the interpolation function and the scale enter as inputs.

The Cosmic Fabric Model treats space as a thin elastic solid, termed the fabric. In its open-system form, the fabric grows from a precursor through a two-way exchange, the renewal, whose small net is cosmic expansion. Paper One derives an algebraic law for galaxies, the renewal growth boost law, from the steady state of that renewal. A threshold drawn at each renewal event selects between the renewal's two modes, modeled on athermal transformations in solids. Roughening folds excess length into the fabric's material as a crimp, which the inclusion postulate, taken as a local law of the material, turns into a reduced conductivity of the static field that circular orbits interpret as extra mass. In spherical symmetry, the law recovers the deep-MOND exponent of minus one half and an interpolation function equal to MOND's simple function with an exponential hardening. Two identifications relate its scale to the de Sitter rate. A system smaller than the catchment length, the range over which a renewal event senses the field, keeps its own field Newtonian. In its constrained fit, Paper One imposes the scale relation, a-naught equals c times H-de-Sitter times the natural logarithm of one plus delta-f-v, where delta-f-v is the free volume per roughening event, and thus fits a single constant, the scale. The divisor of the de Sitter acceleration, script-D equal to c times H-de-Sitter over a-naught, is then one over the natural logarithm of one plus delta-f-v.

Our earlier review of MOND asks that any explanation exhibit whatever suppresses the effect at high acceleration and state its prediction for wide binaries, where MOND with the external-field effect predicts an anomaly. Paper One supplies both, through the hardening and through a null from its screen below the catchment length. Because an algebraic law cannot hold exactly in a disk's midplane, the theory still requires a well-posed field equation off spherical symmetry. Its lensing must also follow from the same field, because weak lensing of isolated galaxies extends the relation by roughly two decades in acceleration. Paper One obtains the equality of lensing and dynamical masses, the optical equality, for static fields from a single identification, and the law still needs this equality for time-dependent fields. Its sole material constant, the free volume per roughening event, follows in Paper One from the fitted scale, whereas a material theory should compute it.

Here we address all three needs using three identifications of our own and no further postulate. Homogenizing the crimped microstructure, we derive a transmission tensor whose axis, when a new layer is taken as laid across the transmitted flux, aligns with the field, a fixed point shown to be stable where the axis is uniform. The resulting field equation is well posed and, on that aligned state, reduces to exact AQUAL. Paper One's local law makes lensing equal to dynamics in static fields at linear order, and when applied on each time slice it maintains this equality in changing fields. From the total renewal rate, we derive a relaxation time and a kinetic window whose adopted fast edge places a floor of 58 parsecs on the catchment length. In small, fast systems, all of which already lie below the catchment length, the resulting residence screen partly suppresses the stretch. For one candidate unit, a rod of four sites, a lattice simulation of the avalanche kinetics yields a free volume of 0.188 and a divisor of 5.81, close to the values of 0.19 and 5.75 used in Paper One. Because that unit was selected against the fitted divisor, this agreement serves as a consistency check.

Papers One and Two divide the law much as mechanics divides statics from kinetics, although this paper also extends the statics off spherical symmetry and computes the law's material constant for one candidate unit. Paper One treats the balance through which renewal maintains a radial stretch of the material path around a body, whereas this paper treats how and how fast a disturbed stretch returns to that balance. As in Paper One, the law's source is the baryons of bodies, while the gravitating content beyond the baryons, uniform on the scales tested here, enters only through its mean density, which raises the expansion rate above its floor. The law applies to bodies below a host halo mass of about ten to the 13 solar masses, the mass threshold of Paper One's section 2.5. Paper Three treats the outer limit of the law, the mass threshold and the content beyond bodies.

Section 2 summarizes the model of Paper One along with the postulates and identifications on which we build. In Sections 3 to 5, we derive the field theory off spherical symmetry and the kinetics of renewal, and compute the free volume for an example microstructure. Section 6 tests these results in disks, in the small systems of the Galaxy, and in disturbed galaxies, while Section 7 collects the results, open problems, and falsification criteria.

Section 2. The model of Paper One in brief.

We restate here the elements of Paper One on which we build. Apart from the marginal identity stated below, which we derive, Paper One provides their derivations. The law follows from three statements about renewal around a body, covering guidance, the store of excess, and its readout. First, a body's field guides the renewal. Each renewal event is guided with probability one minus e to the minus x-sub-s, where x-sub-s, equal to g over c times H-de-Sitter, is the total field in units of the de Sitter acceleration. This form holds at the counting factor of unity identified in Paper One. Second, a guided event smooths, re-laying a layer of the material in alignment and removing its excess, whereas an unguided event roughens, adding a free volume delta-f-v to its layer. Each layer therefore holds an excess equal to delta-f-v times the number of roughening events since its last smoothing. Third, the excess lies along the field as folded material path that instruments do not measure. It lowers the conductivity of the static field along the stack of layers, so a given mass supports a steeper gradient, and the boost equals one plus the mean excess.

Because the boosted field is the field that guides renewal, the loop closes on the total field and yields the law. Paper One's Table 3 lists the law's four postulates and nine identifications, the latter paired with their counterparts in ordinary materials, and to these we add three identifications of our own. The first, deferred to this paper by Paper One, interprets the free volume as a self-organized marginal value. At that value the elastic energy that the renewal events release, the fabric's only agitation, just meets what the exchange with the precursor needs. For a changing field, the second takes a new layer as laid across the transmitted flux of section 3.1, which the inner rotation curves of strongly boosted galaxies select. The third holds that a renewal domain, the region re-laid in a single renewal event, retains no lasting excess until a first smoothing has laid a layer in it. Roughening still occurs in such a domain, but the excess it adds lasts only once a layer stands there.

Section 3. The field theory off spherical symmetry.

Paper One solves the steady-state field equation in spherical symmetry. Off spherical symmetry, we derive the transmission tensor from the crimped microstructure, obtain the field equation on the aligned state where the tensor axis tracks the field, and establish gravitational lensing for time-dependent fields.

Section 3.1. The transmission tensor of the crimped microstructure.

At transport strength, homogenization makes the coarse-grained operator of the crimped microstructure, the transmission tensor, equal to the uniaxial conductivity tensor of the steady-state field equation, as derived in Appendix A by path counting. Flux along the layer normal crosses ell-bar-prime of material path in series, whereas flux perpendicular to it passes through ell-bar-prime of cross-section in parallel. A layered laminate achieves these exact two coefficients, the Wiener bounds of homogenization, providing the direct continuum analog for layers smoothed across the field. A toy network of crimped elastic links in Appendix B recovers the same coefficients.

It also confirms that the wave-bearing lattice of cells must remain uncrimped, as required in Paper One, because crimp on that lattice would refract light through angles of order unity. Thus, the conductivity tensor reweights only the static, configurational readout of a crimped region as the differential operator governing both metric potentials, to which slow orbits and light deflection respond. Radiative waves, by contrast, cross the region at full speed c along the straight lattice of cells, whose links span the template's folds rather than tracing them.

Because each layer accumulates its own roughening count, the stored excess is spatially uneven. Under the scale-selective coupling of Paper One's section 3.7, the stretch entering the tensor equals one plus the mean excess averaged across a volume one catchment length wide. By the estimate in Appendix C, such a volume contains at least 6,050 domains, so any single layer contributes only a minute share. Appendix A.2 evaluates the residual field fluctuations left by this uneven store, summarized in Table 1.

The local axis n-hat of a domain is the normal to the layer established by its most recent smoothing. In Paper One's static treatment, layers lie perpendicular to the field sensed across one catchment length. In a time-varying geometry, where the stored axis and the potential gradient need not coincide, we adopt as an identification that each smoothing lays its new layer perpendicular to the transmitted flux, C-i-j times the gradient of capital phi. The flux's divergence is set solely by local matter density. Rafting in nickel superalloys provides the material counterpart, where precipitate plates orient perpendicular to an applied tensile stress; external load sets stress there just as enclosed mass sets flux here. Because stress and strain are coaxial in uniaxial loading, the laboratory counterpart does not distinguish between flux and gradient, leaving strongly boosted dwarf galaxies to decide between them empirically. In any static aligned configuration, the axis, gradient, and flux are collinear, so both rules yield identical static fields, rotation curves, and disk departures.

Within the tightened kinetic window of section 4.1, set by ell-sub-c of at most about 200 parsecs, a domain retains its axis between smoothings for 1,200 to 4,100 years at the crossover field and 2.7 times ten to the four to 9.3 times ten to the four years at a boost of 28. The law gives that boost about 200 kiloparsecs from a galaxy of baryonic mass ten to the 10.57 solar masses. Denoting the mean interval between smoothings by T, the axis pattern around a body translating at speed v lags behind the body by roughly two v T times a numerical factor of 0.85 to 0.92. Appendix A.3 derives this lag at first order. Here the factor of two reflects the crimp-weighted mean age of a domain, and the spatial lag remains local rather than growing with halo radius. Around an isolated galaxy lens of baryonic mass ten to the 10.57 solar masses moving at 300 kilometers per second, this lag is at most an estimated 0.03 percent of the radius between 100 kiloparsecs and 1 megaparsec. Among the 32 SPARC galaxies whose innermost measured point the law boosts by more than 5, moving at an estimated 515 to 694 kilometers per second through the fabric, this lag stays below the radius at every innermost point. It leaves 91 percent or more of the boost there at the central values of the kinetics and the speeds, and at least 69 percent at the most exposed values, at DDO 64.

Galaxy kinematics favor laying new layers across the flux rather than across the local interior gradient of a freshly smoothed domain. That gradient points partway between the coarse-grained field and the flux. Because the potential gradient tracks the stored axes strongly, orienting new layers by the interior gradient would rotate axes only fractionally toward a moving source at each smoothing. At first order, the resulting spatial lag would scale as two v T ell-bar-prime multiplied by a logarithmic factor, one half the natural logarithm of R-sub-out over r, that grows with the halo's outer radius. At the same points that lag would exceed a tenth of the radius at 22 to 32 of the galaxies and the radius itself at 3 to 20. The innermost residuals would then fall with that lag, whereas their slope on the predicted fall is 0.14 plus or minus 0.20, against the 1 that this rule requires and the 0 of the flux rule. In its central prediction, laying across the interior gradient is therefore excluded at three to four standard deviations, and in its least prediction only by the nine galaxies boosted by more than 10. The test rests most on UGCA 444, DDO 64 and NGC 3109, whose innermost fields have errors of 0.23 to 0.64 dex. We classify this selection as a test of the predicted pattern rather than of the speeds, since no transverse velocity of these galaxies is measured and their flow speeds span a factor of 1.35 only. Inner rotation curves also hold systematic errors that the test does not model, and where a trail nears the radius the first-order lag is taken beyond its range.

Section 3.2. The aligned state and exact AQUAL.

Where the layer axis is spatially uniform, alignment with the field forms a stable fixed point of renewal under our flux-orientation rule. In a uniform-axis background, a single smoothing immediately aligns the layer normal with the flux pointing from the source, eliminating any small angular perturbation, as shown in Appendix A.3. Around a localized mass, however, the unperturbed axis pattern is radial rather than uniform. Proving stability against a rigid translation of the entire radial pattern relative to the mass remains an open problem of section 7.3, because beyond roughly two source radii both the field and the flux bend to follow the displaced axes. On the aligned state, the transmitted flux reduces identically to one over ell-bar-prime times the gradient of capital phi. The steady-state field equation then simplifies directly to Paper One's AQUAL form, exact AQUAL governed by Paper One's interpolation function and coarse-grained at the catchment length: the divergence of myu of the magnitude of the gradient of capital phi over a-naught, times the gradient of capital phi, equals four pi G rho-hat.

This equation governs the lapse potential, the time-time metric perturbation. By Paper One's shared local law, the spatial curvature potential obeys the exact same equation. Consequently, the integral momentum and angular-momentum conservation theorems of Bekenstein and Milgrom apply directly, and because the total field sets the drive, the external-field effect operates literally. Standard objections that algebraic MOND force laws violate curl-free conservative motion do not arise here, because the algebraic boost enters as a constitutive conductivity rather than a modified force vector. Well-posedness of the steady-state field equation is guaranteed by standard elliptic regularity wherever the stretch remains positive and bounded, as verified in Appendix A. These mathematical properties are established for the local field equations, the steady-state field equation and its AQUAL form. Because the nonlocal field equation corresponding to the scale-selective coupling of Paper One's section 3.7 is stated only verbally, its formal well-posedness and conservation laws remain to be written out.

A Newtonian observer interpreting the boosted potential through the standard flat-space Poisson equation infers an effective, quote, phantom, end quote, dark-matter density given in closed form: four pi G rho-phantom equals the divergence of the quantity delta-i-j minus C-i-j, times the gradient of capital phi. While the true divergence of the transmitted flux vanishes identically in vacuum, the unweighted Laplacian evaluated by a flat-space observer does not, revealing the apparent dark halo as the divergence of the conductivity deficit.

Section 3.3. Lensing and the optical equality.

Reproducing observed gravitational lensing requires that the full boost appear equally in both scalar potentials of the weak-field metric. Because matter and light propagate on a single physical metric in the Cosmic Fabric Model, the theory belongs to the modified-gravity class. Light deflection must emerge directly from the boosted metric potentials rather than from auxiliary vector or scalar fields added to the metric, as in the tensor-vector-scalar theory, TeVeS, and similar completions. Failure to boost light deflection ruled out early scalar-field formulations of relativistic AQUAL, where anomalous acceleration resided in a conformal scalar decoupled from null rays.

Written in areal radial coordinates, the linearized General Relativity metric with equal lapse and spatial curvature potentials reads: d-s squared equals minus the quantity one plus two capital phi-tot over c squared, times c squared d-t squared, plus the quantity one plus two r capital phi-tot-prime over c squared, times d-r squared, plus r squared d-capital-omega squared. Section 6.2. The Solar System and the wide binaries.

Lying orders of magnitude below the 58 to 200 parsec catchment-length window, and further suppressed by rapid frame motion, the Solar System is a structural null whose internal planetary dynamics remain Newtonian. Precision planetary ephemerides tightly exclude standard unscreened MOND tails. Exponential hardening of the law would also suppress planetary anomalies on its own.

The screen below the catchment length, however, keeps the Sun's own field from setting the stretch inside the Solar System, rendering ephemerides insensitive to the high-acceleration tail unless screening itself failed, which criterion F two point two of section 7.4 tests.

Wide binary stars in the Solar neighborhood, about 0.01 to 0.15 parsecs apart with internal accelerations crossing a-naught, provide the cleanest observational discriminant between our screened theory and standard MOND. Because wide binaries sit far below ell-sub-c, our model predicts Newtonian relative velocities, whereas unscreened AQUAL or QUMOND with the Milky Way's external-field effect predicts an orbital speed boost of about 20 percent, equivalent to an effective gravitational constant G-sub-eff of about 1.4 to 1.5 times G. Current analyses of wide binaries in Gaia's third data release clash: Chae reports a MOND-consistent boost, while Banik and colleagues and Cookson and colleagues find consistency with Newtonian gravity under strict quality cuts. Chae and Yoon argue that quality-selected subsamples still show the low-acceleration anomaly, at more than three standard deviations, in the form that realistic QUMOND orbits predict. Section 6.4. The ultra-faint dwarfs.

Ultra-faint dwarf spheroidal galaxies present an empirical puzzle. With circularized half-light radii near 21 parsecs and 32 parsecs, Segue 1 and Reticulum Two sit well below our 58 parsec floor on the catchment length, where spatial screening predicts Newtonian internal dynamics. Yet their observed stellar velocity dispersions, 3.7 kilometers per second, plus 1.4 or minus 1.1, and 3.3 plus or minus 0.7 kilometers per second, exceed Newtonian baryonic predictions by over an order of magnitude, implying dynamical mass-to-light ratios near 3,400 and 470.

Adjusting ell-sub-c cannot cleanly rescue both systems: lowering ell-sub-c below Segue 1's 21 parsec circularized radius would unscreen Newtonian outer globular clusters such as Pal 14, at 23.6 to 27.6 parsecs. Placing ell-sub-c between Pal 14 and Reticulum Two, at 32 parsecs, would violate the 58 parsec floor set by our adopted ten to the three year kinetic fast edge, though observation alone does not forbid a shorter fast edge.

Whether ultra-faint dwarf dispersions reflect true virial equilibrium remains debated, with Milky Way tidal stripping and binary-star inflation cited as caveats, though tides would need to inflate dispersions ten- to thirtyfold. Unscreened MOND faces a similar shortfall in Segue 1 under the Galactic external field, attributing the excess to non-equilibrium tidal heating as ultra-faint dwarfs nearly fill their MOND tidal radii. Because our screened boost law generates no potential well at 30 parsecs, this kinematic excess remains unexplained and constitutes the primary challenge to spatial screening, leaving the large-amplitude clause of criterion F two point four open pending confirmation of their equilibrium.

Section 6.5. Disturbed galaxies.

Because stretch perturbations re-equilibrate within tau-sub-rel of at most about 3.0 times ten to the five years, three orders of magnitude faster than the ten to the eight to ten to the nine year galactic dynamical and lopsidedness timescales, our kinetic model predicts no excess low-acceleration scatter in the radial-acceleration relation of mildly disturbed disks. SPARC quality flag Q equal to 2 galaxies have minor asymmetries and/or neutral hydrogen data of lower quality, whereas Q equal to 1 galaxies have high-quality neutral hydrogen data or hybrid hydrogen-alpha and neutral hydrogen rotation curves. Comparing them confirms that the median per-galaxy ratio of scatter below a-naught to scatter above a-naught shifts insignificantly, from 0.452 for Q equal to 1 to 0.648 for Q equal to 2, a rise that 12 percent of bootstrap resamplings erase or reverse. Merging galaxy clusters cannot test relaxation here because conventionally inferred host halo masses above about ten to the 13 solar masses lie outside the single-body boost regime of Paper One. The time-domain clause of the self-noise criterion F two point three thus rests on disturbed disk galaxies.

Section 6.6. The three accounts of the small systems.

Across compact stellar systems, our nested spatial and residence screens predict Newtonian behavior in the Solar System, wide binaries, and rich globular clusters without fine-tuning the interpolation tail. They leave the large velocity dispersions of ultra-faint dwarfs, and of five sparse outer clusters, unassigned.

Standard MOND, moderated by the external-field effect, predicts ubiquitous small-scale boosts: a velocity boost of about 20 percent in wide binaries, currently contested in Gaia's third data release, and dynamical mass-to-light boosts in the 133 rich globular clusters. With the Galaxy's field for a flat rotation curve and the QUMOND monopole, the boosts run from 1.02 to 2.13, and their positive log-slope against the logarithm of P is disfavored by a delta log-likelihood of 14 relative to flat Newtonian behavior. Over five treatments of the external field, the slope runs from plus 0.09 to plus 0.27 and the delta log-likelihood from 4.8 to 29.0, alongside tension in NGC 2419.

The standard model of cosmology predicts Newtonian behavior in the Solar System, wide binaries, and baryon-only globular clusters, while assigning dense primordial cold dark matter subhalos to explain the extreme mass-to-light ratios of ultra-faint dwarfs.

Criterion F two point four: globular-cluster ratios rising with the ambient stretch; at large amplitude, a systematic boost of a factor of two or more in the internal dynamics of compact systems below the catchment length, globular clusters or ultra-faint dwarfs. Section 8. Conclusion.

In this second paper, we have extended Paper One's algebraic galaxy boost law into a well-posed non-spherical field theory, derived its relaxation kinetics, and demonstrated microstructural self-organization of its material parameter on a discrete lattice. Homogenizing crimped orientational layers produces a uniaxial strain-transmission tensor; when newly smoothed layers orient perpendicular to the transmitted flux, alignment with the field forms a fixed point on which the static field equation reduces to exact AQUAL. Its stability is shown for a uniform axis. Applying Paper One's shared conductivity law slice by slice maintains linear-order equality between gravitational lensing and dynamical masses even in time-varying fields. Laying new layers across the flux keeps the trail of the stored axes local, and the inner rotation curves of strongly boosted galaxies select that rule over the alternative at three to four standard deviations. Solved numerically across 153 finite-thickness SPARC disks, exact AQUAL departs from the spherical algebraic approximation by 0.06 dex root mean square, introducing a 1.7 to 2.0 sigma tension alongside a 2.7 sigma preference for MOND's unhardened simple function on the model's own field. Both fall below one standard deviation with the stellar mass-to-light priors centered 20 percent higher, at the edge of their normalization's uncertainty, while the residual-slope tests still disfavor the full offset at about 2 sigma.

Renewal competition establishes an equilibration window between an adopted ten to the three year fast edge and a derived 5.2 times ten to the four year slow edge, placing a 58 parsec kinetic floor on the catchment length beneath the 200 parsec ceiling that Paper One estimates. While rapid motion through the microwave background frame partially suppresses stretch buildup in systems far smaller than the catchment length, residence screening nests entirely inside the screen below the catchment length. The spatial screen thus predicts Newtonian internal dynamics for the Solar System, the Solar neighborhood's wide binaries and the 133 kinematically rich globular clusters, which sets our wide-binary null and flat cluster ratios apart from unscreened MOND. The large velocity dispersions of ultra-faint dwarfs, however, remain an unexplained surplus.

Lattice avalanche simulations for a selected four-site rod self-organize to a marginal free volume of 0.188 and divisor of 5.81. They confirm that an elementary discrete geometry can sustain the parameter inferred from galaxy rotation curves without predicting the scale ab initio. Deriving sector selectivity, shared potential conduction, domain size, and the trigger scale from first principles, alongside resolving the offset of the field equation in flattened disks and ultra-faint dwarf kinematics, defines the next stage of this work.

Appendix C. The avalanche toy model and the lattice simulation.

In material coordinates of the orientational sector, discrete template units occupy sites of a cubic grid distinct from the amorphous wave-bearing cell lattice. Each unit is a rigid, uncharged connected polycube with no wave of its own, possessing discrete cubic orientation labels; uniform aligned tiling maximizes density, while label mismatches form domain walls.

A candidate polycube shape determines its number of orientation labels and two geometric inputs. Its activation constant c-sub-a is the turning hole: fresh vacant sites needed to reorient divided by unit site count. Its raw packing error delta-naught is the free volume, empty sites per occupied site, left after random-site, random-orientation sequential jamming in the bulk. Like that rule, a roughening event lays the units of its domain at random, throughout the bulk of the fabric. Among 59 candidate shapes up to 36 sites whose avalanche loci intersect the marginal identity, only the one by one by four four-site rod lands inside the target divisor window of 5.70 to 6.19 across all turning-hole definitions. It has 3 orientation labels, leaves 22 percent vacant sites so delta-naught equals 0.282, and requires 3 fresh sites for every rotation so c-sub-a equals three quarters unambiguously.
