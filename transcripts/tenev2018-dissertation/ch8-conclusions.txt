Conclusions.

In this dissertation work, we explored the analogy between physical space and a material solid to understand how the structure of space itself affects the behavior and structure of matter within it. Fundamental to the ideas presented here was the proposition that the universe as a whole is a functional system and as such, it exhibits complexity and structure at multiple length scales. Furthermore, physical space, being a component of said system, must also exhibit structure at multiple length scales, whose effects can be measured and numerically simulated as it is done with components of ordinary mechanical systems.

Our approach was to first establish the theoretical basis for the material analogy of physical space, which we called the Cosmic Fabric Model. Next, we used this model to propose and numerically substantiate a solution to an outstanding problem in cosmology, namely, the explanation of galactic-scale gravitational anomalies. We referred to these as the Dark Matter effect, since they are currently attributed to the presence of invisible mass. We proposed the Inherent Structure Hypothesis as an alternative explanation for the Dark Matter effect. In support of this hypothesis, we first showed analytically that inherent spatial curvature can emulate the effects of dark matter distribution by amplifying the gravity of ordinary masses. Our analytical solution was limited to spherically symmetric configurations. To overcome this limitation, we built a numerical framework, which can test the Inherent Structure Hypothesis under a wide variety of configurations including asymmetric ones. Using this framework we performed several numerical experiments demonstrating that inherent spatial curvature does indeed extend the gravitational influence of ordinary matter and is able to produce the type of galactic rotation curves that had been traditionally used as indicators of dark matter.

Below we recount, in greater detail, the list of takeaway points, read as a list.

First, physical space is material-like. The behavior of physical space is like that of an elastic material subject to Hooke's Law. It can be modeled as the mid-hypersurface of a four-dimensional hyperplate that deforms within four-dimensional hyperspace. We called this hyperplate the cosmic fabric. The enclosing four-dimensional hyperspace, which we called the reference space, is not directly observable at continuum length scales, discussed further in Chapter Three.

Second, matter acts as inclusions. Matter acts as inclusions inducing prescribed strain that causes the fabric to bend. The induced volumetric strain epsilon relates to the matter-energy density rho of the inclusion by the equation: the Laplacian of epsilon equals minus c squared times kappa times rho, over two, where kappa is the Einstein constant and c is the speed of light. In the presence of inherent curvature, the Laplacian, written nabla squared, should be replaced by the more general covariant Laplacian, nabla-sub-i of nabla-superscript-i, discussed further in Chapter Three and Chapter Five.

Third, the cosmic fabric is the medium for matter-matter interactions. The cosmic fabric mediates matter-matter interactions by signals propagating through it. These are mechanical-like disturbances within the fabric whose speed, measured with respect to the reference space, depends on the fabric's mechanical properties and state, discussed further in Chapter Three.

Fourth, the time lapse rate depends on the signal speed. The rate of clock ticks within the fabric depends on the rate of interaction amongst the clocks' internal components, which in turn depends on the speed of signal propagation. The observed rate of clock ticks is inversely proportional to the volumetric stretch epsilon. Because clock rates specify the time components of the spacetime metric tensor, g-sub-myu-nyu, and clock rates themselves depend on the deformation state of the fabric, it follows that g-sub-myu-nyu is fully specified by the mechanical state of the fabric, discussed further in Chapter Three.

Fifth, there is an action equivalence. The action integral of the cosmic fabric, computed from energy considerations based on Hooke's Law, is equivalent to the Einstein-Hilbert action of spacetime, discussed further in Chapter Three.

Sixth, the gravity equations are analogous to plate-bending equations. The Einstein field equations of gravity are analogous to the plate-bending equations from thin plate theory, with Einstein's constant being analogous to the stiffness constant. The fabric's thickness is needed to offer resistance to bending, discussed further in Chapter Three.

Seventh, the signal speed appears invariant to all observers within the fabric, and equals c. Because signal speed affects clock rates within the fabric, the measurement of such speed within the fabric appears one and the same irrespective of the observer's reference frame. This observed signal speed is identified with the speed of light in vacuum, discussed further in Chapter Four.

Eighth, Lorentz transformations apply. Coordinate transformations of events within the fabric conform to Lorentz transformations, discussed further in Chapter Four.

Ninth, relativity applies. Although the fabric ostensibly defines a special rest reference frame, such a reference frame is undetectable from within the fabric based on just local observations at the continuum length scale. This undetectability of the special rest reference frame is also the explanation for the negative outcome of the Michelson and Morley experiment, discussed further in Chapter Four.

Tenth, this is a generalization of the Lorentz aether theory. At the continuum length scale, the Cosmic Fabric Model of gravity generalizes the Lorentz aether theory similarly to how General Relativity does Special Relativity.

Eleventh, there is a physically meaningful coordinate assignment. For an appropriate choice of coordinates, which was the type of coordinate choice used throughout this dissertation work, the h-sub-myu-nyu quantities from linearized gravity have the meaning of twice the strain components, that is, two times epsilon-sub-myu-nyu, which, being strain, has concrete physical meaning. By contrast, most applications of linearized gravity choose a type of coordinate assignment that makes mathematical expressions involving h-sub-myu-nyu simple, at the expense of obfuscating the direct physical meaning of these quantities, discussed further in Chapter Three.

Twelfth, the gravitational potential corresponds to volumetric strain. Under the weak-gravity regime, the classical gravitational potential, capital fai, relates to the volumetric strain epsilon, according to the equation: capital fai over c squared equals minus epsilon, discussed further in Chapter Three.

Thirteenth, the Poisson ratio is unity. The Poisson ratio of the cosmic fabric, nyu, equals one. Such a Poisson ratio implies that cosmic space has non-trivial, weave-like substructure, discussed further in Chapter Three and Chapter Five.

Fourteenth, there are no pressure waves. Because Poisson's ratio is unity, the pressure-wave modulus vanishes. Consequently, we expect that no pressure, or longitudinal, waves can propagate in the fabric, but only shear waves. This conclusion agrees with observations, since waves propagating in vacuum, such as gravity and electromagnetic radiation, all oscillate in directions perpendicular to the direction of propagation. The fact that there can be no pressure waves in space is also consistent with Birkhoff's theorem, which states in part that all spherically symmetric solutions of Einstein's field equations must be static, discussed further in Chapter Three.

Fifteenth, gravity waves act as shear waves in the fabric. Gravity waves are analogous to transverse waves in the fabric, and exhibit the same number of polarizations, just two. The latter conclusion can be derived from Solid Mechanics reasoning, discussed further in Chapter Three.

Sixteenth, there is a materialization of Flamm's paraboloid. The gravity of a stationary, spherically symmetric body can be understood in terms of the cosmic fabric bending into the shape of Flamm's paraboloid, discussed further in Chapter Five.

Seventeenth, there is a natural reparametrization of the Schwarzschild solution. A reparametrized version of the Schwarzschild metric can be derived for a static, spherically symmetric body from purely Solid Mechanics principles. It is equivalent to the Schwarzschild metric up to coordinate rescaling. Such rescaling does not affect General Relativity's use of the metric, because of coordinate-assignment freedoms. However, from a Solid Mechanics perspective, the particular choice of coordinates attributes additional physical meaning to the resulting metric, namely, that of describing the deformation of the fabric. Specifically, the metric derived from Solid Mechanics considerations shows that the fractional amount by which space is stretched volumetrically always equals the fractional amount by which the time lapse rate decreases, discussed further in Chapter Five.

Eighteenth, there is discrete substructure of matter. Investigating the fabric's membrane energy as due to matter-fabric interaction led to the conclusion that matter cannot exist in large, continuous chunks, but must be made up of small, discrete particles. This conclusion is consistent with our understanding of matter that comes from outside the field of General Relativity. Reaching such a conclusion illustrated the cross-length-scale aspect of the Cosmic Fabric Model. In this particular case, the thickness of the fabric, which is on the order of Planck's length, functioned as a length-scale parameter, discussed further in Chapter Five.

Nineteenth, there are length-scale effects. The Cosmic Fabric Model generalizes General Relativity by naturally introducing additional length-scale parameters, such as the fabric's thickness, at the minutest scale, and its inherent curvature at the galactic length scale and above. The former may be giving rise to quantum effects, while the latter, as we showed in Chapter Six, is likely the cause of the Dark Matter effect. Only at the continuum length scale is the Cosmic Fabric Model equivalent to General Relativity, because General Relativity is strictly a continuum-scale theory, discussed further in Chapter Four, Chapter Five, and Chapter Six.

Twentieth, the inherent structure of space causes the dark-matter effect. The presence of inherent curvature of space, one that is uncaused by matter, amplifies the gravity of ordinary bodies, and could manifest as the gravitational anomalies currently attributed to dark matter, discussed further in Chapter Six.

Twenty-first, a computational framework was introduced that is suitable for weak-gravity simulations in the presence of inherent curvature. We built a numerical framework by enhancing the particle-mesh method to take into consideration the non-flat geometry of space. Using this framework, and galactic density data found in the literature, we computed the rotation curve of the Triangulum galaxy, also known as M-thirty-three, and showed that the presence of inherent spatial curvature did result in a rotation curve consistent with the Dark Matter effect.
