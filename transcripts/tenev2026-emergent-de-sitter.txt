Abstract. This paper answers Green's objection to an elastic space without longitudinal waves, the instability of its negative bulk modulus, by treating that space as an open system that persists only through continual replenishment from outside itself. Raised in 1838 against the elastic theories of light, the objection applies to any material theory of space with a negative bulk modulus. The Cosmic Fabric Model, which treats space as a thin elastic hyperplate in a four-dimensional bulk, corresponds to General Relativity for the scalar curvature of space and the field of one body. It needs that answer, since keeping the fourth dimension unobservable requires a vanishing P-wave modulus. The open system combines finite-growth kinematics, a linear law for the exchange across a phase boundary with a higher-dimensional precursor fluid, and one new postulate, the Background Datum. Under it the fabric's placement in the bulk and every bend of its shape have no inertia and obey, on each slice, General Relativity's Hamiltonian and momentum constraints. Rather than being repaired, the instability drives a steady expansion, while accretion keeps the solid at its natural density and its moduli fixed. The interface kinetics act as a bulk viscosity that holds a small residual strain, and the expansion obeys General Relativity's Friedmann constraint with the gravitational constant of local gravity. The same kinetics set the constraint's constant term, an exact de Sitter floor above which matter raises the rate. Every flow of energy in the growth crosses the boundary between the fabric and its reservoir, so their total energy is conserved. In the ideal-reservoir limit the equation of state of the expansion's constant term is exactly w equals minus one, with no adjustable parameter. Here w is the ratio of pressure to energy density that a fit of the standard model reads as dark energy. Without replenishment the fabric would thin as it grows, raising the gravitational constant, which its stiffness sets, by about 2 times ten to the minus ten per year. Planetary ephemerides exclude that rise by more than three orders of magnitude and hold any shortfall of replenishment below about 4 times ten to the minus four, so the open system is required rather than chosen. An inertial bend would raise a moving body's inertia above its gravitational mass by about the square of the ratio r over L, within a radius r of a fabric of thickness L, so observation requires that bends have no inertia. Because a hypersurface of flat four-dimensional space cannot have uniform negative curvature, the curvature parameter satisfies capital omega-k less than or equal to zero. For engineering mechanics, the result shows how a solid of negative stiffness can be held at a fixed material state without a stiff surrounding structure, by replenishment across a phase boundary.

Section 1. Introduction.

The Cosmic Fabric Model describes gravity with continuum mechanics by postulating that physical space behaves as an elastic material. The model treats the physical universe as the mid-hypersurface of a four-dimensional hyperplate that is broad in the three ordinary spatial dimensions but thin along a fourth transverse spatial dimension. The original formulation and its 2019 extension established a correspondence between General Relativity and solid mechanics for the scalar curvature of space and for the field of one spherical body.

The elastodynamic approach to spacetime developed here builds on prior work connecting gravitation to vacuum elasticity, thermodynamics, and condensed-matter physics. Sakharov proposed that General Relativity could emerge from the elasticity of the vacuum driven by quantum fluctuations. Jacobson later derived Einstein's field equations as a thermodynamic equation of state. Concurrently, condensed-matter analogs such as the superfluid-helium models of Volovik, the World Crystal hypothesis of Kleinert, and recent quantized elastic-medium frameworks of Izabel have mapped Lorentz invariance and gravity to lattice and fluid dynamics.

Advancing the fabric model requires moving from a mathematical correspondence to an open-system thermodynamic framework to resolve a physical paradox. For the hyperplate to reproduce the geometry of physical space and to keep its four-dimensional thickness unobservable at the macroscopic scale, the fabric must have no stiffness against compression waves. This requirement dictates a vanishing P-wave modulus, M equals zero, corresponding to a Poisson ratio of exactly unity, nyu equals one. The fabric's volume is then anti-restoring, with a negative bulk modulus, K-naught less than zero, as the paragraphs below show.

In the original work, we cited synthetic, complex fibrous structures, such as compressive dilatant and stretch-densifying materials, to show that such a Poisson ratio is feasible. Each such material attains this Poisson ratio under one sign of volumetric loading only, the dilatant class in compression and the stretch-densifying class in extension. An isotropic vacuum that sustains general, three-dimensional gravitational strain requires a different physical foundation.

The requirement that M equals zero poses a thermodynamic problem. The fabric bears the shear load and its shear modulus sets the light cone, so the shear modulus must be positive, myu greater than zero. Because the bulk modulus is K, equal to M minus four-thirds myu, setting M equal to zero forces the intrinsic bulk modulus of the vacuum to be negative, K-naught less than zero. In the continuum mechanics of closed systems, a negative bulk modulus signals a thermodynamic instability, an unbounded volumetric expansion or implosion under a hydrostatic perturbation. Resolving it without geometric constraints that would break the correspondence with General Relativity requires an updated physical framework.

The condition and its difficulty predate the fabric model. In the elastic theories of light, Green required the longitudinal wave of the aether to be infinitely fast, because a medium in which that wave vanished would be unstable. Kelvin defended the opposite choice in 1888, setting the P-wave modulus to zero so that K equals minus four-thirds myu, and he called the resulting equilibrium labile. He held it stable with a rigid containing vessel and with restoring forces of third order in the strain, and Section 2.2 shows that neither means is available to a fabric that constitutes space. Hehl and Kiefer have since found Lamé constants that satisfy the same condition in General Relativity's DeWitt metric, read as an elasticity tensor. A recent survey of material theories of space lists Green's objection among the requirements that a modern construction must answer.

We address this paradox, and with it Green's objection, by treating the hyperplate, whose mid-hypersurface is three-dimensional space, as an open thermodynamic system rather than a closed equilibrium system. A continuous solid-liquid phase boundary with the surrounding four-dimensional precursor fluid maintains it. Instead of viewing the negative bulk modulus as a defect, we treat it as the intrinsic state of the fabric. The fabric is then an energetic solid, a material whose unstrained configuration is not an energy minimum with respect to volumetric deformation. Ongoing solidification replenishes the fabric from the precursor fluid, so the material state stays fixed while the volume grows. The fabric is therefore not self-sustaining, since in isolation it would thin as it grows, and it persists at a fixed state only through continual exchange with a reservoir that lies outside physical space. The rate of growth obeys the Friedmann constraint of General Relativity, the equation that sets the square of the expansion rate by the density of the universe's content. The constraint's energy of motion, its term in the square of the expansion rate, is negative for a change of volume, as the bulk modulus is. The phase transition sets the constraint's constant term, the square of the floor rate H-de-Sitter, which plays the role of the cosmological constant. By resolving this paradox, we establish a continuum basis for the universe's accelerated expansion.

This work makes three contributions. First, an open system resolves the negative bulk modulus paradox without repairing the instability. Green's objection is therefore accepted rather than evaded, since the instability remains and the open system makes it drive the growth while the Friedmann constraint sets its rate. Accretion keeps the fabric at its natural density, and the interface kinetics set the constant term of the expansion's Friedmann constraint, an exact de Sitter floor fixed by material constants, as Section 3 states. Given the Background Datum postulate, Sections 2.2 and 7.2 show the open system to be necessary by exhausting the elastic and closed-system alternatives. The measured constancy of the gravitational constant, which the equation of the vacuum moduli in Section 2.1 ties to the fabric's stiffness, confirms that necessity, since it excludes a fabric without replenishment by more than three orders of magnitude.

Second, the Background Datum postulate separates the fabric's configuration in the bulk, which holds energy but no momentum density, from the deviations that move within the fabric, to which inertia is confined. General Relativity's constraints, not the fabric's elasticity, then govern the uniform expansion and every bend of the fabric's shape, and the expansion obeys the Friedmann constraint with the gravitational constant of local gravity. Inertia does not apply to the fabric's motion in the bulk, namely the motion of its placement and of its bends normal to itself. Observation requires this, since an inertial bend would make a moving body's inertia exceed its gravitational mass, as Section 5.2 shows. Within the fabric, only the curl-free part of the material's motion, which moves in step with the bends, lacks inertia. Because the reservoir exchanges momentum only with motion that has inertia, the curl-free slip of the fabric against it, which a fabric holding matter cannot avoid, exerts no lateral force that would reveal the fabric's rest frame.

Third, the total energy of the fabric and its reservoir is conserved, since every flow of energy in the growth crosses the open boundary between them. The replenishment that holds the floor fixed makes the framework a mechanistic realization of the cosmological constant, as Section 7.3 shows.

The framework rests on a short list of assumptions. It inherits three postulates from the original formulation. The fabric is an elastic thin hyperplate, whose correspondence with General Relativity requires M equals zero and which sets the vanishing coupling, alpha equals zero, and the stiffness contrast across the thickness. Matter-energy fields are inclusions that prescribe volumetric strain, and the lapse rate of proper time follows the shear-wave speed. The present paper adds one postulate, the Background Datum of Section 3.2. It gives the fabric's configuration in the bulk an energy but no momentum density. The configuration's motion obeys General Relativity's Hamiltonian and momentum constraints. These are the four of Einstein's field equations that contain no second time derivative, and they relate the geometry, rate of strain and content of space at each instant. Two further assumptions are stated where they enter. The reservoir is ideal and static, of unbounded capacity and fixed state and with no flow of its own. The interface exchanges material in both directions, by attachment and detachment, with no threshold between them. Its net flux is then the near-equilibrium residual of two gross rates, linear in the driving potential by the flux-force law of Onsager, as Section 3.1 derives. The exchange's blindness to the static dilatation of inclusions is derived rather than assumed. The constraints hold the field of a static mass, and the exchange re-lays the fabric's natural configuration to match it within one relaxation time. After that the field neither drives growth nor relaxes, as Section 6.1 shows.

The exclusion of alternatives forces every other step of the argument. The framework's principal prediction concerns the equation of state of the expansion's constant term. In the standard model each component of the content has an equation of state w equals p over the quantity rho c squared, the ratio of its pressure to its energy density, which fixes how its density changes as the universe expands. Matter has w equal to zero and dilutes with the volume, radiation has w equal to one-third, and the cosmological constant lambda has w equal to minus one, so its density does not dilute at all. The dark-energy component of a fit to the expansion history is characterized by its w. Whether that value is exactly minus one or departs from it and evolves decides whether the component is lambda or a dynamical field. The floor gives w equal to minus one exactly in the ideal-reservoir limit, with no adjustable parameter. Section 9.3 collects the framework's three falsifiability commitments. The derivations are worked on a closed hyperspherical shell, the geometry that growth from a finite seed favors. Closure is a choice rather than a result, and none of the results above depends on it, as Section 4.1 states. The inherited geometric setting nevertheless excludes a fabric of uniform negative curvature, as Section 4.1 shows.

The paper presents its results before their grounds. Sections 2 and 3 state the results, from the paradox of the negative bulk modulus to the open-system resolution, with the equations and the postulate that each step requires. Section 4 sets out the working geometry. Sections 5 to 8 then give the derivations and grounds behind those results for the fabric, the interface, the reservoir and the shell in turn. Section 9 compares the framework with observation and the standard cosmological model. Section 10 discusses its limitations and open problems before concluding, and two appendices give the longer derivations.

Section 2. The negative bulk modulus paradox.

Section 2.1. Elastic constraints.

Before evaluating the thermodynamic mechanisms required to resolve the fabric's instability, we state the constraints on its elastic moduli. We model the cosmic hyperplate as a thin layer of a transversely isotropic four-dimensional continuum. To map its macroscopic deformation, we use a generalized Voigt notation in which the index 4 denotes the transverse direction along the thin dimension L. The four-dimensional symmetric strain vector, epsilon in brackets, is ordered as follows, and the four-dimensional material stiffness tensor, C in brackets, is characterized by independent elastic parameters. The strain vector is a column of ten entries, in this order: epsilon-one-one, epsilon-two-two, epsilon-three-three, epsilon-four-four, two epsilon-two-three, two epsilon-one-three, two epsilon-one-two, two epsilon-one-four, two epsilon-two-four, and two epsilon-three-four. The stiffness tensor is a ten-by-ten matrix whose rows and columns follow the order of the strain vector. In each of its first three rows, the first three columns hold lambda plus two myu on the diagonal and lambda elsewhere, and the fourth column holds alpha. The fourth row holds alpha in each of the first three columns and gamma on the diagonal. The fifth, sixth and seventh diagonal entries are myu, and the eighth, ninth and tenth diagonal entries are beta. All other entries are zero.

In this matrix, gamma represents the transverse normal stiffness, and beta denotes the out-of-plane transverse shear moduli. In this transversely isotropic context, the P-wave modulus is M, equal to lambda plus two myu, the Poisson ratio is nyu, equal to lambda divided by twice the quantity lambda plus myu, and the bulk modulus is K, equal to M minus four-thirds myu. To suppress longitudinal pressure waves and preserve the invisibility of the fourth dimension during uniform scaling, the P-wave modulus must vanish, M equals zero, which implies a Poisson ratio of exactly unity: M, equal to lambda plus two myu, equals zero, and nyu, equal to lambda divided by twice the quantity lambda plus myu, equals one.

The total volumetric strain epsilon is the local trace of the three-dimensional spatial strain tensor: epsilon equals epsilon-one-one plus epsilon-two-two plus epsilon-three-three.

Throughout this work, we distinguish two independent regimes of volumetric deformation. The first is the static, localized expansive strain, epsilon-loc, that local matter inclusions induce, as in the original formulation. The second is the homogeneous, global dilation driven by cosmic expansion. For the global mode, we adopt in Section 3.1 the multiplicative kinematics of finite-growth mechanics. They resolve the cosmic dilation into a stress-free growth Jacobian, J-g, tracking the volume created by accretion, and a small residual elastic dilation, epsilon-res, that alone generates stress. Throughout, epsilon-res denotes the instantaneous value of this residual dilation, a dynamical variable. Its steady-state value without matter, under the balance of Section 3.2, is written epsilon-res-star and is a constitutive constant of the interface. The inclusion strain epsilon-loc can be mapped as a function of the local coordinate metrics. This local dilation geometrically weakens the reference shear modulus myu-naught: myu equals the quantity one plus epsilon-loc, raised to the power minus three, times myu-naught.

This relationship belongs to the elastic description that corresponds to General Relativity for the scalar curvature of space and for the field of one spherical body. The vacuum values of the three-dimensional moduli, namely Young's modulus Y-naught, the shear modulus myu-naught and the bulk modulus K-naught, are defined as follows: Y-naught equals three c to the fourth, divided by pi G L squared; myu-naught equals three c to the fourth, divided by four pi G L squared; and K-naught equals minus c to the fourth, divided by pi G L squared.

Because M equals zero fixes the relation between the moduli, the vacuum is intrinsically unstable: K-naught equals minus four-thirds myu-naught, which is less than zero.

The first postulate of the original formulation makes each three-dimensional hypersurface of the fabric an isotropic elastic body obeying Hooke's law in its own strains. In the notation of the stiffness matrix, that postulate sets the cross-dimensional coupling parameter alpha to zero: alpha equals zero.

The condition is therefore inherited, and the invisibility of the fourth dimension under volumetric deformation requires it. Any non-zero alpha would couple uniform three-dimensional dilation to a change in the hyperplate's transverse thickness and so render the fourth dimension mechanically observable from within the continuum. It would likewise permit transverse disturbances to drive observable three-dimensional dilation. Transversely isotropic materials with alpha not equal to zero are mechanically admissible and stable whenever the stiffness matrix is positive definite. Neither stability nor symmetry therefore forces the vanishing coupling, which is instead a defining material property of the cosmic fabric. With alpha equal to zero, moreover, the in-plane block of the stiffness matrix reduces exactly to isotropic three-dimensional elasticity, so the isotropic definitions of nyu and K used above apply without correction.

Furthermore, we require that the four-dimensional transverse stiffness and interdimensional shear dominate the three-dimensional intrinsic shear stiffness, gamma much greater than myu-naught, which is greater than zero, and beta much greater than myu-naught, which is greater than zero. The requirement is the four-dimensional form of the Kirchhoff-Love assumption of the original formulation, that material normals stay normal through bending. These parameters remain finite but are treated as asymptotically large, a shielding of the fourth dimension rather than an empirical tuning. The stiffness contrast enforces a thin-plate limit that suppresses the four-dimensional mechanical degrees of freedom, so transverse perturbations cannot draw energy out of the three-dimensional continuum, and three-dimensional Lorentz covariance is preserved. Because the ratios gamma over myu-naught and beta over myu-naught are decoupled from spatial length scales, their large magnitude does not perturb the weak-field correspondence with General Relativity.

Throughout this paper, myu and nyu denote the shear modulus and the Poisson ratio, and lambda denotes the Lamé constant of the stiffness matrix. The calligraphic script-K, introduced in Section 3.1, denotes the kinetic mobility of the phase interface and is distinct from the bulk modulus K. The rate of strain K-i-j of Section 3.2 follows General Relativity's notation for the extrinsic curvature and is distinct from both. The coefficient lambda-K of its squared trace, introduced in Section 5.2, is distinct from the Lamé constant. The symbol w denotes the equation of state of the expansion's constant term, defined in Section 1. The symbol h denotes the shell's normal displacement in Sections 4.2 and 8.2 and in Appendix B. Its ratio to the shell's radius, capital ksai, equal to h over R, is the fractional amplitude of a bend. Finally, the operator delta applied to a quantity, as in delta epsilon-res or delta H, denotes a small perturbation of that quantity.

Section 2.2. The limits of elasticity.

Elasticity alone cannot resolve the instability of K-naught less than zero while maintaining the condition M equals zero. Any static geometric boundary constraints or competing elastic restoring forces would alter the effective moduli, violating the requirement nyu equals one and breaking the model's correspondence with General Relativity. Therefore, under the two premises stated at the end of this section, a resolution requires an open system.

That necessity rests on premises that are not adjustable modeling choices. The fabric model's defining requirements are inherited from the original formulation. They are the invisibility of the fourth dimension at the continuum scale and the correspondence with General Relativity for the scalar curvature of space and the field of one body. Invisibility under uniform scaling requires the P-wave modulus and the cross-dimensional coupling to vanish, M equals zero and alpha equals zero, as Section 2.1 states. With the positive shear modulus that Section 1 requires, the negative bulk modulus then follows algebraically, K-naught equals minus four-thirds myu-naught. Relaxing any of these requirements therefore compromises the model's premises rather than adjusting a parameter.

Given these premises, elasticity cannot arrest the instability, and we show this by exhausting the known stabilization mechanisms. First, constraint stabilization is unavailable. Lakes and Drugan argued that a stiff surrounding structure can stabilize a negative-stiffness phase. The only stiff structure in the model is the transverse one, with moduli gamma and beta, which the invisibility condition, alpha equals zero, decouples from three-dimensional dilation. Conversely, any cross-coupling strong enough to stabilize would render the thickness response observable and shift the effective in-plane moduli away from nyu equals one, so either choice violates a defining requirement. Second, strain-gradient and nonlocal regularizations stabilize only finite-wavelength perturbations, and the homogeneous dilational mode has no gradients and is untouched. Third, nonlinear stiffening of K as a function of epsilon would arrest the instability at a static post-buckled equilibrium whose moduli no longer satisfy nyu equals one, contradicting both the correspondence with General Relativity and the observed ongoing expansion. Kelvin held the labile aether stable by the first and third of these means, a rigid containing vessel and restoring forces of third order in the strain. Fourth, inertia does not arrest the dilation, since an inertial term would set only the time scale of the runaway and would supply no restoring force. In any case, the Background Datum postulate stated in Section 3.2 gives the fabric's collective motion no inertia against the bulk. The postulate's constraint, whose geometric energy of motion ties the rate to the content's energy, restores nothing either. Under that constraint alone the fabric would thin as it grows, and its moduli would drift with its energy density by order one per e-fold. The constraint would also admit no steady growth without a constant term.

The conclusion also rests on two further premises. The material constants must not drift, since the equation of the vacuum moduli ties the gravitational constant to the shear modulus. The constant term must also belong to the fabric rather than enter as an external cosmological constant, since this paper sets out to obtain it from the interface's kinetics. Under them the fabric must be an open system: some mechanism outside it must replenish it and supply that constant term, and that mechanism's dissipation sets the residual strain.

Section 3. The open-system resolution.

Section 3.1. Open-system accretion and phase transition kinetics.

To resolve the instability established in Section 2.1, we treat the fabric as an open system and introduce an accretion model. The four-dimensional embedding space functions as a fluid reservoir, while the physical three-dimensional universe is an embedded solid-state hyperplate, as Figure 1 shows. As the hyperplate expands in three dimensions, its negative bulk modulus offers no resistance to the dilation, which lowers the density of the fabric. This expansion draws the surrounding four-dimensional fluid across the interface at the fabric's two faces, where it solidifies into the lower-energy three-dimensional crystalline state. Because this liquid-to-solid transformation expands rather than contracts, we call it expansive solidification. Where the liquid-to-solid character of the transition matters, we state it explicitly. Water, which expands by roughly 9 percent on freezing, shows that solidification need not contract, although its expansion arises from directional bonding rather than from an anti-restoring bulk modulus.

Rather than depositing as distinct outer layers that would thicken the hyperplate into a solid four-dimensional sphere, the four-dimensional precursor fluid is drawn interstitially into the expanding three-dimensional lattice. This interstitial accretion continuously generates new three-dimensional volume while maintaining the local rest-mass density of the fabric, rho-naught. The transverse four-dimensional thickness of the shell remains constant, and that constancy is a balance that the two-way exchange stated below maintains, not a given. Attachment and detachment proceed at both faces of the hyperplate, and the two channels of the solid respond oppositely to a departure from the natural state. In the plane, the anti-restoring bulk modulus, K-naught less than zero, lowers the free energy of the solid as it dilates, so attachment runs ahead of detachment and the net flow creates three-dimensional volume. Along the fourth axis, the positive transverse stiffness gamma raises the energy of any material that attaches beyond the natural thickness, so detachment runs ahead of attachment there until the excess is gone. A site thinned by detachment draws attachment for the same reason. The thickness is therefore the fixed point of the transverse exchange, and the three-dimensional volume is the drift of the in-plane exchange. Because alpha equals zero, neither channel responds to the other: a dilation of the plate does not thin it, and re-laid material joins at the thickness of its neighbors. A departure of the thickness relaxes on the time scale of the gross turnover. That time is far shorter than the growth time, because growth is the small biased difference of the two directional fluxes.

We describe the mass accretion rate per unit current volume, rho-dot-acc, driven by the difference in specific free energy between the two phases, delta capital fai, equal to capital fai-fluid minus capital fai-solid: rho-dot-acc equals script-K times delta capital fai, where script-K is the kinetic mobility. The driving potential delta capital fai is the transformation driving force of single-component phase-change thermodynamics, which solidification theory commonly expresses as a chemical-potential difference and which we take per unit mass. The mobility script-K is the coefficient of a flux-force law, not a state variable of the material.

Physically, the accretion law is a near-equilibrium reduction of a two-way exchange, the attachment of precursor fluid to the lattice and the detachment of solid back into the fluid. The driving potential delta capital fai sets the ratio of the two rates, which equals one at equilibrium. When delta capital fai is small the net rate is linear in it and proportional to the gross rate at which either process runs. Onsager showed this for a reaction whose forward and reverse transitions balance at equilibrium. A linear relation between a flux and its driving force is the simplest kinetic law of irreversible thermodynamics, which Abeyaratne and Knowles set out for moving phase boundaries. Its coefficient, here script-K, is of Onsager type and cannot be negative, as the dissipation derived below confirms. The reduction has been derived explicitly for crystal growth. In the stochastic-hopping model of Patrone, Einstein and Margetis, the averaged flux into a step is proportional to the departure of the adjacent density of mobile atoms from its equilibrium value. Its coefficient is the kinetic coefficient of a step in the modern form of the Burton-Cabrera-Frank theory, and script-K is its macroscopic counterpart. The precedent concerns the form of the law rather than its origin. In crystal growth thermal agitation drives the individual events, whereas the fabric's interface has no temperature, as the Configurational Thermodynamics corollary of Section 5.1 states, and must therefore generate its own agitation.

The accretion law is accordingly the residual of a much larger two-way turnover rather than a one-way deposition. The paper assumes that both processes actually occur at the interface, rather than invoking them only to justify the form of the accretion law. Later results rest on this assumption, among them the fixed thickness of the fabric and the gross turnover of the interface. We return to the decomposition once the steady state is established.

To describe how this accretion generates space, we adopt the multiplicative kinematics of finite-growth, or morphoelastic, mechanics. The Jacobian J, defined as the determinant of F and equal to V over V-ref, the determinant of the deformation gradient F relative to the nucleated reference configuration, measures the local change of volume as the ratio of current to reference volume. Finite-growth theory splits the deformation gradient multiplicatively into a growth part and an elastic part, F equals F-e times F-g. The Jacobian factors correspondingly into a stress-free growth Jacobian, J-g, equal to the determinant of F-g, which measures the volume created by accretion, and an elastic Jacobian, J-e, equal to the determinant of F-e, which measures the recoverable elastic dilation: J, defined as V over V-ref, equals J-e times J-g, and J-e equals one plus epsilon-res.

The growth Jacobian J-g is stress-free by construction, because accreted material joins the lattice at the natural rest-mass density rho-naught. The residual elastic dilation epsilon-res measures the deformation of the current material away from its own stress-free state and is the sole source of elastic stress. Because each unit of accreted mass creates stress-free volume at the natural density, mass conservation across the open interface fixes the growth rate to leading order in epsilon-res: the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-g equals rho-dot-acc over rho-naught.

Sustaining solidification at a finite rate requires holding the interface out of equilibrium, with the free-energy difference, delta capital fai, as the driving potential and the inverse of the finite mobility, script-K, as the resistance. The power dissipated by the transition per unit volume is the product of the specific potential drop and the mass flux, delta capital fai times rho-dot-acc. Expressing this power as a stress acting through the volumetric growth rate, delta capital fai times rho-dot-acc equals sigma-diss times the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-g, by the growth-rate equation, identifies the dissipative stress conjugate to growth as sigma-diss equals rho-naught times delta capital fai. That stress is the energy dissipated per unit volume and per unit of logarithmic growth in maintaining the interfacial disequilibrium. Combining the accretion law and the growth-rate equation then gives delta capital fai equals rho-naught over script-K, times the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-g, so the kinetic mobility maps to an effective macroscopic bulk viscosity: sigma-diss equals zeta times the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-g, with zeta defined as rho-naught squared over script-K.

Because script-K greater than zero implies zeta greater than zero, the associated dissipation rate satisfies the condition that zeta times the square of the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-g is greater than or equal to zero, so the kinetic law is consistent with the second law of thermodynamics. We treat the interface kinetics as linear, consistent with the linear-response regime of continuum phase-boundary theory. While phase transitions can exhibit nonlinear drag under high interface velocities, the interfacial disequilibrium of the present expansion is small enough for the linear approximation to describe its macroscopic behavior.

Section 3.2. The governing balance and the Background Datum.

The negative intrinsic bulk modulus, K-naught equals minus the magnitude of K-naught, renders the stress-free state unstable. Any dilation of the nucleated solid generates a volumetric stress K-naught times epsilon-res, negative for dilation and hence pressure-like rather than restoring, so the bulk modulus offers no resistance to further dilation. In a closed system nothing replenishes the fabric, so it thins as it grows and its moduli drift, as Section 2.2 shows. In the open system, dilation of the lattice lowers its density and draws in the precursor fluid. The interface kinetics of Section 3.1 then convert elastic dilation into stress-free grown volume at a finite rate. Quasi-static stress equilibrium of the fabric, in which the elastic driving stress balances the dissipative interface stress, K-naught times epsilon-res plus sigma-diss equals zero, yields the governing balance: zeta times the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-g equals the magnitude of K-naught times epsilon-res.

The balance has no inertial term. This is not an overdamped approximation, which neglects a small inertial term, but an exact property of the uniform growth, which has no inertia at all. We adopt this absence of inertia as a postulate of the open-system framework, alongside the three postulates of the original formulation. The first of those postulates casts physical space as an elastic hyperplate and thereby makes gravity an emergent property of the fabric. The present postulate extends the same emergence to inertia, which attaches to the material's motion within the fabric rather than to the fabric's placement in the bulk.

Under the Background Datum postulate, the fabric's homogeneous configuration in the embedding space is the dynamical datum of the theory. It comprises the uniform density rho-naught, the residual strain and the configuration's trajectory through the embedding space. The datum holds energy but no momentum density, and no inertia or dissipation against the embedding space resists its motion normal to itself, however accelerated. Motion of the configuration along that trajectory is evolution of the datum itself. A bend of the configuration's shape has no inertia either. On each slice the configuration obeys General Relativity's Hamiltonian and momentum constraints, with the energy and momentum of the deviations as their source, and these constraints, rather than the fabric's elasticity, govern every bend. The irrotational part of the material's displacement has no inertia of its own, so it has no equation of motion of its own. It moves in step with the bend instead, at a rate set by the momentum constraint, which ties the fabric's rate of strain to the momentum of its content. Where the momentum constraint leaves that motion undetermined, as it is for the localized field of a single spherical body in flat space, nothing acts on it. The material then keeps the place in which it was laid, and the field is the bend alone. The bend's dilatation outside such a body then follows from the Hamiltonian constraint, so the exterior Poisson form of the inclusion postulate is a consequence of that constraint. Outside such a body the lapse reads the bend's own volumetric strain, as the inherited postulate states. For the uniform expansion, the Hamiltonian constraint becomes the Friedmann constraint, whose energy of motion is that of General Relativity, c squared over sixteen pi G, times the quantity K-i-j times K-i-j minus the square of K-k-k, in the rate of strain K-i-j, with the G that governs the deviations. The first-order interface kinetics of Section 3.1 set its constant term. That energy of motion is negative for a change of volume and is not an inertia against the embedding space. Inertia is an emergent property confined to the fabric. It attaches exclusively to the deviations that move within the fabric, namely the material's divergence-free motion, its transverse waves included, and the mass-energy inclusions. Deviation dynamics is defined relative to the co-moving, co-accelerating datum frame, and the static fields that inclusions hold are fixed on each slice and follow them at once. Motion of the material tangent to the fabric, relative to the reservoir, is slip. The reservoir exchanges momentum only with motion that has inertia, so any tangential traction it exerts acts on the divergence-free part of a slip, and an irrotational slip exerts none.

The term datum is used in its engineering and geodesy sense, a reference relative to which position and deviation are defined and which is not itself an object of measurement. It has no connotation of data. The postulate designates the homogeneous background as the reference entity of the dynamics rather than one of the deviations that it governs.

The terms slice and constraint come from the Hamiltonian, or three plus one, formulation of General Relativity of Arnowitt, Deser and Misner. That formulation divides spacetime into a sequence of three-dimensional spaces, one for each instant of time, and each such space is a slice. In the present model a slice is the fabric's configuration at one instant. Einstein's field equations then separate into two groups. The evolution equations contain second time derivatives, and their transverse part describes gravitational waves. The four constraints contain no second time derivative, so they hold on every slice as relations among the slice's geometry, its rate of strain and its content, rather than as laws of motion. The Hamiltonian constraint balances the energy of motion of space, written in its rate of strain K-i-j, against the energy density of the content and the intrinsic curvature of the slice. Its uniform part is the Friedmann constraint, stated in Section 3.3. The three components of the momentum constraint tie the divergence of the rate of strain, less the gradient of its trace, to the content's momentum density.

The constraints complement the fabric's elasticity rather than contradict it. Any motion of the material splits into an irrotational, or curl-free, part, which dilates or compresses without swirling, and a divergence-free part, which swirls without compressing. On the closed shell the split is unique. At M equals zero the elastic force of a displacement u is the curl, minus myu-naught times the curl of the curl of u, so elasticity exerts no force on the irrotational part and supplies no law for its motion. Elasticity would also make a bend with inertia unstable, as Appendix B shows. The uniform dilation, the bends and the irrotational motion have no inertia, so their law of motion reduces to a balance on each slice, and the constraints state that balance. With no rate of strain, the Hamiltonian constraint ties the scalar curvature of the slice to the energy density of the content, the correspondence that the fabric's static elasticity already gives. Only the constraints' terms in the rate of strain are therefore new, and Section 5.2 states their footing.

The postulate also requires a reading of the transverse wave-speed relation, c equals the square root of myu-naught over rho-naught, in which rho-naught plays no bulk-inertial role. Relativistic continuum mechanics supports that reading in any case, because in a relativistic medium wave speeds are ratios of elastic moduli to the energy density per c squared, not to a Newtonian rest-mass density. The denominator is strictly the energy density plus pressure, and for the fabric the pressure is the residual stress, smaller than the energy density by a factor of order epsilon-res. We write u-naught, defined as rho-naught c squared, for the fabric's rest energy density, reserving the symbol u for energy density. The kinetic coefficient of the deviation wave equation is then the intrinsic quantity u-naught over c squared, a property of in-fabric dynamics with no reference to motion through the bulk. The wave-speed relation becomes the constitutive statement that the shear modulus equals the energy density, myu-naught equals u-naught. This is a principled condition rather than a tuning. For any relativistic medium, causality bounds the rigidity by myu less than or equal to u, since the shear-wave speed can be at most c. The condition therefore states that the fabric is maximally rigid, as stiff as causality permits, and transverse waves then propagate at exactly c. A restoring modulus equal to the energy density is likewise the elastic expression of the equation of state w equals minus one, to which the framework is independently committed, as Section 7.2 shows. The wave equation of the original formulation for the material's transverse waves is unchanged, with rho-naught, defined as u-naught over c squared, kept as notation for the rest energy density in units of mass.

The postulate has two corollaries. Under the first, Configurational Thermodynamics, the precursor and the interface have no temperature, and under the second, Datum Energy, the energy that growth lays sources no curvature. Section 5 states both. It also gives the grounds of the postulate's clauses on the energy of motion and on slip, and consequences that use the floor derived below and the working geometry of Section 4.

Section 3.3. The de Sitter floor and the Friedmann constraint.

The governing balance describes a self-regulating growth process. The expansion continuously regenerates elastic dilation, which the anti-restoring bulk modulus does not resist, while accretion continuously consumes it by converting it into created volume. Without matter, the two rates balance at a small, steady residual strain epsilon-res-star, which the present macroscopic formulation treats as a constitutive constant of the interface. Determining epsilon-res-star from the underlying solidification kinetics is left to future work. Because accretion restores the rest-mass density to rho-naught at all times, the material state stays fixed. The moduli do not drift as the universe grows, since the identity myu-naught equals u-naught, of Section 3.2, ties them to the energy density, and neither does the effective viscosity zeta. We treat the reservoir as ideal, of unbounded capacity and fixed state, the configurational counterpart of a thermodynamic bath. The coefficients of the governing balance are therefore constant in time. Defining the expansion rate through the growth of created volume, three H equals the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-g, the steady state of the matter-free fabric grows at the rate given by the floor equation: three H-de-Sitter equals the magnitude of K-naught times epsilon-res-star, divided by zeta, so that the created volume grows exponentially, J-g of t proportional to e to the power three H-de-Sitter times t, an exact de Sitter expansion. We write H-de-Sitter for this rate, the floor of the expansion, to distinguish it from today's rate H-naught, which matter raises above the floor. The Background Datum postulate identifies the square of the floor with the constant term of its constraint. With the content's mean density rho-bar and, on the closed shell of Section 4, the shell's radius R, the constraint then reads: H squared equals H-de-Sitter squared, plus eight pi G over three, times rho-bar, minus c squared over R squared.

With matter present, the Friedmann constraint sets the rate and the governing balance sets the residual strain, epsilon-res equals epsilon-res-star times H over H-de-Sitter. This holds whenever the relaxation time of the exchange channel, defined in Section 6.2, is short against the Hubble time. Observation fixes the material combination, the magnitude of K-naught times epsilon-res-star, divided by zeta, through the late-time floor of the expansion rate rather than through H-naught itself. On the closed shell the curvature term keeps the matter-free rate slightly below H-de-Sitter, so the floor is the rate's late-time asymptote rather than a strict lower bound. The difference in H squared is the fraction, the magnitude of capital omega-k, at most about ten to the minus three, that Section 4.3 bounds. The curvature term belongs to the working geometry. A flat fabric has none, and no result of this section depends on it.

The full expansion history, with its radiation and matter eras and the transition to the floor, follows from the Friedmann constraint. Matter does not act on the rate through the material constants. The weakening of the moduli by inclusions, which the softening equation of Section 2.1 describes, is a deviation from the datum. The floor, as the floor equation gives it, depends only on the datum's constants, as the next paragraph states. Were it otherwise, a denser past would have weaker moduli and a lower floor, the opposite of a matter era. The constraint instead has General Relativity's energy of motion, so matter raises the rate through the gravitational constant and decelerates the expansion, as in the standard model. Its form is part of the Background Datum postulate, and Section 5.2 sets out three grounds for it. This form extends to motion the invisibility of the fourth dimension and shares the tensorial structure of the fabric's elasticity at M equals zero. Among energies of motion that weight the squared trace differently, it is also the only one under which the expansion and local gravity weigh matter alike. Its derivation from the fabric's microstructure remains open, and none of the results below depends on that derivation.

The identification also settles which changes reach the constant term, since the floor depends only on the constants of the floor equation. Accretion holds the modulus, the magnitude of K-naught, and the density rho-naught at their natural values. The floor can therefore move only through the constant epsilon-res-star and the mobility script-K, which a change of the interface or of the reservoir's state can alter. A disturbance that acts through the fabric's strain or motion, such as matter, a slip, an asphericity or a traction, alters the interfacial disequilibrium at a rate that the constraint has already set. Such a disturbance shifts only the residual strain.

The growth variable, the natural logarithm of J-g, tracks the created volume and holds no stress, while the elastic strain stays small, at epsilon-res-star without matter and at epsilon-res-star times H over H-de-Sitter with matter present. Accretion converts dilation into stress-free grown volume as fast as the expansion produces it. The small-strain elastic framework of the original formulation therefore remains valid at all times, even as the created volume grows without bound. The process begins with only an infinitesimal seed perturbation. Because the material constants, the magnitude of K-naught, epsilon-res-star and zeta, alone fix the floor, it is independent of that initial condition, which sets only the onset time. Without matter, the growth-rate equation and the floor equation make the mass accretion rate per unit current volume constant, rho-dot-acc equals three H-de-Sitter times rho-naught, and with matter present it is three H times rho-naught, the same for every region whose content is the same.

The floor of the expansion rate thus emerges from the interface's thermodynamics, through the identification that the Background Datum postulate makes, rather than as an external or arbitrary cosmological constant.

Section 4. The working geometry.

Section 4.1. The choice of a closed shell.

The curvature term of the Friedmann constraint requires a geometry for the fabric, and this section states the one on which the derivations are worked. The geometric setting is inherited from the original formulation: the fabric is a smooth three-dimensional hypersurface embedded in a flat four-dimensional Euclidean space, the continuum idealization already underlying the stiffness matrix and the relations of Section 2.1 that follow it. To this we add one further physical assumption: the precursor fluid is static, at rest in some global frame of the embedding space, with no bulk flow, shear, or rotation. Section 7.1 examines the status of this assumption and the observable consequences of relaxing it.

Neither the postulate's constraints nor the isotropy of the expansion selects the geometry. A flat plate that expands uniformly is consistent with both. Its slip against the static fluid is the gradient of H times the squared magnitude of x, divided by two, with x the position relative to its center, so the slip is curl-free and exerts no traction under the postulate. A bounded cap with a free rim likewise expands on its interior exactly as a closed shell does, since the constraints preserve its shape and its slip exerts no traction. The results of Sections 2 and 3 therefore hold on each of these geometries.

The geometric setting itself excludes one class of geometry, a slice of negative curvature or open universe in the usual terminology, whatever the fabric's formation. The spatial curvature of the uniform fabric is that of a hypersurface of flat four-dimensional space. By the Gauss equation, the curvature of the plane through two principal directions at a point is the product of their principal curvatures, kappa-i times kappa-j. A homogeneous and isotropic slice gives every plane through a point the same curvature K, so that kappa-one kappa-two, kappa-one kappa-three and kappa-two kappa-three all equal K, which implies that kappa-one squared, equal to the product of kappa-one kappa-two and kappa-one kappa-three, divided by kappa-two kappa-three, equals K.

A negative K is therefore impossible, even in a small patch. Cartan showed more generally that a real manifold of p dimensions whose constant curvature is below that of the surrounding space requires a space of at least two p minus one dimensions, five for a three-dimensional slice. A uniform fabric is therefore flat or closed, capital omega-k less than or equal to zero.

The fabric's origin favors closure. The process begins with an infinitesimal seed, as Section 3.3 states, and a first-order phase transition nucleates finite patches. A fabric grown at a finite rate is therefore bounded at every moment since its nucleation, so an unbounded configuration such as a flat plate cannot arise from it. The rim of a growing patch is the most favorable attachment site of a solidifying phase, so an active rim advances fastest and brings the patch toward closure. A single patch that closes its rim forms a topological three-sphere, and the derivations below are worked on this closed shell, which Figure 1 draws. Closure is favored rather than forced. It depends on the state in which the fabric nucleates and on the kinetics of its formation, which this paper does not model. A cap whose rim has stopped advancing or lies beyond the causal horizon is therefore not excluded here.

Section 4.2. The hypershell and its interior.

The constraints of the Background Datum postulate preserve the shape with which the fabric nucleates rather than select one. With uniform content every fabric element creates volume at a common specific rate, three H-naught today, as Section 3.3 describes. A common rate of volume creation does not by itself select a shape, since at M equals zero a curl-free displacement exerts no elastic force and the fabric can redistribute its volume at no cost. On a closed shell, a bend is a slight departure from the round shape, and its fractional amplitude, capital ksai equals h over R, is the bend's depth h in units of the radius. The irrotational displacement moves in step with the bend, and the constraints give the amplitude the rate capital-ksai-dot equals the magnitude of capital omega-k times H times capital ksai, as Appendix B derives. A bend absent at nucleation therefore stays absent, and a congenital bend keeps its fractional amplitude, which takes 1.5 times ten to the power thirteen years or more to change by one e-fold. A fabric that nucleates round stays round, with the rate of strain H times g-i-j, uniform and isotropic, so its expansion is self-similar, a homothety about the shell's center. Conversely, a fabric that is round today to within the isotropy bound of Section 8.2 was round to the same level when it nucleated. Section 10 draws this inference.

For a fabric that is uniform and round, the closed configuration is therefore a centrosymmetric hyperspherical shell, which we call the hypershell. Three-dimensional space is then a closed expanding spherical shell within the radially symmetric fluid reservoir. The geometric center of expansion lies off the fabric entirely, at the center of the shell within the four-dimensional bulk.

The hypershell's topology requires defining the thermodynamic state of the enclosed four-dimensional volume. The steady floor needs a constant boundary condition on the shell's inner face, since a time-varying state of the fluid there would vary the interface's constants and with them the floor that the floor equation gives. Accretion by transmutation in place meets this condition without any seal, since the shell's motion displaces no fluid. Its outer face attaches the fluid it sweeps and its inner face returns that fluid by detachment, less the share the shell accretes, so both faces see the same static fluid of the ideal reservoir. The interior therefore needs neither a void nor a separately enclosed body of fluid.

Section 4.3. Curvature, radius and the causality horizon.

To compare with observation, we evaluate the physical radius of curvature R of the hypershell. In standard cosmological solutions, a pure de Sitter metric is a spacetime of constant curvature whose spatial curvature depends on the choice of slicing. The closed slicing, with positive spatial curvature, is the natural description for the shell geometry considered here. In this slicing the cosmological event horizon tends to c over H-de-Sitter as the expansion approaches its floor, and distances below are measured in units of today's Hubble radius, R-H equals c over H-naught. Numerical values take H-naught equal to 67.4 kilometers per second per megaparsec and capital omega-m equal to 0.315 from the microwave background.

With the shell's radius in Hubble radii, its curvature parameter is capital omega-k equals minus the square of R-H over R, and the empirical flatness constraint bounds the radius. The combined baryon-acoustic-oscillation and microwave-background analysis of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument, DESI, gives capital omega-k equal to the quantity 2.3 plus or minus 1.1, times ten to the minus three, whose closed side reaches capital omega-k of about minus ten to the minus three at three standard deviations. A closed shell therefore has the magnitude of capital omega-k at most about ten to the minus three, and R at least about 31.6 times R-H. Its antipodal distance, pi R, then exceeds some thirty-fold the distance of the surface of last scattering, about 3.2 Hubble radii. The closed geometry therefore does not wrap within the observable universe, and the local causal structure of the three-dimensional hypersurface is preserved.

The closed shell of the working geometry has capital omega-k less than zero, and a detection near the level of ten to the minus three, within reach of forthcoming surveys, would measure its radius directly. A result consistent with flatness would only raise the bound on that radius. A significant measurement of capital omega-k greater than zero would falsify the geometric setting inherited from the original formulation, as Section 4.1 shows, and not only the closed shell. At present DESI's central value lies about two standard deviations on the side of negative curvature, which its authors do not count as a significant preference for curvature.

The radius bound implies that the shell moves outward through the bulk at R-dot equals H-naught times R, at least about 31.6 times c, today. Under the Background Datum postulate the steady accretion is indifferent to this speed, since the interface consumes static fluid in place and no shock can form. Causality within the fabric remains bounded by the three-dimensional elastic wave speed of the hyperplate, as the original formulation established, and the accretion introduces no faster in-plane signal. With alpha equal to zero the fluid's fast bulk modes cannot drive observable in-plane dilation, and their only in-fabric effect is the drag they exert on deviations at the interface. Fluid-borne signals that act on those deviations, such as the reservoir's traction on the divergence-free part of a slip, whose law Section 7.1 leaves open, must outrun the shell if they are to remain active. The reservoir's signal speed, c-f, must then exceed the shell's, c-f greater than R-dot, at least about 31.6 times c.

Finally, the elastic-gravitational correspondence of the original formulation was established on a flat reference plate. On the hypershell, curvature corrections to that derivation enter at order the square of R-H over R, at most about ten to the minus three, given the flatness bound above, and are therefore negligible for present purposes. The exact re-derivation on the closed geometry is deferred to future work.

The sections that follow give the grounds of the results of Sections 2 and 3, and the consequences that the working geometry adds.

Section 5. The fabric: grounds and consequences of the Background Datum.

Section 5.1. Configurational Thermodynamics and Datum Energy.

Two corollaries follow from the Background Datum postulate of Section 3.2, one for the thermal description of the precursor and the interface, and one for the energy that growth lays.

Under the Configurational Thermodynamics corollary, only energy and strain are dynamical variables outside the fabric, since the configuration's placement in the bulk has no inertial consequence for anything the fabric holds. Because momentum and kinetic energy belong only to motion that has inertia, the postulate also excludes a kinetic interpretation of thermal behavior. Temperature as mean microscopic kinetic energy, heat as its exchange and any bath defined by either are alike undefined for the precursor, the interface, and the fabric's bulk motion through both. Only the configurational description remains meaningful there: geometry, configurational or strain energy and its conjugate stresses, and entropy in its state-counting rather than kinetic sense. Ordinary thermodynamics already draws the same split between configurational and vibrational contributions. The datum's energy of motion is geometric rather than a kinetic energy of mass, a term of the constraints rather than of moving parts, so it defines no temperature either.

The Datum Energy corollary defines datum energy as the energy that the growth lays in the datum, in every channel. That includes the rest energy u-naught of the part of the fabric that bears the shear load and the configurational energy of whatever overlay the exchange maintains in the fabric. The overlay comprises the arrangement classified below and any further configurational structure that the fabric holds. It also includes whatever residual strain the exchange holds, uniform or not, whether the local rate or a disturbance of the interface sets it. The energy that gravitation reads as a source is that of the deviations from the maintained state, the fabric's natural configuration together with that residual strain, and the maintained state itself is the reference. The spatial metric and the energy of motion depend instead on the strain measured with rulers, the strain of the fabric's actual configuration, which the constraints govern. It includes the growth that the constraints admit, so the uniform growth appears in them as the expansion, while a local excess of growth does not, as Section 6.2 shows. A change of that state, whether laid by growth or re-laid by the turnover, is a change of the reference and not a deviation from it. Datum energy therefore sources no curvature, and the exchange alone supplies and returns it. Apart from the rest energy u-naught, which sets the transverse wave speed through myu-naught equals u-naught, it enters no wave speed either. The identity myu-naught equals u-naught, of Section 3.2, counts the energy of the part that bears the shear load alone. A changed natural configuration does alter how the fabric transmits a static field through it, but that alteration lies outside the present work and is not a source term.

Section 5.2. The energy of motion, the denial of inertia and the clause on slip.

The Background Datum postulate fixes the form of the constraints' energy of motion, one form for the uniform mode and the bends alike. This section explains why that form is natural. Its footing is that of alpha equals zero, and three grounds support it. First, the form extends to motion the invisibility of the fourth dimension that fixes M equals zero and alpha equals zero. Second, the form has the tensorial structure of the fabric's elasticity tensor at M equals zero, applied to the rate of strain instead of the strain. The momentum constraint accordingly applies the operator of the elastic force to the rate of strain. With no momentum density it therefore keeps the in-plane elastic force of the strain it reads constant in time, as Appendix B's reduction of the momentum constraint on the closed shell shows. At M equals zero the Lamé constant is lambda equals minus two myu-naught, so the elastic energy density of a strain epsilon-i-j takes the same form: u-el equals one-half lambda times the square of epsilon-k-k, plus myu-naught times epsilon-i-j epsilon-i-j, which equals myu-naught times the quantity epsilon-i-j epsilon-i-j minus the square of epsilon-k-k.

Its squared trace enters with unit weight, as in the energy of motion. Hehl and Kiefer read the DeWitt metric, which contracts the rate of strain in General Relativity's energy of motion, as an elasticity tensor. Its Lamé constants, lambda equals minus one and myu equals one-half, satisfy the same condition, lambda plus two myu equals zero. A uniaxial rate then costs no energy, a change of shape costs energy, and a change of volume releases it, as the corresponding strains do in the elastic energy density just stated.

Third, the expansion must weigh matter as local gravity does. When the energy of motion is written as K-i-j times K-i-j minus lambda-K times the square of K-k-k, with a trace coefficient lambda-K distinct from the Lamé constant lambda of the stiffness matrix, the postulate sets lambda-K equal to one for every mode. With any other value the growth would obey a Friedmann equation with the gravitational constant two G over the quantity three lambda-K minus one, in place of G. A comparison of the expansion with local gravity would then reveal the fabric's slicing, which the fourth dimension defines. The coefficient c squared over sixteen pi G belongs to the postulate as well, and because one set of constraints governs every mode, the uniform mode shares the form and the coefficient of the deviations. The postulate fixes the form and the coefficient at the rates that observations of the expansion reach. At higher rates the data do not test it, and a departure there, such as an upper limit on the rate, lies outside this paper. No inertia and no dissipation give the form, since each gives the rate of strain a non-negative quadratic energy, while this one is negative for a change of volume. The energy of motion is therefore geometric, and although it gives the shell's radius a canonical momentum, the datum has no momentum density.

On any geometry of the fabric, observation requires that the bends have no inertia. A body of mass m and Schwarzschild radius r-s bends the fabric with the slope, the square root of r-s over r, at a distance r much greater than r-s. A body moving at the speed v therefore changes the bend at the rate minus v times cosine theta times the square root of r-s over r, with theta measured from its direction of motion. If the bend had the material's inertia, rho-naught equals three c squared over the quantity four pi G L squared, by the equation of the vacuum moduli and the identity myu-naught equals u-naught, the kinetic energy of that motion within a radius r-max would be: T equals one-half rho-naught v squared times the integral of r-s over r, times cosine squared theta, d V, which equals one-half m v squared times the square of r-max over L.

For a thickness near the Planck length the factor is about 4 times ten to the power sixty-nine within 1 meter and 9 times ten to the power ninety-one within 1 astronomical unit, so every moving body would be heavier inertially than gravitationally by those factors. The bends must therefore have no inertia. On the closed shell two further arguments support this conclusion. An inertial bend would be unstable under elasticity alone, as Section 8.2 states. An inertial trajectory through the bulk would give every planet the same perihelion drift, which the ephemerides exclude, as Section 9.3 states.

The postulate's last clause extends the same reasoning to slip. A traction transfers momentum, and the irrotational, or curl-free, part of the material's motion, which the momentum constraint fixes with the bend, has no momentum density to receive or give. The reservoir's traction therefore acts on the divergence-free motion alone: the fabric's transverse waves, the relaxation of shear structure frozen into it during formation and any rotation. Section 7.2 reasons in the same way when it excludes radiation drag on the homogeneous mode, a transfer of momentum without mass. The clause matters because a fabric that holds matter cannot avoid curl-free slip. A flat fabric slips against the static fluid at H times the magnitude of x about its center, as Section 4.1 states. On the closed shell the uniform growth does not slip, but the static fields of matter do. The shell moves outward at a speed R-dot of at least 31.6 times c, as the radius bound of Section 4.3 implies. A static field moves with it and keeps its shape, so the field's tilted face slips against the reservoir at about R-dot times its slope. Held at zero slip, the field would instead reshape at the rate R-dot times its curvature, by one e-fold in 31 hours or less at 1 astronomical unit from the Sun. The constraints would also give it only the fraction one over the quantity one plus R-dot squared over c squared, about ten to the minus three or less, of the curvature that its source fixes in General Relativity, as Appendix B shows.

Section 5.3. The microstructure class.

The maximal rigidity of Section 3.2 also constrains the fabric's microstructure. Saturating the causal bound, myu less than or equal to u, requires every unit of the fabric's cohesive energy to bear shear affinely. Architecturally open networks cannot do so, since their directional constraints hold volume that bears no shear and admit soft rigid-unit modes. The fabric's configurational arrangement is therefore of the dense, central-force-dominated class. Whether the exclusion extends to every architecture of discrete elements in void, leaving a structureless component whose stress state is the saturation itself to bear the shear load, is left to future work.

Section 5.4. Five consequences and the momentum balance.

The postulate has five consequences.

First, the governing balance is exactly first order in time rather than an approximation, as are the kinetic relations of continuum phase-transformation theory, in which the transformation coordinate has no inertia. With the rate set by the postulate's constraint, the governing balance determines the residual strain. In the original formulation the background was static, so the question of its momentum never arose, and the expanding extension forces the choice.

Second, accretion proceeds by transmutation in place, with the interface consuming the adjacent static fluid where it stands. No force is required to bring accreted mass onto the background's accelerating trajectory. Motion with the datum is not a dynamical state to be maintained but the reference from which dynamics is measured. The background expansion requires no bulk flow of the reservoir.

Third, the background's bulk trajectory is unobservable from within. Its velocity and its acceleration through the bulk, both normal to the fabric, leave no dynamical imprint on the fabric's contents beyond General Relativity's tidal term. Across a bound system of size r that term is an acceleration of order H-de-Sitter squared times r, with H-de-Sitter the floor that the floor equation gives. This extends to the accelerated trajectory the kinematic hiding of the fabric's rest frame derived in the companion paper on recovering the principle of relativity. It parallels the equivalence principle, since the local inertial frames of the theory are, by postulate, the frames riding the datum. The trajectory itself is not arbitrary. The constraint of the postulate and the interface kinetics of Section 3.1 determine it, and the postulate states only that inertia references it. The fabric's contents register instead the rate of strain, the rate at which distances measured within the fabric grow as new fabric forms between bodies at rest in it. On the round shell that rate is H equals R-dot over R. The constraints' energy of motion depends on the rate of strain alone, so the expansion has an energy of motion while the speed through the bulk has none. Motion tangent to the fabric relative to the reservoir is slip, and only its divergence-free part, such as a rotation, meets the reservoir's traction. Section 7.1 shows that part to be observable.

Fourth, the resulting division of modes parallels that of General Relativity. The scalar part of the geometry obeys only the first-order constraints at every wavelength, its uniform part being the Friedmann constraint, and only the fabric's transverse excitations obey second-order wave equations. The postulate extends to momentum and inertia the principle that governs gravitation in Section 7.3, where the uniform background is the reference from which gravitating strain is measured and only deviations gravitate.

Fifth, the same reasoning settles the reservoir's thermal status. Since the datum leaves no momentum or kinetic-energy channel, the precursor supports no bath and no temperature, and any fluctuation available to the interface kinetics must be generated by the accretion itself. Section 10 returns to the properties that such agitation would require.

Under the postulate the background's momentum balance holds identically, deviations included, since co-evolution with the datum is force-free by construction. The deviations' own evolution relative to the datum remains dynamical, but none of it re-enters the governing kinetic balance at a significant level. Bends of the shell, which the constraints govern, contribute no first-order term at their fractional amplitude capital ksai. Their second-order elastic imprint is about myu-naught times capital ksai squared, at most about ten to the minus ten times myu-naught, at the isotropy bound of Section 8.2, capital ksai at most about ten to the minus five. The governing balance and the floor equation therefore stand without correction in the absence of matter. The deviations' energy enters instead through the constraint of the postulate, and in this way matter raises the rate above the floor, as the Friedmann constraint states. The deviations' own dynamics, such as the response of the interface kinetics to inclusion strain fields, is posed in Section 10 as a problem for later work.

Section 6. The interface's structure and stability.

Section 6.1. Internal state variables and the static dilatation.

The accretion law, the multiplicative split of the Jacobian, the growth-rate equation and the bulk-viscosity law complete the structure of the growth model in the sense of internal state variable theory. The kinematic, strain-like internal state variable is the accumulated growth, the natural logarithm of J-g, an inelastic, history-dependent variable and the counterpart of the plastic strain of viscoplasticity, and the growth-rate equation states its evolution. Its conjugate thermodynamic force is the dissipative stress, sigma-diss equals rho-naught times delta capital fai, identified in Section 3.1, and the mobility script-K, equivalently the viscosity zeta, is the kinetic coefficient connecting the force to the rate. The quasi-static balance of Section 3.2 sets sigma-diss equal to the magnitude of K-naught times epsilon-res, so the driving force is proportional to the residual elastic strain. The growth element is thus of Maxwell type: an anti-restoring elastic spring in series with a viscous dashpot, with the spring sustaining, rather than relaxing, the steady flow through the dashpot.

The accretion flux responds, through delta capital fai, to the density deficit of the elastic dilation, the excess of the fabric's actual volume over its natural volume. The static dilatation that matter induces enters it only transiently. The field of an inclusion is a bend of the fabric, whose membrane strain holds the field's curvature. Only its volumetric strain, epsilon-loc, is the dilatation of a displacement field. The strain itself is not compatible, and at M equals zero it exerts the in-plane force three K-naught times the gradient of epsilon-loc, which equals minus four myu-naught times the gradient of epsilon-loc, directed away from the mass, in every gauge. The constraints hold the field, with the mass's density as their source, and the material has no law of motion that the force could drive. The exchange responds instead. Where the fabric's actual volume exceeds its natural volume the interface lays more, so the natural configuration follows the field with the relaxation time tau-zeta of Section 6.2. The deposit it lays is epsilon-loc where the exchange reads the dilation, as the governing balance does, and three epsilon-loc where it reads the force. After that the field's elastic dilation vanishes where the exchange reads the dilation, and its force vanishes where the exchange reads the force. In either case the exchange is blind to the field, and the field itself is unchanged, since the constraints hold it and the deposit is datum energy. A static field therefore drives a bounded transient and no sustained growth, and the exchange does not relax it. The transient is about ten to the minus eight at 1 astronomical unit from the Sun and ten to the minus six in a galaxy, and it is invisible to clocks and to light. A body that moves through the fabric is followed with the lag v times tau-zeta. For the Sun's motion that lag exceeds the planets' distances for any relaxation time above a year, so the solar system's field is never re-laid at those distances. A galaxy's field, which its bodies cross in millions of years, is re-laid in full, since tau-zeta, a fraction epsilon-res-star over three of the floor's Hubble time by the relaxation-time equation of Section 6.2, is far shorter than that crossing time for any residual strain below about ten to the minus four. The exchange's blindness to static fields is thus the steady state of its own kinetics rather than an assumption.

At M equals zero elasticity exerts no force on the irrotational part of a displacement, as Section 3.2 states, and this has an energetic counterpart for a localized misfit, of the kind that a defect or an incomplete accretion event would leave. In the classical center-of-dilatation problem of Eshelby, a dilation e of a spherical region of volume V stores energy both inside the region and in the shear field around it: E-internal plus E-external equals one-half K-naught e squared V, plus two-thirds myu-naught e squared V, which equals one-sixth times the quantity three K-naught plus four myu-naught, times e squared V.

At the signed modulus, K-naught equals minus four-thirds myu-naught, this sum vanishes, since three K-naught plus four myu-naught equals three M, which equals zero, so the material exerts no force on a misfit's volume. The two-modulus equilibrium on which the classical partition of a misfit's energy rests therefore does not exist in the fabric. The division of that energy between the misfit and its surroundings depends on what holds the misfit, which lies outside the continuum treated here.

Section 6.2. The relaxation time and the stability of uniform growth.

Two standing quantities of the interface accompany the governing balance. First, the relaxation time of the exchange channel is given by the relaxation-time equation: tau-zeta, defined as zeta over the magnitude of K-naught, equals epsilon-res-star over three H-de-Sitter.

It measures how quickly the kinetics that sustain the steady state erase a departure of the residual strain from the value that the rate requires, as the next paragraph discusses. Second, as Section 3.1 assumes, the gross turnover rate of the interface, attachment and detachment combined, greatly exceeds the net rate of accretion, its small biased residual. Neither quantity is a further constant of the balance that the floor equation states, which the magnitude of K-naught, epsilon-res-star and zeta alone fix, since the relaxation time is the ratio, zeta over the magnitude of K-naught, of two of them. The relaxation time is the clock of the re-laying of static fields in Section 6.1 and of the misfit's relaxation below, and the gross turnover rate matters once the interface's microphysical turnover is studied. The turnover re-lays the configurational overlay, the arrangement of the microstructure class and any further configurational structure that the fabric holds, far faster than the fabric grows.

The balance also determines how a uniform state of the fabric, once present, evolves. Perturbations of the residual strain, delta epsilon-res, are self-correcting. A region strained above the balance point maintains a larger interfacial driving potential, delta capital fai, so accretion consumes its excess dilation faster and returns it toward that point. A region below it is re-dilated by the expansion, which its anti-restoring modulus does not resist. The balance point is the strain at which accretion keeps pace with the local rate, which the Friedmann constraint sets from the mean content of regions larger than the Hubble radius. Without matter, it is epsilon-res-star everywhere, and every element grows at the floor H-de-Sitter of the floor equation. With matter present the residual strain follows the rate, epsilon-res equals epsilon-res-star times H over H-de-Sitter, and a uniform rate requires uniform mean content on those scales. This restoring behavior follows from the signs of the kinetics, and its formal demonstration belongs with the microphysical determination of epsilon-res-star deferred in Section 3.3. The self-correction acts on the elastic factor of the multiplicative split of the Jacobian. Section 6.1 shows that a static field drives only a bounded transient of it and is not relaxed by the exchange.

Perturbations of the accumulated growth, delta of the natural logarithm of J-g, are the same self-correction seen from the natural configuration. Let growth lay a non-uniform stress-free dilation, theta, into the fabric. At M equals zero this growth misfit exerts the force density, the magnitude of K-naught times the gradient of theta, on the fabric, while the elastic force of any displacement u is a curl, minus myu-naught times the curl of the curl of u. The divergence of a curl vanishes, so a curl can balance the gradient only if the Laplacian of theta equals zero, which no localized growth pattern satisfies. Grown volume therefore has no state of equilibrium in the plane, already in flat space, and it is not frozen in place. A bend of the shell cannot take it up either, because the constraints govern every bend and the misfit has no density to enter them, so the actual configuration does not change. The region that has grown more then holds more natural volume than its place admits, so its elastic dilation falls by theta. The governing balance therefore lays less there. The misfit relaxes at the rate one over tau-zeta of the relaxation-time equation, at every wavelength. No growth perturbation grows, and the stability this section establishes covers both state variables. Congenital structure in the fabric's volume therefore persists only in the shell's shape, as a bend, with the bound that Section 8.2 states. Shear structure frozen in at formation is a separate matter, since it belongs to the divergence-free motion on which the reservoir's traction acts, as Section 5.2 states.

Section 7. The reservoir and the exchange.

Section 7.1. The static reservoir and the tangential coupling.

The frame of the static reservoir that Section 4.1 assumes is fixed only in part. A material point of the homothetically expanding hypersphere at position x relative to its center moves with velocity v equals H-naught times x, normal to the fabric everywhere. A superposed rigid drift of the configuration relative to the static fluid adds at every point the tangential part of a uniform velocity. That part is curl-free and exerts no traction, while the normal part alters only the residual strain. The motion of the shell's center relative to the reservoir is therefore unobservable. A rigid rotation of the configuration is divergence-free, by contrast, and the reservoir's traction resists it as it resists any inertial motion.

The static reservoir is therefore exposed to observation, though only through part of the reservoir's motion. A reservoir with a uniform bulk velocity relative to the shell's center adds a coherent slip component across the fabric. Its normal component alters only the residual strain, as for a drift of the shell. Its tangential component is curl-free, so under the Background Datum postulate it exerts no traction, and a uniform drift of the reservoir is unobservable. Internal velocity structure of the reservoir, such as shear, rotation or inflow, exerts traction through the divergence-free part of its tangential component. That traction would act on the fabric as a lateral force with a directional pattern on the sky, distinct in origin from the kinematic dipole of observer motion. The observed isotropy at the level of ten to the minus five bounds that traction. Turning that bound into a tolerance on the reservoir's departure from its static state requires the law of the tangential coupling, which the last paragraph of this section leaves open.

The division of motion on which this test rests is not peculiar to the postulate. The fabric's elasticity already draws it, since curl-free and divergence-free motions propagate in an isotropic solid as longitudinal and transverse waves, and at M equals zero only the transverse waves have a restoring force. General Relativity draws it as well, since its constraints fix the longitudinal part of the geometry and only the transverse part radiates. The postulate adds only that the reservoir's traction follows the same division. That clause does not follow from ordinary fluid mechanics, in which a viscous fluid also drags a body in irrotational motion. It stands as part of the postulate, exposed to the observational test just described.

The accretion of Section 3.1 specifies the dilational coupling between fabric and fluid. The Background Datum postulate confines the tangential coupling to the fabric's divergence-free motion, which comprises its transverse waves, the relaxation of any shear structure frozen into it and any rotation. No result of this paper depends on the law of that coupling, and we leave it open. One condition bounds it. If light's field loads the fluid at the faces, the fluid must not damp it, since light propagates undamped over paths of the horizon's length. Whether light's field loads the faces at all remains open, as Section 10 records.

Section 7.2. Alternatives to accretion and the replenishment fraction.

Accretion is not the only channel that could regularize the instability at the level of the governing balance, and we set aside three alternatives. An intrinsic bulk viscosity of the fabric, a bare material constant in place of the derived coefficient of the bulk-viscosity law, would damp the dilation with no reservoir at all. Nothing would then replenish the density, so dilution would weaken the moduli and the drive by order one per e-fold. No steady rate or state results, and it forfeits the reservoir on which the nucleation origin and the energy conservation of Section 7.3 rest. Radiation of the dilation's work into the precursor fluid, a drag of magnitude zeta-rad, about rho-f times c-f times L, set by the fluid's density rho-f and wave speed c-f, would retain the reservoir. It would let the fluid act on the homogeneous mode by a transfer of momentum without mass, which the Background Datum postulate excludes. A phase front that consumes static fluid in place displaces none, so neither the uniform dilation nor a bend radiates, and acoustic drag is left to the fluid-borne signals of Section 4.3. An external tensile traction on a conventional material with a positive bulk modulus would also expand it. That drive postulates an unobserved mechanism in the bulk and cannot give the steady, viscosity-limited rate of a de Sitter floor without tuning. With a positive shear modulus it also has M greater than zero, which the invisibility of the fourth dimension excludes. The drive of the open system is instead internal, the vacuum's native elastodynamics balanced by the phase-boundary kinetics.

Suppose that the postulate is relaxed to admit that transfer, so that accretion and radiation damping compete. The replenishment fraction kai, in the range from zero up to one inclusive, the share of new volume whose rest mass solidification supplies, then parametrizes the competition. The density then evolves as rho-dot over rho equals minus three times the quantity one minus kai, times H, the moduli drift with it, and the equation of state of the floor departs from minus one by order one minus kai. The coefficient follows from how the floor scales with the density. With the moduli proportional to the density by the maximal-rigidity identity myu-naught equals rho-naught c squared, the floor equation gives H-de-Sitter proportional to rho-naught to the power of the quantity s minus one. Here s is the sum of the logarithmic derivatives of the set point epsilon-res-star and the mobility script-K with respect to the density, which only the interface's microphysics gives. The departure is then one plus w equals two times the quantity s minus one, times the quantity one minus kai, of order one minus kai unless s is large. The endpoints are the open-system model of Section 3, kai equal to one with w equal to minus one exactly, and the purely radiative variant, kai equal to zero, closed to mass exchange and strongly drifting. Full replenishment is the attractor of the coupled kinetics. Accretion responds to the accumulated density deficit while radiation drag responds only to the instantaneous rate, so any shortfall deepens the deficit until solidification erases it. Sustaining kai less than one would require suppressing mass transfer altogether, script-K equal to zero, at an interface in contact and out of equilibrium, or a reservoir of drifting state.

Observation bounds any deficit, whatever its cause. The equation of the vacuum moduli ties the gravitational constant to the shear modulus, which moves with the density, so a fabric that dilutes at a fixed thickness makes G grow at the rate three times the quantity one minus kai, times H. The planetary ephemerides of Pitjeva and Pitjev hold that growth below 7.8 times ten to the minus fourteen per year at 95 percent probability, and with it one minus kai below about 4 times ten to the minus four. A fabric closed to mass exchange would make G grow at three H-naught, about 2 times ten to the minus ten per year, three orders of magnitude above the bound. A replenishment deficit could therefore account for a departure of w from minus one of order ten to the minus three at most, unless the exponent s is large. A larger departure would measure a drift of the interface's constants, which Section 9.3 discusses. Both readings keep the Background Datum postulate, whereas the radiative channel requires the postulate to admit momentum transfer without mass.

Section 7.3. Energy conservation of the fabric and its reservoir.

Showing that the open system conserves the energy of the fabric and its reservoir requires stating every flow of energy between them. Conservation is meant in the ordinary sense of the First Law of Thermodynamics, applied to the fabric and its reservoir jointly. By the Configurational Thermodynamics corollary of Section 5.1, every flow below is purely configurational, an exchange of rest mass-energy and configurational energy. Dissipation means the irreversible degradation of the transformation's driving free energy, with the accompanying entropy production understood in the state-counting sense, rather than the release of heat. At the steady state of the floor equation, the growth rate, the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-g, is three H-de-Sitter, and the mass accretion rate per unit current volume, rho-dot-acc, is three H-de-Sitter times rho-naught. With matter present the same flows hold with H in place of H-de-Sitter and the residual strain of Section 3.3 in place of epsilon-res-star. Three energy flows, each taken per unit current volume and time, make up the balance.

The conservation also rests on the symmetry that Noether's theorem assigns to it, the invariance of the laws under a translation in time. In General Relativity the expanding metric admits no timelike Killing vector, so matter and geometry together have no conserved energy, and the energy of the matter alone changes as the universe expands. Here the reservoir restores the symmetry. It is static and of fixed state, and the interface's law has no explicit dependence on time. The laws of the fabric and its reservoir are therefore invariant under translation in the time of the reservoir's frame, which Section 4.1 assumes and Section 7.1 examines. The energy that the balance below conserves is the charge of that symmetry. Dissipation within the fabric does not break it, because the joint system is autonomous: the degraded configurational energy crosses the interface into the reservoir, whose fixed state absorbs it, as the second flow below states. The expansion therefore conserves energy for the same reason that a crystal growing from a bath does. The bath, not the crystal, supplies the time-translation symmetry.

First, the fabric's rest mass-energy density is maintained at rho-naught c squared while its volume grows. The fabric gains energy at the rate three H-de-Sitter times rho-naught c squared, supplied entirely by the mass flux solidifying across the phase boundary. Second, the transformation dissipates. Each unit of accreted mass descends the specific potential drop, delta capital fai equals the magnitude of K-naught times epsilon-res-star, divided by rho-naught, so configurational energy is degraded at the constant rate: rho-dot-acc times delta capital fai equals zeta times the square of three H-de-Sitter, which in turn equals three H-de-Sitter times the magnitude of K-naught times epsilon-res-star.

That is the flow that ordinary solidification would release as latent heat, and here it has no kinetic-thermal channel to enter. This dissipation and the bulk-viscous dissipation of the bulk-viscosity law are one and the same energy flow, since the dissipative stress is the mechanical expression of the interfacial disequilibrium. The fabric's transverse wave speed equals the speed of light, and this equality is the maximal-rigidity condition, myu-naught equals u-naught equals rho-naught c squared, of Section 3.2. The specific potential drop is therefore a small constant fraction of the rest energy, delta capital fai over c squared equals four-thirds epsilon-res-star. Third, the elastic energy density of the residual strain, one-half K-naught times epsilon-res-star squared, is negative for the energetic solid, whose elastic energy decreases under dilation. It is constant in time and of second order in epsilon-res-star, so it contributes only negligibly as new volume is created.

The energy balance therefore holds to first order in epsilon-res-star. The reservoir delivers three H-de-Sitter times rho-naught times the quantity c squared plus delta capital fai, per unit current volume and time. Of this, three H-de-Sitter times rho-naught c squared becomes the rest mass-energy of new fabric, and three H-de-Sitter times rho-naught times delta capital fai is returned to the reservoir as dissipated configurational energy, absorbed on the fluid side of the interface. With the reservoir ideal, as Section 3.3 assumes, the total energy of fabric plus reservoir is conserved by construction, with every term traceable to a flux across the open interface. Absorption on the fluid side does not conflict with the transverse stiffness shielding of Section 2.1: the shielding confines the elastic wave modes of the hyperplate, whereas this absorption belongs to the interfacial transformation itself. A small fraction of the dissipated flow might instead be delivered into the deviations that move within the fabric, where by the Background Datum postulate kinetic energy is defined and a fluctuation background is meaningful. That possibility bears on whether the interface can generate its own agitation, a question that Section 10 poses for later work.

The Friedmann constraint is not part of this balance. Its energy of motion is geometric, and its constant term is a rate that the interface kinetics set rather than an energy that the reservoir delivers. At each moment the constraint balances the energy of motion against the content's energy and the constant term, as the Friedmann constraint of General Relativity does. The balance therefore concerns the fabric's own energy, which sources no curvature.

This balance also clarifies why the fabric's own rest density does not act as a gravitational source. With myu-naught equal to rho-naught c squared, the equation of the vacuum moduli gives rho-naught equals three c squared over the quantity four pi G L squared, which for a hyperplate thickness near the Planck length is of the order of the Planck density. In the fabric model, however, gravitation is the deformation of the fabric induced by inclusions. Only departures from the uniform reference state, the localized strains epsilon-loc of the softening equation, enter the correspondence with General Relativity. The uniform fabric defines the datum, the reference from which gravitating strain is measured, and its only curvature is that of the closed shell. Its energy density is the medium, not a source within the medium. The cosmological constant problem therefore does not arise in the model in the form familiar from semiclassical gravity, in which all vacuum energy gravitates.

The open system is thus a mechanistic realization of the cosmological constant rather than an alternative to it. In the standard model with a cosmological constant, the simplest of the standard dark-energy models, dark energy maintains a constant energy density as the universe expands. Because General Relativity lacks a global time-translation Killing vector in an expanding metric, however, global energy is not conserved in general. In the open system the replenishment holds the material constants fixed, so the floor that the floor equation gives, and with it the constraint's constant term, does not change as the volume grows. The fabric model thus obtains the non-diluting behavior of lambda from an explicit, conserved flux across an open thermodynamic boundary, with the value of the floor tied to material kinetics through the floor equation. The conserved quantity is the energy of the fabric and its reservoir rather than the global energy of General Relativity, since the Friedmann constraint lies outside that balance.

The residual stress of the steady state, the magnitude of K-naught times epsilon-res-star, is not itself the model's counterpart of dark energy. It is the mechanical drive of the accretion, and as part of the uniform background it is gravitationally inert. Observation fixes only the ratio of the floor equation, the magnitude of K-naught times epsilon-res-star, divided by zeta, and the dark-energy density of a standard fit, three H-de-Sitter squared c squared, divided by eight pi G, is that ratio re-expressed rather than the stress. The equation of state w equals minus one likewise tracks the replenishment that Section 7.2 discusses.

Section 8. The shell's kinematics and shape.

Section 8.1. Lorentz invariance and slip.

The expansion must preserve Lorentz invariance. The fabric model already has a rest frame, that of the fabric itself. The earlier derivation in the companion paper on recovering the principle of relativity obtains the Lorentz transformations for rods, clocks and light from the fabric's material postulates, with the contraction of rods taken from the Michelson-Morley experiment. The rest frame is thereby hidden kinematically, in the Lorentz-Poincaré sense, since it exists but no measurement with rods, clocks or light reveals it. Hiding it also in the fields of moving bodies is not derived here. The expansion must therefore avoid not a preferred frame as such but observable, position-dependent lateral forcing at the phase boundary, which would break the kinematic invariance derived in that companion paper. We accordingly examine the boundary velocities relative to the four-dimensional embedding space.

Under the Background Datum postulate the examination is short, since the reservoir exerts traction only on divergence-free slip. A flat plate expanding through the static fluid would slip against it at H times the magnitude of x about its center, as Section 4.1 states, which is the stretching-sheet problem of fluid dynamics. That slip is curl-free, so it exerts no traction, and the disequilibrium it makes alters only the residual strain, itself datum energy. On the closed shell the uniform growth does not slip at all, since each face is adjacent to every point of the continuum and both faces see the same static reservoir. The static field of an inclusion tilts the fabric, and its tilted face slips at about R-dot times its slope, at least 1,300 kilometers per second at 1 astronomical unit from the Sun, as Section 5.2 describes. That slip is the gradient of the field's height, so it exerts no traction either. Lateral forcing would arise only from a divergence-free slip, such as a rotation of the fabric relative to the fluid, which Section 7.1 bounds. The kinematic invariance derived in the companion paper therefore holds on the shell and near masses alike. Whether the slip alters the interface's growth rate belongs to the response of the interface kinetics to inclusions that Section 10 lists.

Section 8.2. Bends of the shell.

The closed shell keeps its shape, as Section 4.2 states, because it has no bend modes of its own. The constraints, not the fabric's elasticity, govern its shape on each slice. With uniform content they keep a congenital departure from the round shape at an almost constant fractional amplitude and admit none that was absent at nucleation, as Appendix B shows. Beyond the observational grounds of Section 5.2, the stability of the shell requires that bends have no inertia, as the same appendix derives. Under elasticity alone the normal displacement of an inertial bend, h, would couple to the hoop strain, the stretch h over R that it imposes in every tangent direction of a shell of radius R. With a negative bulk modulus that coupling would make every bend mode grow, at the rate two c over R, times the square root of the quantity ell plus three, for the mode of degree ell, about two c times the square root of k over R for a bend of wavenumber k much greater than one over R. At the smallest radius that Section 4.3 admits, every bend shorter than about 3.6 gigaparsecs would grow faster than the expansion, one of 1 astronomical unit in about 500 years. An exchange that absorbed the bend's dilation would only freeze it, as a stress-free bulge whose curvature no matter supplies, and that curvature would deflect light where orbits see no mass. The same appendix shows that the momentum constraint fixes the irrotational part of the material's displacement with the bend.

An asphericity of the shell slips against the static reservoir, because under the constraints its material moves with the homothety, but the slip is curl-free and exerts no traction. With matter present an asphericity acts instead as a curvature perturbation, which dilutes the content unevenly and seeds density contrast, as Appendix B shows. The observed isotropy of the universe bounds it at about ten to the minus five through that gravitational imprint.

Section 9. Evaluation against observation.

Section 9.1. Homogeneity and the Cosmological Principle.

The standard cosmological model relies on the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric and on the Cosmological Principle, which requires the universe to be homogeneous and isotropic on large scales. The framework's expansion law has a different footing. The governing balance is a local constitutive statement, obeyed pointwise by every fabric element, rather than a global metric solution whose validity depends on a symmetric source distribution. Without matter, that law gives every element the same steady growth. With matter present the constraint of the Background Datum postulate raises the rate of each region larger than the Hubble radius with that region's mean content, as it would for a separate universe. That rate is measured in the region's own proper time, and a uniform rate on those scales therefore requires uniform mean content, as Section 6.2 states.

The derivation takes the fabric itself to be uniform in two senses. Its constitutive properties are uniform, the standard idealization of any continuum theory, and its deformation state is maximally symmetric, which on the working geometry is also round. The second is taken from the observed isotropy rather than assumed for the state in which the fabric nucleated. Homogeneity of the expansion state therefore enters as an observed property of the fabric rather than as a standing assumption about the matter distribution. Because the constraints preserve the fabric's shape rather than select it, that property also bears on the fabric's origin, as Section 10 discusses. A quasi-uniform state leaves the derivation intact, since every element still obeys the same local law and the energy balance of Section 7.3 holds regardless. A non-uniform content yields a perturbed geometry, as in the perturbed metrics of standard practice.

Section 9.2. The expansion history and steady-state cosmology.

The present work addresses the mechanism of late-time acceleration. The matter-free fabric's expansion is exactly de Sitter and the observed expansion is not. Its present deceleration parameter, q-naught equals minus R-double-dot times R over R-dot squared, is about minus 0.53 in the standard fit, whereas de Sitter expansion has q equal to minus one, since matter still decelerates the growth. The two are compatible as they are in the standard model, whose lambda component is itself exactly de Sitter. The mechanism derived here supplies the counterpart of that component, the non-diluting part of the expansion, with w equal to minus one, and the radiation and matter eras follow from the Friedmann constraint, as Section 3.3 states. Beyond the matter content, the framework admits two deviation channels of its own. They are a persistent offset of w from minus one that measures reservoir non-ideality and a slow drift of the floor if the interface's settling after formation has a long tail, which Sections 9.3 and 10 discuss.

Because the model maintains a constant vacuum density by continuous creation, it resembles in one respect the steady-state cosmology of Bondi, Gold, and Hoyle. Steady-state cosmology held the matter density constant through continuous matter creation, predicting a universe without cosmic evolution. It was refuted by the counts of radio sources of Ryle and Clarke and by the cosmic microwave background that Penzias and Wilson detected, which demonstrated that the matter content evolves. The fabric model creates fabric, space itself, at the mass rate per unit volume, three H times rho-naught, of Section 3.3, constant in the absence of matter. The matter content meanwhile dilutes and evolves exactly as in standard cosmology. The observations that eliminated steady-state cosmology constrain the matter content, which the present framework leaves conventional.

Measured against the standard model with a cosmological constant, the framework reproduces the expansion history. The floor, H-de-Sitter, is identified with the interface's constants rather than derived. Its value requires the steady residual strain without matter, epsilon-res-star, and the mobility, script-K, which no observation independent of the expansion reaches, and the hyperplate's thickness, which the present work does not determine. The framework therefore has the same single constant as the lambda component, and it shares that component's equation of state, w equals minus one. Its content beyond the standard model is the mechanism behind that constant. The mechanism attributes any departure of w from minus one either to a replenishment deficit, which the constancy of the gravitational constant bounds, or to a drift of the interface's constants, as Section 7.2 shows. The inherited embedding also excludes a negatively curved geometry, which the standard model admits, as Section 4.1 shows. Section 10 discusses a galactic acceleration scale that the same floor would set.

Section 9.3. Falsifiability.

The framework makes three commitments that observation can refute or measure. The closed shell itself is not among them, since it is a choice of working geometry rather than a result, as Section 4.1 states. The third commitment, capital omega-k less than or equal to zero, is the weaker one that the inherited setting forces.

First, as Section 7.2 shows, the equation of state of the floor is exactly w equals minus one, without evolution, in the ideal-reservoir limit. Combined with microwave-background and supernova data, the DESI baryon-acoustic-oscillation analysis prefers an evolving equation of state at 2.8 to 4.2 standard deviations. A robust detection of w not equal to minus one would falsify the ideal-reservoir model with a settled interface. Within the framework such a deviation would measure a drift of the interface's constants, through a reservoir of drifting state or a floor still settling from the formation epoch, as Section 10 discusses. A replenishment deficit could not supply it, because the constancy of the gravitational constant holds one minus kai below about 4 times ten to the minus four, as Section 7.2 shows. The exception is a large exponent s in that section's relation, whose value Section 10 leaves to the interface's microphysics.

Second, the solar system and gravitational waves test the Background Datum postulate itself. Because neither the configuration's trajectory through the bulk nor its bends have inertia, the expansion's acceleration leaves no inertial imprint on bound systems. On the closed shell, the alternative reading, in which deviations retain inertia against the bulk, would tilt the force that holds each mass in the fabric and moves it with the accelerating background. The tilted force has an in-plane component proportional to the local embedding slope. This component adds to the field of every gravitating body an unscreened attractive term proportional to R over R-H, times c times H-naught, times the square root of r-s over r, where r-s is the body's Schwarzschild radius. Applied to the Sun, the term's form, r to the minus one-half, predicts a perihelion-precession rate that is the same for every planet: three over twice the square root of two, times the magnitude of q-naught, times R over R-H, times H-naught, which is approximately 0.008 times R over R-H milliarcseconds per year, with q-naught the present deceleration parameter given in Section 9.2. Appendix A derives this rate. Modern ephemerides bound unmodeled precession rates at the level of 4 times ten to the minus four milliarcseconds per year for Mars. The bound excludes that reading by a factor of about 600 at the smallest radius that Section 4.3 admits, and by more at any larger radius. It thereby selects the kinematic form of the postulate adopted here. Conversely, a robust future detection of a universal, planet-independent anomalous perihelion drift would falsify the postulate in its present form. On any geometry, observation already requires the bends to have no inertia, since an inertial bend would make every moving body heavier inertially than gravitationally by about the square of the ratio r over L, as Section 5.2 shows. The postulate's constraints are testable as well. They leave the geometry no scalar mode of its own, so a gravitational wave with a scalar, breathing polarization would falsify the postulate as stated. The tests of the third catalog of the LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA collaborations find no evidence for polarization modes beyond those of General Relativity.

Third, the spatial curvature is not negative. A hypersurface of flat four-dimensional space cannot have uniform negative curvature, even locally, so the inherited setting requires capital omega-k less than or equal to zero, as Section 4.1 shows. A significant measurement of capital omega-k greater than zero would falsify that setting. The combined baryon-acoustic-oscillation and microwave-background analysis of DESI finds capital omega-k equal to the quantity 2.3 plus or minus 1.1, times ten to the minus three, about two standard deviations on the side of negative curvature, which its authors do not count as a significant preference for curvature.

Section 10. Discussion and conclusion.

In this work, we extended the Cosmic Fabric Model to an open-system thermodynamic framework to resolve the instability of the negative bulk modulus, K-naught less than zero, that the vacuum's vanishing P-wave modulus requires. That instability is the one that Green raised against a medium without longitudinal waves, and the open system answers it as a recent survey of material theories of space requires of a modern construction. It does so without repairing the instability, since the medium persists at a fixed material state through continual replenishment from outside itself rather than through any stiffness of its own. By modeling the four-dimensional embedding space as a reservoir of a precursor fluid, we showed that the continuous accretion of this fluid keeps the hyperplate at its natural density as it grows. The interface kinetics act as an effective bulk viscosity and hold a small residual elastic strain. The Background Datum postulate identifies their steady rate with the constant term of General Relativity's Friedmann constraint, which governs the expansion. The matter-free fabric's expansion is then exactly de Sitter, and matter raises the rate above that floor with the gravitational constant. This thermodynamic treatment gives a mechanical account of the accelerated expansion that conserves the total energy of the fabric and its reservoir. The Background Datum postulate confines inertia to the deviations that move within the fabric, as observation requires. Since the reservoir exchanges momentum only with motion that has inertia, the slip of the fabric and of matter's static fields against it exerts no traction.

Two derivations remain open. The first is the constraint for moving and newly made matter, which the correspondence with General Relativity gives for static matter and the Background Datum postulate of Section 3.2 extends. The second is the microphysical basis of the constraints' energy of motion, whose form the postulate fixes and through which matter decelerates the expansion. The text also leaves four questions open. Two concern the residual strain, its microphysical value epsilon-res-star and the formal proof that its perturbations self-correct. The others are whether light's field loads the reservoir at the faces and the law of the reservoir's tangential response. The dynamics of the deviations poses two further problems. The first is the response of the interface kinetics to the strain fields of inclusions, including any dependence of the growth rate on the slip of their tilted faces. The second is the relaxation of a departure of the residual strain on the time tau-zeta of Section 6.2, whose observable is yet to be identified, since the residual strain's energy is datum energy.

Four calculations are likewise left to later work. The correspondence with General Relativity was derived on a flat reference plate, and its re-derivation on the closed shell, where corrections enter at order the square of R-H over R, is deferred. The hiding of the fabric's rest frame is derived for rods, clocks and light but not for the fields of moving bodies. Their divergence-free part slips against the reservoir with the traction that Section 7.1 leaves open. The scaling of the set point and the mobility with the fabric's density, the exponent s of Section 7.2 that fixes how a replenishment deficit moves w from minus one, is left to the interface's microphysics. The formation epoch, with the settling law of the interface's constants, is left to later work. Its kinetics also decide whether the fabric closes, so the closed shell on which the derivations are worked remains a choice that the fabric's origin favors.

Although the formation epoch lies outside this paper, the results above support two inferences about it. First, the fabric had a beginning. The observed content outweighs the curvature term of the Friedmann constraint at every past epoch, so the rate in the past exceeded the floor. Run backward, the growth then shrinks the volume of a bounded fabric, such as the closed shell, by more than a factor e within every interval of one over three H-de-Sitter. Within a finite past time its extent would fall to its own thickness, where its description as a thin hyperplate ends, so the growth that this paper describes began at a finite time. Second, the fabric within the horizon nucleated in a uniform state, as a single patch of uniform curvature, a spherical cap or a closed sphere alike, round to the level of the observed isotropy. Whether it closed at nucleation or grew to closure belongs to the formation epoch left above. The constraints preserve the fabric's shape rather than select it, and on the closed shell a bend takes 1.5 times ten to the power thirteen years or more to change its fractional amplitude by one e-fold, as Section 4.2 shows. The present roundness is accordingly congenital, present since nucleation rather than produced by the growth. A fabric nucleated in several patches would instead have grown into separate shells, unobservable from one another, or into shells joined along junctions. A junction within the horizon would be an extended defect of the fabric, and none has been observed. Uniform nucleation thus follows from observation rather than from an assumption of the framework.

Once the fabric has formed, the open system reproduces the standard model at the scale of the continuum. In the ideal-reservoir limit every new element joins the fabric in the same state, and the floor takes the place of the standard model's lambda component, as Section 9.2 states. Beneath that uniform history, the gross turnover of Section 3.1 re-lays material at every point of space at a rate far above the net growth. The fabric's continuum state stays fixed, while the arrangement of its material is continually renewed from a supply outside physical space. Where that arrangement matters, at the scale of galaxies and at the scale of inclusions, the openness can become observable, and the next three paragraphs take up these two scales.

Under the Background Datum postulate the background's acceleration exerts no force on inclusions beyond General Relativity's tidal term. Any galactic-scale departure from General Relativity must therefore arise from the fabric's response, and the interface supplies a natural channel. An inclusion's far-field strain gradient is the local Newtonian field divided by c squared, the magnitude of the gradient of epsilon-loc equals g-sub-N over c squared, while the floor of the homogeneous growth supplies the background's only inverse length that material constants fix, H-de-Sitter over c. Any dependence of the interface kinetics on the strain gradient therefore transitions at an acceleration set by the universal scale, c times H-de-Sitter, approximately 5.4 times ten to the minus ten meters per second squared, within an order of magnitude of the empirical acceleration scale of Modified Newtonian Dynamics. Because the net exchange becomes blind to a static field once it has re-laid the natural configuration to match it, as Section 6.1 shows, such a dependence adds no sustained growth keyed to the static field. It could set only the configurational state of the material that the turnover re-lays, which alters how the fabric transmits a static field.

An earlier paper attributed the dark-matter effect to an inherent structure of space, read through the rate of clocks, without a mechanism that produces that structure. Modeling the interface's step-mediated kinetics explicitly, in the spirit of Burton-Cabrera-Frank crystal-growth theory, could supply that mechanism and test it against rotation curves.

The vanishing energy of a volumetric misfit at the signed modulus, which Section 6.1 shows, is a first step toward modeling discrete mass-energy as defects. Whether the energy a misfit delivers to the surrounding fabric can sustain the self-generated agitation posed in Section 5.4 is a quantitative question for later work on the dynamics of the deviations. That work must also decide whether one such defect triggers a cascade of further re-layings, an avalanche, in the fabric around it.

A further question is whether the coefficients hold the same values in every epoch. The moduli and the energy density can move only in lockstep, through the identities K-naught equals minus four-thirds myu-naught and myu-naught equals u-naught, which preserve nyu equal to one and the transverse wave speed c in every epoch. The mobility script-K and the constant epsilon-res-star are instead internal parameters of the interface, and nothing forbids their slow relaxation as a freshly formed interface settles toward its mature state. Through the relation three H-de-Sitter equals the magnitude of K-naught times epsilon-res-star times script-K, divided by rho-naught squared, such settling would appear as a floor still approaching its mature value. Any such drift is a residual channel, not the primary account of the observed q-naught, approximately minus 0.53, which the matter content dominates through the Friedmann constraint. Separating the two requires fits of the expansion history that resolve their different time dependence. The quantitative treatment of the settling law is left to later work with the formation epoch.

The resolution also has a counterpart in engineering mechanics. A stiff surrounding structure ordinarily stabilizes a phase of negative stiffness, as Lakes and Drugan argued. The open system instead keeps a solid with a negative bulk modulus at a fixed material state while it grows, by replenishing it across a phase boundary. Its growth element is a Maxwell model whose anti-restoring spring sustains rather than relaxes the flow through the dashpot, as Section 6.1 states. The first-order form of that balance rests on the Background Datum postulate. In a laboratory realization the inertia that the postulate removes from the fabric's placement in the bulk would be present, and the balance would acquire an inertial term.
