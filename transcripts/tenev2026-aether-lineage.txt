The Aether Lineage: Material Theories of Space, Their Failure Modes, and Their Transferable Results.

This is a preprint in the public portfolio, first deposited to Zenodo on August 25, 2026, and revised as a second Zenodo version on August 28, 2026, under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license. By Tichomir Tenev, independent researcher.

Abstract. This is a review. It reports no new results and proposes no model, and it makes three points about a line of work usually treated as closed. The first is that the study of the aether was productive. Green, MacCullagh, Kelvin, Maxwell, Larmor, the Cosserats, de Broglie and Dirac all worked on it, and the mathematics they built for it is still in service: the strain-energy function that founded energy-based elasticity, the couple-stress mechanics that became micro-continuum theory, the defect topology that entered physics through a theory of matter which delivered no numbers. The second is that the aether was not disproven. Its constructions were set aside for other reasons, and the reasons are more specific than the textbook account allows. The vortex atom was never shown wrong by experiment and ended when its practitioners lost confidence in it, Michelson and Morley did not cause the aether's rejection, and nothing was settled against the pilot wave at Solvay in 1927. The third is that those reasons are themselves usable. Five kinds of failure recur, of delivery, construction, explanation, audience and language, and each implies a condition that a later construction must satisfy; Section 6 states the eleven requirements that follow, and assesses no construction against them. None of this argues that a material theory of space is correct.

For two and a half thousand years, the proposal that space is a material medium has been made, developed, and set aside, and then made again. This survey follows that line from the Greek aither to the condensed-matter emergence programs of the present, and asks two questions of each entry. What did it produce that outlived it, and why was it abandoned? The survey closes on the observation that the concept's disappearance was partly a change of vocabulary, documented in the words of the physicists who changed it, and in one case in a name coined a second time by a physicist who appears not to have known it had been used before. That observation is the survey's one interpretive move, it is drawn from the literature cited here, and its first statement belongs to Whittaker rather than to this document. It carries a limit. The twentieth-century vacuum recovered the medium's structural role and acquired none of its rest frame, so a claim that current physics already contains an aether erases the property that made the historical one testable. The reader is assumed to know undergraduate mechanics and electromagnetism.

Preface. Why this survey exists.

Three things make the material worth assembling, and none of them requires believing that any historical medium was correct.

The first is that the mathematics is still in service. A physicist using a hyperelastic constitutive law is using Green's aether calculation, and the standard modern term for such a material is Green-elastic. A physicist using couple-stress or micropolar continuum mechanics is using the formalism the Cosserats built for a problem the aether posed. Knot theory began as Kelvin's theory of matter. This is not a claim that the media were real. It is the narrower observation that the theories were productive at the level of structure and method, which is compatible with the complete abandonment of the objects that produced them, and in these cases abandonment is what happened.

The second is that the standard account of why they were abandoned does not survive contact with the sources. Holton established that Michelson and Morley's result was not Einstein's reason. Kragh records that the vortex atom failed by loss of confidence rather than by experiment. Bacciagaluppi and Valentini establish that no argument defeated the pilot wave at Solvay. Navarro shows that the early accounts of the aether's death reshaped the object they were describing, so that the target modern readers understand to have been refuted is partly a retrospective construction. A field that has misremembered why it discarded something has a reason to look at the record again.

The third is that the modern literature which builds media does not read the older literature which built them. Sakharov, Kleinert, Hehl and Kiefer, the Einstein-aether papers, and the analogue-gravity reviews cite nothing from the nineteenth century. The consequence is visible in Section 2: a group of mathematicians reconstructed MacCullagh's rotationally elastic medium in 2008 while citing only a text from 1909, apparently without knowing whose construction it was. Assembling the record is what makes such repetitions visible.

Nothing here argues that a material theory of space is correct. The claim is narrower. Such theories have a record of producing transferable structure, their recorded obstacles were contingent rather than principled, and that combination is what earns the line continued attention. Unification is not offered as evidence for anything.

Section 1. Purpose and method.

This document surveys the theories that treated space, or the carrier of light and force, as a material medium. It is written to serve several lines of research at once rather than any single one.

Two ontologies travel under that description, and the word aether has named both. In the first, the medium is a substance residing in space, and space remains a container distinct from its tenant; a medium of this kind can meaningfully flow, be dragged, and carry a velocity that a laboratory could in principle measure, which is what the drift experiments were built to do. The luminiferous aether of the nineteenth century is of this kind. In the second, space itself is the medium, and there is nothing distinct from the medium left over for it to move through; the aether of Einstein's Leiden address is of this kind, and his qualification that the idea of motion may not be applied to it is the mark of the kind. The survey covers both, because the lineage passes from the first to the second and the passage is part of its story. Where the distinction bears on an argument the text says which is meant, and the conflation of the two is recorded in Section 3 as part of the failure of language.

The survey has two functions that discipline each other. The first is the narrative of Section 2: the line runs continuously from antiquity to the present. The second is the scored lineage of Section 4: each precedent is scored for its failure mode and for what it can still supply. An entry earns its place by naming what transfers or by standing as an explicit recorded caution, and an entry that does neither is flagged for pruning. That rule is what keeps this document a supply chain rather than a memorial.

Grades used here are five, and each says what an entry can supply to a later construction rather than what any particular reader has taken from it. Imported marks content that has already been taken up and put to work, whether by the field at large or by a named later theory. Consonance marks a structural agreement between an entry and later work, with verification or development still outstanding. Caution marks an instructive failure, recorded so that it is not repeated. Precedent marks context only: an entry citable in an introduction, with no technical transfer. Secondary is reserved for a source that documents the lineage rather than belonging to it.

Two limits govern the document. It must not become the framework paper for any model it serves. And where a source is cited for a claim about the persistence of the medium concept, the source's own qualification is carried with it, because several of the physicists quoted in Section 5 qualify themselves in the same breath.

Section 2. The story.

The demanded medium.

The aether begins as an ontological demand rather than as a theory. In the Greek poets aither is the bright upper air, the pure substance of the high sky. Aristotle makes it systematic in De Caelo as a body prior to the four elements and more divine than they, ungenerated and indestructible, moving naturally in circles, and confined to the heavens, since everything below the Moon is composed of the four elements. He derives the name from a verb meaning to run always, so that perpetual motion sits in the word before any physics is attached to it. In the Physics he argues that no void exists separately, so that space is a plenum by necessity. The medieval quintessence carries this demand forward for a millennium and a half.

Descartes rebuilt it as mechanism. Extension alone is the nature of body, so that a vacuum is a contradiction in terms, the heavens are fluid, and a vortex with the sun at its centre carries the planets. All force is contact force. The vortex medium fills the terrestrial and the celestial regions alike, so the two-realm cosmos that had confined the quintessence to the heavens does not survive the mechanization. Newton's Principia, Book two, demonstrated that the vortices are quantitatively incompatible with Kepler's laws, which was the first occasion on which the medium was dismissed by calculation rather than by argument. Newton himself could not accept his own action at a distance. Writing to Bentley on February 25, 1692, or 1693 by the New Style calendar, he called it, quote, unconceivable, end quote, that brute matter should operate upon and affect other matter without mutual contact. He nonetheless sketched in the Opticks queries a density-graded aether whose pressure gradient would push bodies together, while conceding that he did not know what this aether is. Huygens gave the medium its first quantitative task with a wave theory of light, reasoning from Romer's observations of Jupiter's satellites that the propagation of light is successive rather than instantaneous.

The pattern set in this era persists through the whole lineage. The medium is demanded by the refusal of unmediated action, and it survives exactly as long as it does quantitative work.

The elastic-solid century.

The nineteenth century is the aether's productive period, and its central difficulty is stated at the start. Young and Fresnel established that light is transversely polarized. Fluids carry no transverse waves, so the luminiferous medium must be a solid, stiffer than steel by the wave-speed arithmetic, and yet the planets pass through it without resistance. Elasticity theory itself was developed substantially for this problem. Navier, Cauchy and Poisson built the mechanics of elastic solids with the aether as a standing client, and Poisson's demonstration that an elastic solid carries two wave types created the second difficulty, the compressional wave, which light does not exhibit. Every elastic aether must silence its longitudinal wave, and the lineage's answers to this one problem are its most instructive record.

Three answers matter here. Green, in the memoir on reflexion and refraction read in 1837, introduced the strain-energy function, deriving the medium's mechanics from a potential energy density constrained by invariance rather than from molecular force hypotheses. This is the founding document of energy-based elasticity, and the standard modern term for a hyperelastic material is a Green-elastic material. Green's earlier 1828 essay on electricity and magnetism, the source of Green's theorem, arose from Laplacian potential theory and not from optics; the two projects are separate and should not be merged, per Grattan-Guinness.

Cauchy's third aether theory of 1839 took the direct route, choosing the constants so that the compressional wave has zero velocity. This is the labile aether, revived and defended by Kelvin in 1888, who argued that the medium is stable in the manner of a film supported at its boundary. The zero-longitudinal-speed condition is a specific constitutive identity. The compressional modulus, K plus four-thirds myu, vanishes, so that K equals minus four-thirds myu, a negative bulk modulus tuned against the shear modulus. Kelvin's sections four and five identify Green's combination, A minus four-thirds B, as the bulk modulus, and his section seven then sets A equal to zero outright for the optical problem. In modern language A is the P-wave modulus M, equal to K plus four-thirds myu, so Kelvin's own condition, A equals zero, is the condition M equals zero, the same identity and the same silencing of the longitudinal wave, 138 years apart.

Kelvin's December note goes further and supplies stability material. With a spherical-globe embedding argument he shows that when A equals A-prime equals zero on both sides of an interface, stability forces B to equal B-prime. Equal rigidities are a stability requirement of the labile construction rather than a simplifying convenience. This is precedent and raw material rather than a discharge of the stability obligation, because Kelvin stabilizes a medium with M equal to zero by positing unspecified higher-order forces plus a rigid confining boundary.

Green's own objection to Cauchy is the oldest criticism of this constitution and it has never been answered. Whittaker records it: the equilibrium of the medium would be unstable if its compressibility were negative. Cauchy did not reply, and Kelvin's 1888 answer is the posited higher-order forces described above. Any modern construction carrying this identity meets an objection that is 187 years old, raised by the author of the energy method it also uses.

The third answer is the road not taken. MacCullagh in 1839 abandoned ordinary elasticity altogether. His medium stores energy in the curl of the displacement only, giving rotational stiffness with no resistance to compression or ordinary shear. The construction yields the full structure of what would later be Maxwell's equations, two decades early, with no longitudinal wave to silence. It was rejected on mechanical grounds, principally by Stokes, on two counts: no known material stores energy that way, and the required stress asymmetry is impossible in classical elasticity. Both objections dissolve in couple-stress continua, which is the mathematics the Cosserats would later build and Mindlin would complete.

The electromagnetic aether and its matter.

Maxwell built the field equations from a mechanical medium, the 1861 to 1862 honeycomb of molecular vortices with idle-wheel particles, from which the displacement current and the light speed emerged. In the 1865 dynamical theory he then discarded the machinery while keeping the dynamical structure it had produced. This double move is a lesson in method rather than a failure. The mechanism was scaffolding and the structure was the product.

Kelvin then aimed the medium at matter itself with the vortex atom of 1867, in which atoms are knotted vortex defects of the aether-fluid, topologically protected and therefore stable and countable. This is the historical origin of knot theory. It is also the lineage's most instructive failure. Kragh states the diagnosis precisely: the theory, quote, was never unambiguously proved wrong by experiment, but after twenty years of work it degenerated into mathematics, failing to deliver what it promised of physical results, end quote, and, quote, physicists simply lost confidence in the theory, end quote. He records a second and social cause, that the mechanical foundation appeared unappealing in an environment increasingly hostile to the mechanical world view. He also records that the concept survived its own refutation as a guiding picture, and that the demise of the vortex atom did not signal the demise of the aether.

The mathematical legacy of that failed program is large and is documented separately. Epple traces the topological content, and Moffatt identifies the surviving physical result as the conservation of helicity.

Larmor closed the century with the other half of the answer. His electron is a rotational strain figure of the aether, moving through the medium as a pattern rather than as a foreign body. This dissolves Fresnel's difficulty of the rigid yet permeable solid, because an inclusion is a configuration of the medium and motion through it is re-patterning rather than displacement of one substance within another. Larmor's own statement of ambition is worth recording: matter may be and likely is a structure in the aether, but the aether is certainly not a structure of matter.

The quantum medium.

De Broglie's pilot-wave theory of 1927 belongs to this lineage, and the placement is not new. Petroni and Vigier made it explicitly in 1983, deriving de Broglie waves as collective processes of a stochastic aether in Dirac's sense.

The failure here was not empirical. Bacciagaluppi and Valentini establish that nothing was settled at the 1927 Solvay conference and that no argument defeated the pilot-wave theory there. De Broglie abandoned it under his own discomfort with a guiding wave in configuration space rather than in three-space, and Colin, Durt and Willox record a second-order failure, in which the double-solution program, the version closest to a medium ontology, was eclipsed not by its opponents but by its own more tractable descendant. A medium program survived only in a form from which the medium had been removed.

The structural feature that matters for this survey is the preferred foliation. Valentini argues that pilot-wave theory does contain a preferred state of rest with an absolute time, and that its operational inaccessibility follows from the statistical equilibrium condition. His concealment is statistical and contingent rather than exact. In every single run the guidance depends instantaneously on the distant configuration, and what vanishes on averaging is the mean; the condition is a property of the state the universe relaxed into rather than a law, and he names the circumstances under which it would break. He also declines to claim necessity, holding that from this standpoint one may propose a kinematics and argue it is simplest, but never prove that it must be adopted.

The laboratory analogue is worth recording with its limits. Bush and Oza review walking-droplet hydrodynamics, in which a medium with wave memory reproduces quantized orbits, single-particle diffraction and quantum-like statistics. The analogy has a documented boundary: Andersen and co-workers failed to reproduce the double-slit interference result, and single-slit diffraction rather than two-slit interference is what survives replication.

The audience leaves.

The ending was preceded by a winnowing of versions, and the survey's claim that the aether was not disproven has to be read against that record, because versions of it were. Fizeau's measurement of the speed of light in moving water decided for Fresnel's partial convection of light against full convection, per Whittaker. The null result of Michelson and Morley excluded a stationary aether without contraction at second order. Lodge's whirling machines found no convection of light by nearby masses in rapid motion. Each experiment eliminated constitutions, which is experiment doing its ordinary work, and the claim of this survey is the narrower one: the record contains no such result against the construction that survived the winnowing.

That construction was Lorentz's. Lorentz stripped the aether of its mechanics, leaving an immobile non-mechanical medium with matter coupled to it only through the electron theory. Faced with the null result of Michelson and Morley, he and FitzGerald supplied contraction, and by 1904 the theorem of corresponding states delivered the full transformation group. With Poincare's completion the kinematics were exactly those of special relativity.

The failure mode here is subtle, because nothing in this final construction was refuted. Janssen states it as a coincidence objection rather than as a detectability objection. In Lorentz's theory it is a brute fact that the several dynamical laws are each Lorentz invariant, and the theory offers no reason why the concealment should be exact. Einstein derived the same kinematics from principle in 1905, and the audience drew the conclusion that a medium with no detectable state of motion is superfluous.

Two corrections to the standard account belong here. Holton showed that the Michelson and Morley result was not the cause of the abandonment, since Einstein consistently named the Fizeau experiment and stellar aberration when volunteering an account of the theory's origin. Navarro showed that the early accounts of the aether's death reshaped the object they described, emphasizing selected contradictions and producing a simplified target. The aether that the modern reader understands to have been refuted is partly a retrospective construction.

The Cosserats published their unified deformable-media mechanics into that departing audience in 1909, deriving balance laws and couple-stress from Euclidean invariance of a single action. The work was aimed at the lineage's whole agenda, was constitutively empty, and found no audience. Through Mindlin's revival it is now the formal language of couple-stress continua.

Einstein reopened the door a decade later. The Leiden address of May 5, 1920 grants that general-relativistic space is endowed with physical qualities, and that in this sense an aether exists. The qualification is immediate and must be carried with the quotation: this medium may not be thought of as consisting of parts trackable through time, and the idea of motion may not be applied to it. Dirac argued in 1951 that quantum mechanics removes the classical objection to a velocity-bearing aether. That objection was that an aether velocity picks out a preferred direction inside the light cone, which contradicts the isotropy relativity demands. Dirac's answer is that the aether velocity is subject to the uncertainty relations like any other variable, so a state can be built in which all aether velocities are equally probable, and such a state can respect the principle of relativity. His analogy is the hydrogen atom, which cannot be spherically symmetric on the classical picture and is so in any S-state. The velocity was not introduced for the purpose. It came out of a constraint on the potentials in his new electrodynamics, which yields a unit timelike four-velocity at every point of spacetime, so that in his words, quote, we are rather forced to have an aether, end quote. Bokulich places the move in Dirac's standing conviction that physical theories stay open to revision, against Heisenberg's view that they close.

Dirac stated the cost himself, and the rule of Section 1 is to carry it. The wave function representing the Lorentz-symmetric state is constant over the hyperboloid of unit four-velocities, its modulus squared integrates to infinity, and it therefore cannot be normalized. The perfect vacuum becomes an idealized state that can be approached but never attained. He accepted that consequence, observing that unnormalizable states are indispensable elsewhere in quantum theory. The aether was nonetheless required by the formalism rather than added to it. When Infeld showed in 1952 that the field equations could be written without one, Dirac replied that this, quote, is not sufficient to make a complete dynamical theory, end quote. An action principle and a Hamiltonian formulation suitable for quantization are needed, and, quote, for this the aether velocity is required, end quote. He carried the line on into 1953.

Wright reads the proposal as a purposeful use of anachronism meant to shock contemporaries in relativistic quantum theory, and Navarro adds Wright's point that the archaic spelling advertised the programme. Neither reading empties the content. Wright holds that Dirac's aether was more physical and substantial than the versions Whittaker and Eddington had proposed, and he closes by correcting Rosenfeld's public jibe that Dirac was conjuring the ghost of the aether. Dirac attempted a resurrection.

The theological aether.

The nineteenth-century medium also carried religious and spiritualist uses, and the survey records them because they are part of the failure record rather than because they bear on any modern construction.

Cantor supplies the analysis. The same explanatory role, a space-filling medium, was recruited to opposite metaphysical ends, and the constructions sort into three families. Animate or spiritual aethers supported divine immanence. Semi-material aethers attracted dualists. Material, mechanistic, contact-action aethers attracted atheists and Low Churchmen, precisely because eliminating action at a distance removes any need for continuous divine sustaining. Connor gives named instances of the same divergence, with Whewell reading the medium's artificial complexity as evidence of design while Tyndall used identical reasoning for materialism.

The conspicuous episodes are two. Stewart and Tait argued in 1875 from the conservation and dissipation of energy that the visible universe had a beginning and will have an end, and must therefore be continuous with an unseen order, with the medium as the interface. Lodge assigned the medium an enormous density and elasticity together with no detectable drag, and left open, tentatively, whether it carries a psychical significance. Maxwell reviewed the sequel to the first of these in Nature in 1878 and opened by doubting whether it fell within his jurisdiction, since it is not the practice of that journal to review either novels or theological works.

The chronology settles the causal question. Specialist opinion turned against the aether between roughly 1905 and 1920, whereas Lodge's spiritualist writing on the medium peaked between 1916 and 1935 and was addressed to theologians and to the bereaved rather than to physicists. Noakes describes this as a post-relativistic afterlife in non-specialist circles, and records that contemporaries doubted whether an outmoded medium could lend credibility to psychical research, which is the opposite of the usual assumption. Stokes, Larmor and Kelvin were medium theorists of the first rank and were unsympathetic to psychical research, so the correlation required by a social-determination account does not hold.

What the episode does contribute is a distinct failure mode, developed in Section 3: a term that serves scientific, technological, popular and religious purposes at once loses the precision that made it usable.

The diaspora and the return.

The aether did not die. It emigrated, and its mathematics survived wherever it had produced structure. The energy function of Green became the foundation of continuum mechanics. The defects of Kelvin became topology in physics. Cosserat mechanics returned as the micro-continuum theories of the 1960s, in Mindlin's formulation. Condensed-matter physics became the working heir and sent its results back into fundamental physics, so that the Higgs mechanism is Anderson's superconductor result and dynamical chiral mass is Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer reasoning transplanted by Nambu and Jona-Lasinio.

One claim previously made here is withdrawn. The rotational-energy construction of MacCullagh did not become gauge structure. Jackson and Okun trace gauge invariance through the non-uniqueness of the vector potential rather than through mechanical models. The defensible statement is narrower: the vector potential, which Maxwell introduced with mechanical motivation as the electrotonic state, is the object whose redundancy later became gauge freedom.

The return began with a proposal that was not about a medium at all. Sakharov argued in 1967 that gravitation is not a fundamental interaction but an induced effect, the action for curvature arising from the vacuum fluctuations of quantized matter fields, and he called the coefficient that carries it the metrical elasticity of space. Section 5 takes up the name. The physics has not delivered. His own expression for the induced constant carries a minus sign against a positive divergent integral, so a physical constant requires a negative coefficient, and the modern form of the same difficulty is that a supertrace over the particle spectrum must come out with the right sign while scalars and fermions contribute with opposite ones. The cutoff is asserted at the Planck scale rather than derived. Visser records that Pauli's route to a finite result imposes conditions on the particle spectrum that Pauli himself called improbable, and that stacking the known constraints leaves no explicit model satisfying them.

The living end of the lineage no longer asks whether a medium could underlie fields and geometry. It exhibits media that do so in the laboratory. Volovik gives the Fermi-point scenario, in which Lorentz invariance is protected by momentum-space topology rather than tuned. Levin and Wen give string-net condensation, from which gauge bosons and fermions emerge together. Barcelo, Liberati and Visser survey analogue gravity, in which an effective curved Lorentzian metric is a generic result of linearizing a field theory around a non-trivial background.

Two features of that literature bear on this survey. Its practitioners police the boundary between analogy and identity, and they distinguish emergent kinematics, which is generic, from emergent dynamics, which is not obtained. And its historical horizon is short: the analogue-gravity review dates its own history to 1923, and the one professional history of the analogue-gravity program begins at 1971. The connection between the nineteenth-century elastic medium and the condensed-matter emergence programs is not drawn anywhere in that literature.

One result from that literature bears directly on the lineage's decisive experiment. Barcelo and Jannes show that observers built from a medium's own excitations contract by exactly the factor that masks their motion relative to the substrate. A bound pair whose interaction is carried by a collective field of the medium has its equilibrium separation reduced by the same Lorentz factor that squashes the boosted field, with no small-velocity approximation anywhere. Jannes draws the consequence for interferometry, that a null drift result does not falsify a medium of this kind. The limits are stated by the authors: the argument is static and applies to one bonded pair in flat space, the step rescaling the interaction energy is argued as natural rather than proven for a general potential, and the conclusion is non-falsification rather than confirmation. Lorentz proposed the same mechanism, molecular forces transmitted through the medium, which those authors record.

The consequence of that short horizon is visible in a single case. Burnett, Chervova and Vassiliev model spacetime as a Cosserat continuum which, in their words, cannot experience displacements but only rotations, and derive the Dirac equation from it in one-plus-two dimensions without spinors or Pauli matrices. A medium with rotational stiffness and no response to stretch or ordinary shear is MacCullagh's construction of 1839. Their reference list contains no MacCullagh, no Kelvin, no Green, no Cauchy, no Stokes, no Larmor and no Whittaker; the only historical citation is the Cosserat text of 1909. They present the result as a mathematical equivalence rather than as a claim about a physical medium, and they record that the general three-plus-one-dimensional case is not equivalent to the Dirac equation because it lacks one real dynamical degree of freedom. This is the lineage's constitutive discoveries being made a second time by researchers who cannot know they are repetitions, and it is the clearest available argument that a survey of this kind is worth writing.

Section 3. Failure modes.

The lineage's failures fall into five kinds. Each is stated here with the cases that establish it, the evidence for the diagnosis, and the condition a later construction must satisfy to avoid repeating it. Section 2 tells the story in chronological order, and this section cuts across that order to group the endings by their cause, which is where the pattern becomes visible. It is the survey's central analysis, and the scored lineage of Section 4 grades each entry against it.

Failure of delivery.

A medium survives only as long as it does quantitative work, and three cases mark out the ways that work can fail to arrive.

Descartes' vortices were refuted outright. Newton demonstrated in Book two of the Principia that a plenum of circulating subtle matter cannot reproduce Kepler's laws, and the refutation was quantitative rather than philosophical. This is the cleanest of the three failures and the least instructive, because a construction that makes a wrong prediction can simply be abandoned.

Kelvin's vortex atom failed differently, and its ending is the one worth studying. The 1867 proposal made atoms knotted vortex defects of the aether-fluid, topologically protected and therefore stable and countable, which is the shape a theory of matter ought to have. Twenty years of work produced no spectrum, no mass, and no quantitative contact of any kind. Kragh records that the theory was never unambiguously proved wrong by experiment, that it degenerated into mathematics while failing to deliver what it promised of physical results, and that physicists simply lost confidence in it. Michelson's remark that the theory ought to be true even if it is not measures how far its appeal had outrun its delivery.

The same case shows that failure of delivery is not failure of value. Epple traces the topological content the program generated, and Moffatt identifies the surviving physical result as the conservation of helicity. A program can fail on its own terms and still leave mathematics behind, which is why Table 1 grades what transfers separately from how the program ended.

The Cosserats failed in a third way, by never making a commitment that could be tested. Their 1909 mechanics derived balance laws and couple-stress from the invariance of a single action and named no constitutive law, so there was nothing to compute and nothing to check. It lay dormant for sixty years.

The condition is that every claim states what quantity it produces. A program that cannot say which number it delivers has already failed, whatever its formal appeal.

Failures of construction.

Four distinct construction problems recur, and the first is the elastic medium's standing difficulty.

The longitudinal wave has to be silenced, and the lineage's answers to that one problem are its most instructive record. The chain that creates the problem is short. Young and Fresnel established that light is transversely polarized, fluids carry no transverse waves, so the medium must be a solid, and Poisson then showed that an elastic solid carries two wave types rather than one. The second type, the compressional wave, is not observed in light. Cauchy's answer in 1839 was to choose the elastic constants so that its speed vanishes, which fixes the bulk modulus at K equals minus four-thirds myu, and Kelvin revived the same condition in 1888 as A equals zero. The identity is correct and the derivation is absent. Green's objection is the oldest criticism on the record and has never been answered, that a medium of negative compressibility is unstable at equilibrium. The condition is therefore double. The silencing must follow from a stated physical requirement rather than from a choice of constants, and the resulting negative modulus must answer Green rather than postpone him.

The second problem is that rigidity and permeability appear to exclude one another. The wave-speed arithmetic makes the luminiferous medium stiffer than steel, and the planets nonetheless pass through it without measurable resistance. Larmor dissolved the difficulty by making matter a pattern of the medium rather than a body within it, so that his electron is a rotational strain figure and its motion is a re-patterning. The condition is that motion through the medium be re-patterning rather than the displacement of one substance inside another.

The third problem is that a conservative action cannot carry history. Every action in the lineage was conservative, and no such action reaches dissipative phenomenology, so any process with a memory falls outside what the formalism can express. Growth kinetics and internal-variable bookkeeping put history where a potential cannot.

The fourth problem is that a medium can acquire its properties one at a time, each introduced to carry the phenomenon in front of it. Kelvin's gyrostatic aether of 1890 is the clearest instance, supplying rotational stiffness by assembling a machinery of flywheels rather than by deriving it from anything the medium already had, and it was never adopted. The Cosserats stand at the other extreme, deriving balance laws and couple-stress from a single action while naming no constitutive law at all. Two further cases bound what the condition may ask. Stokes rejected MacCullagh because no known material stores energy in the curl of a displacement alone, which was correct in 1839 and false by 1909, so the demand is internal consistency rather than agreement with the materials already catalogued. And Maxwell reached the displacement current and the speed of light through a honeycomb of vortex cells and idle wheels that he discarded on arrival, so the demand binds the construction delivered rather than the route taken to it. The condition is that the medium carry one constitution, that every field claimed as one of its excitations follow from that constitution, and that a field which does not be named as an independent entity with its coupling stated.

Failure of explanation.

An undetectable rest frame requires a common origin rather than a set of separate accidents, and Lorentz's theory is the case that establishes the requirement.

Nothing in that theory was refuted. Faced with the null result of Michelson and Morley, Lorentz and FitzGerald supplied contraction. By 1904 the theorem of corresponding states delivered the full transformation group, and with Poincare's completion the kinematics were exactly those of special relativity. What the theory lacked was a reason. Janssen states the objection precisely, and it is not about detectability. In Lorentz's theory it is a brute fact that the several dynamical laws are each Lorentz invariant, and the theory offers no account of why the concealment should be exact. Janssen works the point through five cases, contraction, time dilation, the Fresnel drag coefficient, the velocity dependence of electron mass, and the null torque of Trouton and Noble. Each is a separate dynamical posit in Lorentz's hands, and each follows in relativity from one structural fact.

The objection is more permissive than its reputation. Janssen disqualifies exactly one thing, that the laws each happen to be invariant as brute fact, and he states that Minkowski spacetime is not the only admissible common origin. Another option, in his words, is that invariance is a property of some unified theory for the interactions concerned. He records that the electromagnetic view of nature was a variant of that project, and that it was eliminated empirically rather than in principle.

Two conditions follow. A construction that derives universal invariance from one source satisfies the requirement, and a construction in which each law is separately invariant does not. The second condition is a restriction on presentation. Janssen holds that a deeper theory does not license moving the phenomena in which invariance manifests itself back from kinematics into dynamics, so contraction, dilation, the Fresnel drag, the velocity dependence of mass and the Trouton-Noble result must not be re-explained as system-specific dynamical effects.

Two further constraints bear on this same requirement but arise from modern physics rather than from the historical record. They are stated with the other requirements in Section 6.

Failure of audience.

Programs end for reasons of succession and dissemination as well as for reasons of physics, and three cases show the mechanism.

MacCullagh's rotational medium was rejected on grounds that were correct when they were made and became wrong later. Stokes objected on two counts, that no known material stores energy in the curl of a displacement alone, and that the stress asymmetry the construction requires is impossible in classical elasticity. Both objections dissolve in couple-stress continua, which is to say that the mathematics needed to answer Stokes arrived about seventy years after the objection was raised. The eclipse of MacCullagh lasted fifty years, and nothing in the physics changed during it.

The Cosserats published into an audience that was leaving. Their mechanics was aimed at the lineage's whole agenda at the moment the lineage was being abandoned, and it found no readers, so that Mindlin's revival in the 1960s is what made it the standard language of couple-stress continua. Kragh records a related cause in the vortex atom's decline, that its mechanical foundation had come to seem unappealing in an environment increasingly hostile to the mechanical world view. That is a fact about the audience rather than about the theory.

The pilot-wave theory was set aside without being defeated. Bacciagaluppi and Valentini establish that no argument settled the question at the 1927 Solvay conference, and de Broglie abandoned the theory under his own discomfort with a guiding wave in configuration space. Colin, Durt and Willox record a second-order version of the same failure, in which the double-solution program, the version closest to a medium ontology, was eclipsed not by its opponents but by its own more tractable descendant.

The condition is structural rather than rhetorical. Results must be recorded in documents that survive their authors, and obligations must be written down rather than carried in the memory of a research group.

Failure of language.

A term that serves scientific, technological, popular and religious purposes at once loses the precision that made it usable, and the nineteenth century supplies the demonstration.

Cantor shows that the same explanatory role was recruited to opposite metaphysical ends. Animate or spiritual aethers supported divine immanence, semi-material aethers attracted dualists, and material, mechanistic, contact-action aethers attracted atheists and Low Churchmen, because eliminating action at a distance removes any need for continuous divine sustaining. Connor gives the divergence in named instances, with Whewell reading the medium's artificial complexity as evidence of design while Tyndall used identical reasoning for materialism. A word that can be turned to either purpose is doing no discriminating work.

The dilution outlasted the physics. Lodge's writing on the medium peaked between 1916 and 1935, after specialist opinion had already turned, and Noakes records that contemporaries doubted whether an outmoded medium could lend credibility to psychical research rather than the reverse. Navarro gives the mechanism in its general form, arguing that the multiplicity of the aether's uses may itself explain its disappearance rather than having delayed it.

The modern instance is the word's present state, in which it names both a research program in general relativity carrying published parameter bounds and a large body of work that engages no constraint at all.

That research program supplies the sharpest evidence for the mechanism, because of how it handles the name. Einstein-aether gravity adds to general relativity a dynamical unit timelike vector field, which is a preferred frame that is itself an object of the theory. Jacobson and Mattingly remark that it is convenient to give the field a name, and that aether seems as good a name as any. The status report says the field defines a congruence of timelike curves filling all of spacetime, like an omnipresent fluid, and so has been dubbed the aether. Neither paper defends the choice, apologizes for it, or cites anything from the century that built the object. The word now travels as a bare label, carried for convenience and detached from the content that once made it a claim about the world.

A further equivocation is ontological rather than social, and it is the one stated in Section 1. The nineteenth-century aether was a substance residing in space, with a state of motion a laboratory could in principle measure. The aether Einstein readmitted at Leiden is space itself endowed with physical qualities, with no state of motion even in principle, and his qualification that the idea of motion may not be applied to it marks a difference in kind rather than a hedge on the concession. The two ontologies share the name and share the light-carrying function, and they differ in what would count as detecting them, so an argument against the first does not transfer to the second without further work. A reader who carries the drift experiments from the one to the other has changed the subject without changing the word.

Two conditions follow. A medium must be defined operationally, stating among other things whether it resides in space or is space, and its extensions refused. And the retrospective caricature identified by Navarro must be answered with the historical record rather than assumed away, because the object modern readers take to have been refuted is not the object the nineteenth century built.

The unification discipline.

One further rule governs this survey rather than the lineage. Unification is the residue of solved problems and is never the thesis. The mechanics of the Cosserats is the anti-pattern, unifying from a single action while committing to no constitution and reaching no audience. Morrison gives the general form of the caution, that unification and explanation often have little to do with one another, and that the Maxwell case unified optics and electromagnetism in 1865 but was not accepted until Hertz produced free waves in 1887. A research program's claim must therefore be to cross-domain predictions rather than to unification itself.

Section 4. The scored lineage.

Table 1 scores the lineage. Each entry names what it proposed, how it ended, what it left behind, and the grade of Section 1. Where a classifying pattern adds something the grade does not already say, it is named after the grade: rejected-then-revived, backward import where fundamental physics has already borrowed from a medium, living program, or mathematics-only where formalism transfers but ontology does not.

Table 1, read as a list. Its caption reads: the lineage scored; every entry names what transfers or stands as an explicit caution, and entries with neither are flagged for pruning.

The Greek aither and Aristotle proposed a body prior to the four elements, with no separate void; its failure mode is that it was displaced by mechanics and never became quantitative; what transfers is the plenum demand and the lineage's origin; its standing is precedent.

Descartes, in 1644, proposed a vortex plenum carrying the planets; its failure mode is that it was refuted by Newton's Book two calculation; what transfers is the lesson that mechanism without measure ends by calculation; its standing is caution.

Huygens, in 1690, proposed light as waves in a medium; its failure mode is that it was superseded, and that its waves were longitudinal; what transfers is the wave-medium task description; its standing is precedent.

Newton's queries proposed a density-graded aether for gravity; the failure mode is that it was never developed, held back by the author's own scruples; what transfers is action at a distance as a difficulty for its own proponent; its standing is precedent.

Fresnel, Navier, Cauchy and Poisson proposed light as shear waves in an elastic solid; the failure mode is the conflict between rigidity and permeability, and the longitudinal wave; what transfers is the problem statement every elastic medium must answer; the standing is precedent.

Green, in 1838, proposed aether mechanics from a strain-energy function; the failure mode is sound method applied to the wrong client, which became the foundation of elasticity; what transfers is the energy-based constitutive method; the standing is imported by the whole field, with the pattern noted as backward import.

Cauchy in 1839 and Kelvin in 1888 proposed zero longitudinal speed via A equals zero, that is, K equals minus four-thirds myu; the failure mode is tuning without derivation, with Green's stability objection left unanswered; what transfers is the labile constitutive identity and Kelvin's equal-rigidity stability argument; the standing is consonance, verified at primary source, with the pattern noted as rejected-then-revivable.

MacCullagh, in 1839 or 1848, proposed purely rotational elasticity, reaching the Maxwell structure two decades early; the failure mode is that it was rejected as mechanically unrealizable, by Stokes; what transfers is the complete rotational-medium optics; the standing is imported, with the pattern noted as rejected-then-revived.

Kelvin's 1890 gyrostatic aether proposed a mechanical realization of rotational stiffness; the failure mode is contrived machinery that was never adopted; what transfers is context only, that rotational stiffness admits a mechanical realization at all; the standing is precedent.

Maxwell, from 1861 to 1865, built field equations from a vortex-cell medium and then discarded the scaffolding; this is not a failure but a method lesson; what transfers is the displacement current's origin and the idea of mechanism as scaffolding; the standing is imported, as method, with the pattern noted as backward import.

Kelvin's 1867 vortex atoms proposed matter as knotted defects of the medium; the failure mode is degeneration into mathematics, a loss of confidence rather than a refutation; what transfers is topological matter and helicity conservation; the standing is caution.

Larmor, in 1900, proposed the electron as a rotational strain figure of the aether; the failure mode is that it was absorbed into electron theory and the medium was dropped; what transfers is matter as pattern, and the dissolving of the permeability difficulty; the standing is consonance, with the pattern noted as precedent.

De Broglie in 1927 and Bohm in 1952 proposed a quantum particle guided by a real wave; the failure mode is that it was not defeated at Solvay but abandoned by its own author; what transfers is the preferred foliation, as a structural feature rather than a defect; the standing is caution.

Valentini, in 2005, proposed a preferred foliation with statistical concealment; the failure mode is that the concealment is contingent on equilibrium, with necessity disclaimed; what transfers is the equilibrium-concealment mechanism, and its limits; the standing is consonance, with the pattern noted as living program.

Lorentz, from 1892 to 1904, proposed an immobile non-mechanical aether in which invariance is a set of separate accidents; the failure mode is that its kinematics were kept as special relativity while the medium was declared superfluous; what transfers is the rest-frame ontology with exact undetectability; the standing is imported, with the pattern noted as rejected-then-revived.

Cosserat, in 1909, proposed unified couple-stress mechanics from one Euclidean action; the failure mode is that it was constitutively empty, conservative only, and found its audience gone; what transfers is the micropolar formal language and the three-failure record; the standing is imported and caution, with the pattern noted as rejected-then-revived via Mindlin.

Stewart and Tait in 1875, and Lodge in 1909, proposed the medium as a bridge to a future state; the failure mode is semantic dilution and retrospective caricature; what transfers is Cantor's typology of theological uses; the standing is caution.

Einstein's 1920 Leiden address proposed general-relativistic space as a medium with qualities but no velocity; this is not a failure but a licence, carried with its qualification; what transfers is citable modern medium language; the standing is precedent.

Dirac, in 1951, proposed the quantum vacuum as a velocity-bearing aether, announced in a two-page letter and pursued into the mid-1950s as a new classical theory of electrons; the failure mode is that the programme found no adopters, with Wright calling it unsuccessful and idiosyncratic, and Dirac's own caveat being that the relativistic vacuum state cannot be normalized; what transfers is the relativity-compatibility of a rest frame by a quantum route, the aether velocity being subjected to the uncertainty relations; the standing is precedent.

Sakharov, in 1967, proposed gravitation as an induced effect, the curvature action arising from vacuum fluctuations of quantized matter fields, carried by what he named the metrical elasticity of space; the failure mode is that the induced constant's sign is not secured, the cutoff is asserted rather than derived, and no explicit model meeting the known constraints is on record after fifty-nine years; what transfers is the founding statement of the emergent-gravity programme, and the naming itself, coined a second time in apparent ignorance of its precedent; the standing is precedent, with the pattern noted as backward import.

The Kleinert world crystal proposed gravity from defects in a crystal with only second-gradient elasticity; the failure mode is that it is linearized only, with the author declining a claim to reality; what transfers is the defect-gauge construction and a contrasting degenerate elasticity; the standing is precedent, with the pattern noted as living program.

Hehl and Kiefer, in 2018, proposed reading the DeWitt supermetric as a constitutive tensor; the failure mode is that no substrate claim is made and the identity is left undeveloped; what transfers is an independent arrival at lambda equals minus one, myu equals one-half, K equals minus four-thirds myu; the standing is consonance.

Burnett, Chervova and Vassiliev, in 2008, proposed a Cosserat continuum with rotations only, from which the Dirac equation is derived; the failure mode is that a mathematical equivalence is claimed rather than a medium, and the three-plus-one-dimensional case is incomplete; what transfers is MacCullagh's constitution rebuilt without knowledge of it, and the evidence that this survey is needed; the standing is caution, with the pattern noted as rejected-then-rediscovered.

Einstein-aether theory, from 2001, proposes general relativity plus a dynamical unit timelike vector field, a preferred frame that is itself an object of the theory, with four coupling constants; no failure mode is recorded, the programme being current and constrained, though its own status report leaves loop-induced Lorentz violation in the matter sector open; what transfers is the demonstration that a preferred frame can be constrained rather than dismissed in principle, published bounds on the couplings, and a worked instance of the naming failure of Section 3; the standing is precedent, with the pattern noted as living program.

Whittaker, in 1910, stands as a secondary source; what transfers is the umbrella documentation of the lineage; the standing is secondary.

The living descendants, Volovik, string-nets, analogue gravity, and Kleinert, comprise media that demonstrably emit gauge fields, fermions and effective metrics; no failure mode is recorded, these programs being current; what transfers is universality theorems and laboratory existence proofs; the standing is imported and in use, with the pattern noted as living programs.

Two entries in Table 1 require a note on standing. Hehl and Kiefer reach the same constitutive identity from the DeWitt supermetric on superspace, and their arrival is independent. Their equation nineteen sets the totally symmetric part of the supermetric to zero, which through their own equation fifteen is the statement lambda plus two myu equals zero at their values, but they do not evaluate it, do not name the P-wave modulus, and do not discuss wave content anywhere. The words Poisson, shear modulus, longitudinal and transverse do not occur in their paper. The identity is present in their algebra and is not extracted.

The world crystal of Kleinert is a different construction rather than a competing version of the same one. His action carries a single elastic constant, no second Lame parameter is ever introduced, and the entire first-gradient energy is discarded rather than tuned, because first-gradient elasticity gives the wrong Green function for the Einstein action. A construction that retains first-gradient elasticity and sets M, equal to lambda plus two myu, equal to zero, with myu not equal to zero, is not the same object.

Section 5. What returned, and under what name.

This section carries the survey's one interpretive move. The claim is that the medium concept was not refuted but renamed, and that the renaming has recurred.

The claim is not new, and its first statement belongs to Whittaker. His preface to the 1951 edition makes the argument in three steps. The word fell out of favour after the failure of the drift experiments. Quantum electrodynamics then gave the vacuum zero-point oscillations, charge fluctuations and a polarization corresponding to a dielectric constant different from unity. It is therefore absurd, he concludes, to retain the name vacuum for an entity so rich in physical properties. What this survey adds is the evidence of recurrence, which Whittaker could not have seen.

The recurrence is documented in the words of its authors. Laughlin writes that the modern concept of the vacuum, confirmed every day by experiment, is a relativistic aether, and that the word is not used because it is taboo. Wen coins qubit ether. Levin and Wen put quantum ether in the title of a Physical Review paper. Volovik calls the quantum vacuum the aether of the twenty-first century, always with a qualifier attached.

Those authors all know what they are invoking. The stronger form of the recurrence is the case where the name is reinvented rather than borrowed. Sakharov called the coefficient carrying his induced gravitational action the metrical elasticity of space, in those words, and the phrase is his own. His bibliography spans 1948 to 1967, cites no work in continuum mechanics and no aether theory of any period, and the word aether does not occur in the paper. Visser, reviewing the programme thirty-five years later, uses elasticity once as an analogy, never repeats Sakharov's phrase, and likewise cites nothing from the nineteenth century. The structural role the elastic aether had occupied was reoccupied, and the name for it was coined a second time by someone with no apparent knowledge that it had been used before.

Wilczek makes the strongest form of the claim and then withdraws part of it, and both halves must be reported. He argues three things. The popular account is backwards, since Einstein first purified and then enthroned the concept rather than abolishing it. General-relativistic spacetime became a dynamical medium, an aether if ever there was one. And particles come and go while the aethers abide. His final sentence is a retraction in advance: that just maybe, despite everything he has said, we really may eventually have to do without an aether. Any use of this column in support of the persistence claim must carry that sentence with it.

Whittaker's own case is also the caution attached to this move. His continuity thesis produced the second volume's treatment of relativity as the theory of Poincare and Lorentz, which was rejected by the great majority of scholars and which Navarro reassesses in the light of the low standing of general relativity in the early 1950s. The survey's claim must therefore be bounded: the concept persisted under renaming, and 1905 was nonetheless a genuine break. Volume one of Whittaker's history remains a standard reference and volume two is cited today chiefly in connection with this controversy.

A philosophical form of the productivity claim already exists, and the survey is not entitled to it. Worrall argues that what survived the shift from Fresnel's medium to Maxwell's field was mathematical structure and not content, in a sentence that must be quoted exactly: quote, there was continuity or accumulation in the shift, but the continuity is one of form or structure, not of content, end quote. He attributes the thesis to Poincare. The guard-rail is in the same paper. Worrall holds that Fresnel's elastic solid aether was entirely overthrown, and that the theoretical mechanisms Fresnel postulated are not approximations to, or limiting cases of, the mechanisms of the later theory. He also rejects reinterpreting Fresnel as having meant the electromagnetic field, calling that rational reconstruction taken too far. Saatsi maps the subsequent dispute and finds both Worrall's structuralism and the stronger reading of Psillos wanting, concluding that the term itself is not central in the explanatory sense.

Three consequences follow for this survey. Its claim is about the persistence and renaming of a research strategy, not about the reference of a term. It may not cite Worrall as support for the stronger position that the medium was vindicated, because Worrall denies exactly that. And a survey arguing that the constructions of the lineage were productive must state, as Worrall does, that productivity at the level of transferable mathematics is compatible with the complete abandonment of the object that produced it.

Two further limits on the claim must be stated with it.

The first is the cut between what returned and what did not. The structural role of the medium returned in full, since the vacuum of current physics has order parameters, is polarizable, and has particles as its excitations. The kinematic signature did not return, because the vacuum of quantum field theory is Lorentz invariant and therefore picks out no rest frame. A claim that current physics already contains an aether erases the property that made the historical aether testable.

The second limit concerns the surviving vocabulary. The words permittivity and permeability were coined by Heaviside and by Kelvin respectively to name a medium's responsiveness, and the definition of relative permittivity as a ratio to that of free space is a construction that reads free space as one dielectric among others. That continuity is lexical and it is real. It does not carry ontological weight. From 1948 to 2019 the vacuum permeability was exactly four pi times ten to the minus seven newtons per ampere squared, by definition, and no property of a physical medium is fixed by decision. Since 2019 both constants are rearrangements of the fine-structure constant, and both vanish under a change of unit system. The defensible statement is that the naming and the constitutive-relation grammar of the nineteenth century survive, while the 2019 redefinition completed the removal of any sense in which these constants report a measurement.

Section 6. What a modern construction must supply.

Those failure modes of Section 3 that bear on a construction, restated as standing requirements, together with two constraints that come from modern physics rather than from the historical record. The failure of audience is not among them, since a construction cannot supply its own readers; its lesson bears on how work is recorded rather than on what a model must contain. Figure 2 traces each requirement back to the failure that generates it.

Figure 2, described in words. The figure shows the five recorded failure kinds and the unification discipline on the left, the eleven standing requirements on the right, and arrows mapping each failure to the requirements it generates; two requirements are drawn in orange and fed from a separate box marked as not coming from the historical record. The caption reads: how the recorded failures become the requirements. Nine of the eleven requirements are read off the historical record; the two in orange are not, and the survey says so, they come from modern physics, by way of Collins and co-workers, Liberati and Maccione, and Weinberg and Witten. One failure kind generates no requirement and is drawn with no outgoing arrow. The failure of audience is a fact about how work was received, not a property a construction can carry, and a theory cannot supply its own readers; its lesson falls instead on the practice of recording results and obligations in documents that outlast their authors, which is why Section 3 states it and Section 6 does not restate it as a demand on any model.

Requirement one. A stated quantity at every stage. Every claim must state what quantity it produces. This requirement never closes.

Requirement two. The longitudinal wave silenced by identity rather than by choice. The silencing must follow from a stated physical condition rather than from a choice of constants. Kelvin's stability argument is available as precedent material. Green's objection to a negative bulk modulus is 187 years old and must be answered in the text rather than deferred.

Requirement three. Matter that passes through the medium. Motion through the medium must be a re-patterning rather than the displacement of one substance inside another. Larmor's strain figure is the precedent.

Requirement four. Invariance from a common origin, preserved under every extension. Janssen licenses a unified theory as such an origin, so the requirement is to derive invariance from one source rather than to obtain it separately for each law. The restriction on presentation applies: the phenomena in which invariance manifests itself must not be returned from kinematics to dynamics.

Requirement five. Radiative stability of the derived invariance. This requirement becomes applicable once the matter description is quantized. Collins and co-workers show that a preferred frame entering the ultraviolet region of loop integrals regenerates dimension-four Lorentz-violating operators suppressed only by low-energy couplings, giving fractional differences of order 0.1 to 10 percent against an experimental ceiling well below ten to the minus twenty. Their argument rests on the matter fields being quantized and described by an ordinary interacting field theory, since the effect is a self-energy loop. Their own stated escape is a custodial symmetry that forbids the dimension-four terms without being the full Lorentz group, which they report as not known to exist. Liberati and Maccione give the review-level form of the requirement, that either a symmetry or some other mechanism protects the lower-dimension operators, or the suppression of the non-renormalizable operators exceeds that of the renormalizable ones. They also supply the calibration that decides whether a bound carries theoretical information. The natural suppression is m-sub-e over M-sub-Planck, approximately four times ten to the minus twenty-three, so any constraint of order ten to the minus twenty-two or weaker is ineffective for learning about the underlying theory. This requirement is discharged nowhere in the modern preferred-frame literature, which is worth stating because it locates the difficulty. Jacobson puts it plainly for Einstein-aether gravity: assuming that matter does not couple to the aether at tree level goes against the precepts of effective field theory, since Lorentz violation in the gravitational sector would presumably induce Lorentz-violating terms in the matter action through loop effects. He records this as an open problem rather than answering it. The bounds themselves are compiled by Kostelecky and Russell, in tables revised annually that must therefore be cited by version. Two features bear on any assessment. Every bound is reported in a common Sun-centred celestial-equatorial frame, a convention adopted so that unrelated experiments become comparable rather than a claim that any frame is physically preferred. And the compilation supplies no naturalness calibration at all, since a bound's units fix the mass dimension of the operator it constrains but comparing across dimensions needs a reference mass the tables never state. The division of labour is therefore clean. The tables record what has been measured, and Collins and co-workers and Liberati and Maccione decide whether a given number carries information about the underlying theory.

Requirement six. A stated evasion of the composite-graviton obstruction. This constrains the unification claim rather than the invariance claim. Weinberg and Witten forbid a massless spin-two particle carrying a Lorentz-covariant conserved stress-energy tensor, which is the standing obstruction to obtaining gravity as a collective mode. Any construction of this kind must state which of the known evasions it takes.

Requirement seven. A matter description that produces numbers or records the obligation. Governed by the vortex-atom caution.

Requirement eight. Unification as residue. A standing discipline. This survey must remain a background review.

Requirement nine. History outside the action. Every action in the lineage was conservative, and no conservative action reaches dissipative phenomenology. History must therefore sit outside the action.

Requirement ten. An operational definition, with extensions refused. The semantic-dilution failure of Section 3, stated as a requirement.

Requirement eleven. One constitution, with every claimed excitation derived from it. The medium must carry a single set of fundamental properties and degrees of freedom, and a field claimed as one of its excitations must follow from those properties rather than from a characteristic introduced for that field alone. Where the medium behaves differently in different regimes, the difference must be traced to the one constitution rather than entered as a further property. A field not so derived must be named as an independent entity and its coupling to the medium stated. Requirements two and three are this condition in the two instances the record forced. Requirement two applies it to a single constitutive parameter and requirement three to a pair of behaviours that appeared to exclude one another. Requirement four carries the same form to the invariance of laws rather than to the constitution of the medium. The record sets two limits on it. The condition is internal consistency together with a stated constitution, and not agreement with the materials already known, since Stokes rejected MacCullagh on the ground that no known material stores energy in the curl of a displacement alone, which was correct in 1839 and false by 1909. And it binds the construction delivered rather than the route taken to it, since Maxwell reached the displacement current and the speed of light through a honeycomb of vortex cells and idle wheels that he discarded on arrival. Kelvin's gyrostatic aether is the anti-pattern, a mechanism assembled to supply a property the medium did not otherwise have, and the Cosserats are the null case, a medium with no constitution named at all.
