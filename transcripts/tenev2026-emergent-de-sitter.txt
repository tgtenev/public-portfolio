Abstract. The Cosmic Fabric Model establishes a continuum-mechanics isomorphism to General Relativity by representing physical space as an elastic three-dimensional hyperplate embedded in a four-dimensional spatial bulk. Reproducing vacuum kinematics requires a vanishing P-wave modulus, which in turn implies a negative bulk modulus, an apparent thermodynamic instability in a closed system. This paradox is resolved by recasting the framework as an open system: the instability is not pathological but instead drives cosmic expansion. In this interpretation, the observable universe is a lower-energy solid formed through continuous solidification from a higher-dimensional precursor fluid. Using multiplicative kinematics from finite-growth mechanics, the model shows that solidification kinetics naturally introduce a rate-dependent bulk viscosity that regularizes the instability into steady expansion. The resulting state maintains a small residual elastic strain while the volume grows at a constant rate, yielding an exact de Sitter expansion for the vacuum sector, the counterpart of the cosmological constant of the standard fit. Because this growth law is local to each material element, large-scale homogeneity and isotropy are not assumed in advance; they emerge from a uniform initial solidification front and the marginal stability of the homogeneous expansion mode. Imposing local frame covariance at the open phase boundary prohibits observable lateral slip, which uniquely constrains the global geometry to a hyperspherical configuration. Observational constraints further exclude a bounded shell, implying a closed, centrosymmetric hypersphere. The open-system formulation also restores a well-posed description of global energy conservation. The framework makes five falsifiable predictions, including two decisive observational tests: a strictly closed spatial geometry, and a dark-energy equation of state approaching w equals minus one in the ideal-reservoir limit, with any deviations directly quantifying non-ideal behavior of the precursor fluid. These results are not adjustable; they follow directly from the governing assumptions and interfacial conditions, and thus render the model definitively testable.

Section 1. Introduction.

The Cosmic Fabric Model, or the fabric model, offers a continuum mechanics perspective on gravity by postulating that physical space behaves as an elastic material. Specifically, the model treats the physical universe as the mid-hypersurface of a four-dimensional hyperplate that is broad in the three ordinary spatial dimensions but thin along a fourth transverse spatial dimension. The original formulation established a mathematical isomorphism between General Relativity and Solid Mechanics.

The elastodynamic approach to spacetime developed herein builds upon an established line of prior work connecting gravitation to vacuum elasticity, thermodynamics, and condensed-matter physics. Sakharov proposed that General Relativity could emerge from the elasticity of the vacuum driven by quantum fluctuations. Later, in a thermodynamic interpretation, Jacobson demonstrated that Einstein's field equations could be derived as an equation of state. Concurrently, condensed-matter analogs such as the superfluid-helium models of Volovik, the World Crystal hypothesis of Kleinert, and recent quantized elastic-medium frameworks of Izabel have mapped Lorentz invariance and gravity to lattice and fluid dynamics.

Advancing the fabric model requires moving from a mathematical isomorphism to an ontological basis rooted in open-system thermodynamics, a shift motivated by the need to resolve a physical paradox. For the hyperplate to parallel the geometric behavior of physical space and to ensure its four-dimensional thickness remains unobservable at the macroscopic scale, the fabric must exhibit zero resistance to uniform volumetric scaling. This requirement dictates a vanishing P-wave modulus, M equals zero, corresponding to a Poisson ratio of exactly unity, nu equals one.

In the original work, we demonstrated the feasibility of such a Poisson ratio by pointing to synthetic, complex fibrous structures, such as compressive dilatant and stretch-densifying materials. While these examples show that nu equals one is mechanically permissible, they are limited when applied to the vacuum of space: each such material attains this Poisson ratio under one sign of volumetric loading only, in compression for the dilatant class, in extension for the stretch-densifying class, not symmetrically in both. An isotropic vacuum capable of sustaining general, three-dimensional gravitational strain requires a different physical foundation.

The requirement that M equals zero introduces a thermodynamic challenge. To support the propagation of transverse mechanical disturbances, the fabric must maintain a positive shear modulus, myu greater than zero. Because the bulk modulus is defined as K equals M minus four-thirds myu, setting M equal to zero forces the intrinsic bulk modulus of the vacuum, that is, of the cosmic fabric itself in its unstrained state, to be negative, K-naught less than zero. In the classical continuum mechanics of closed systems, a negative bulk modulus signals a thermodynamic instability: the material should undergo unbounded volumetric expansion or implosion under a hydrostatic perturbation. Resolving this instability without resorting to geometric constraints that would break the model's alignment with General Relativity requires an updated physical framework.

We address this paradox herein by treating the three-dimensional hyperplate not as a closed equilibrium system, but as an open thermodynamic system maintained by a continuous solid-liquid phase boundary with a four-dimensional absolute space. Instead of viewing the negative bulk modulus as a defect, we postulate that it is the intrinsic state of the fabric, which functions as an energetic solid, a material whose unstrained configuration is not an energy minimum with respect to volumetric deformation. The resulting tendency for expansion is regulated and rate-limited by a continuous phase transition that replenishes the fabric from a surrounding four-dimensional precursor fluid. Eliminating observable lateral forcing at this open interface demands a hyperspherical geometry. By resolving this mechanical instability, we establish a continuum basis for the universe's macroscopic accelerated expansion.

The contributions of this work can be stated compactly. First, the negative bulk modulus paradox is resolved not by repairing the instability but by promoting it: in the open-system ontology, K-naught less than zero becomes the engine of cosmic expansion, and the interface kinetics that regulate it yield an exact de Sitter law whose rate is fixed by material constants, in Sections 2.2 through 2.3, a resolution shown to be necessary, not elective, by exhaustion of the elastic and closed-system alternatives, in Section 3.3. Second, the Cosmological Principle is displaced from its role as a standing assumption: the expansion law is pointwise, and homogeneity enters only as a uniform nucleation state preserved thereafter by marginal stability, in Section 3.1. Third, the open boundary restores a well-posed global energy ledger, making the framework a mechanistic realization of the cosmological constant with a conservation account, in Section 3.2. Fourth, the geometry is forced rather than chosen: eliminating lateral slip at the phase boundary compels a closed hyperspherical topology and thereby a strictly closed spatial geometry, in Sections 2.4 and 3.4. Because each step in this chain is either inherited from the original formulation or forced by the exclusion of alternatives, the framework's conclusions are not adjustable; Section 3.6 collects the five falsifiability commitments to which they expose it.

Section 2.1. Elastic Constraints.

Before evaluating the thermodynamic mechanisms required to stabilize the fabric, we formalize the constraints governing its elastic moduli. The cosmic hyperplate is modeled as a slice of a transversely isotropic four-dimensional continuum. To map its macroscopic deformation, we use a generalized Voigt notation where the four-dimensional symmetric strain vector, written epsilon in brackets, is ordered as the transpose of the list: epsilon-sub-one-one, epsilon-sub-two-two, epsilon-sub-three-three, epsilon-sub-four-four, two times epsilon-sub-two-three, two times epsilon-sub-one-three, two times epsilon-sub-one-two, two times epsilon-sub-one-four, two times epsilon-sub-two-four, and two times epsilon-sub-three-four. The total volumetric strain epsilon represents the localized trace of the three-dimensional spatial strain tensor, defined as the uniform three-dimensional volumetric dilation: epsilon equals epsilon-sub-one-one plus epsilon-sub-two-two plus epsilon-sub-three-three.

Throughout this work, we draw an operational distinction between two independent regimes of volumetric deformation: the static, localized expansive strain induced by local matter defects, epsilon-sub-loc, as treated in the original model formulation, and the homogeneous, global dilation driven by cosmic expansion. For the global mode, we adopt in Section 2.2 the multiplicative kinematics of finite-growth mechanics, which resolves the cosmic dilation into a stress-free growth Jacobian, J-sub-g, tracking the volume created by accretion, and a small residual elastic dilation, epsilon-sub-res, that alone generates stress. Throughout, epsilon-sub-res denotes the instantaneous value of this residual dilation, a dynamical variable; its steady-state value under the balance of Section 2.3 is written epsilon-sub-res-star and is a constitutive constant of the interface. Local mass-energy inclusions cause a localized volumetric expansion strain, epsilon-sub-loc, which can be mapped as a function of the local coordinate metrics. To preserve the invisibility of the fourth dimension under volumetric deformation, we postulate that the cross-dimensional coupling parameter alpha vanishes: alpha equals zero.

This condition is a constitutive postulate, on the same footing as M equals zero, and it is required by thickness invisibility: any non-zero alpha would couple uniform three-dimensional dilation to a change in the hyperplate's transverse thickness, rendering the fourth dimension mechanically observable from within the continuum, and would likewise permit transverse disturbances to drive observable three-dimensional dilation. We emphasize that transversely isotropic materials with alpha not equal to zero are mechanically admissible and stable whenever the stiffness matrix is positive definite; the vanishing coupling is not forced by stability or by symmetry, but is a defining material property of the cosmic fabric. A further consequence is that with alpha equal to zero the in-plane block of the stiffness matrix reduces exactly to isotropic three-dimensional elasticity, so the isotropic definitions of nu and K used above apply without correction.

Furthermore, we require that the four-dimensional transverse stiffness and interdimensional shear dominate the three-dimensional intrinsic shear stiffness: gamma much greater than myu-naught, which is greater than zero, and beta much greater than myu-naught, which is greater than zero. These parameters remain mathematically finite but are treated as structurally asymptotic, acting as dimensional shielding rather than empirical tuning. The stiffness contrast serves two functions. First, it enforces a thin-plate limit that suppresses four-dimensional mechanical degrees of freedom, so transverse perturbations cannot bleed energy out of the localized continuum, preserving three-dimensional Lorentz covariance. Second, because the ratios gamma over myu-naught and beta over myu-naught are decoupled from spatial length scales, their large but finite magnitude establishes a high-energy structural baseline that does not perturb the weak-field limit of General Relativity and reserves a sector for the future modeling of discrete matter as localized, self-confining topological defects, discussed in Section 4.

Pure elasticity alone cannot resolve the K-naught less than zero instability while maintaining the M equals zero condition. Any static geometric boundary constraints or competing elastic restoring forces would alter the effective moduli, destroying the nu equals one requirement and breaking the model's isomorphism with General Relativity. Therefore, a resolution necessitates an open-system dynamic; Section 3.3 establishes this necessity by exhausting the elastic alternatives.

Section 2.2. Open-System Accretion and Phase Transition Kinetics.

To arrest the instability established in Section 2.1, we introduce an accretion model. The four-dimensional absolute space functions as a fluid reservoir, while the physical three-dimensional universe is an embedded solid-state hyperplate. As the hyperplate expands in three dimensions due to its negative bulk modulus, it creates a state of reduced density within the fabric. This expansion pulls the surrounding four-dimensional fluid across the outer interface, forcing it to solidify into the lower-energy three-dimensional crystalline state. Because this liquid-to-solid transformation is accompanied by volumetric expansion rather than the familiar contraction, we refer to it throughout as expansive solidification; where the liquid-to-solid character of the transition matters, we state it explicitly.

The pairing this model asserts, solidification accompanied by volumetric expansion, runs against the everyday intuition that solidifying means contracting, but nature contains a familiar counterexample: water, nearly alone among common substances, expands on freezing by roughly nine percent. The precedent is phenomenological rather than mechanistic. Water's expansion arises from directional bonding building an open lattice, whereas the fabric's expansion is driven by its anti-restoring bulk elasticity, K-naught less than zero, with solidification following as the response: the causal order is reversed, but the pairing is the same, and water shows that nature realizes it. The relevance of water's broader family of solid phases at markedly different compactions to the fabric's own solidified state is taken up in the companion kinetic analysis discussed in Section 4.

Rather than depositing as distinct outer layers that would thicken the hyperplate into a solid four-dimensional sphere, the four-dimensional precursor fluid is drawn interstitially into the expanding three-dimensional lattice. This interstitial accretion process continuously generates new three-dimensional volume while conserving the local rest-mass density, rho-naught, of the fabric. The transverse four-dimensional thickness of the shell remains constant, because the cross-dimensional coupling vanishes, alpha equals zero, and the positive transverse normal stiffness, gamma, resists any deformation along the fourth axis.

Two remarks fix the nomenclature of this accretion-rate equation. First, the driving potential: delta-fai is the transformation driving force of single-component phase-change thermodynamics, the quantity that solidification theory commonly expresses as a chemical-potential difference. We phrase it as a specific, per unit mass, free-energy difference between the phases, since no compositional or electrochemical exchange is involved. Second, the standing of script-K: the accretion-rate equation is a flux-force law, and script-K is its kinetic, or transport, coefficient, the linear coefficient relating the mass flux to its driving force, not a state variable of the material. The internal-state-variable structure of the growth model, in the sense of internal state variable theory, is assigned explicitly following the viscosity relation given below, once the kinematic variable it requires has been introduced.

The accretion-rate equation is, physically, a near-equilibrium reduction. Any two-way exchange process, here, attachment of precursor fluid to the lattice and detachment of solid back into the fluid, yields a linear net-rate law of exactly this form when both directional fluxes greatly exceed their small difference; the reduction has been derived explicitly as the near-equilibrium, low-density limit of an atomistic stochastic-hopping model of a stepped interface. The mobility script-K is then the Onsager-type coefficient of that reduction, the macroscopic counterpart of the step kinetic coefficient of Burton-Cabrera-Frank crystal-growth theory, and the accretion-rate equation is the residual of a much larger two-way turnover rather than a one-way deposition; we return to this decomposition once the steady state is established.

To describe how this accretion generates space, we adopt the multiplicative kinematics of finite-growth, or morphoelastic, mechanics. The local change of volume is measured by the Jacobian, J, defined as the determinant of the deformation gradient F, taken relative to the nucleated reference configuration, and hence equal to the ratio of current volume V to reference volume V-sub-ref. Finite-growth theory splits the deformation gradient multiplicatively into a growth part and an elastic part, F equals F-sub-e times F-sub-g, so that the Jacobian factors correspondingly into a stress-free growth Jacobian, J-sub-g, the determinant of F-sub-g, tracking the volume created by accretion, and an elastic Jacobian, J-sub-e, the determinant of F-sub-e, tracking the recoverable elastic dilation. In symbols: J, defined as V over V-sub-ref, equals J-sub-e times J-sub-g, where J-sub-e equals one plus epsilon-sub-res.

The growth Jacobian J-sub-g tracks the volume created by solidification of the precursor fluid. It is stress-free by construction, because accreted material joins the lattice at the natural rest-mass density rho-naught. The residual elastic dilation epsilon-sub-res measures the deformation of the current material away from its own stress-free state and is the sole source of elastic stress. Because each unit of accreted mass creates stress-free volume at the natural density, mass conservation across the open interface fixes the growth rate to leading order in epsilon-sub-res. This growth-rate relation reads: the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-sub-g equals rho-dot-sub-acc divided by rho-naught.

Sustaining solidification at a finite rate requires holding the interface out of equilibrium: the free-energy difference, delta-fai, is the driving potential, and the finite mobility, script-K, is the resistance. The power dissipated by the transition per unit volume is the specific potential drop carried by the mass flux, delta-fai times rho-dot-sub-acc; expressing this power as a stress acting through the volumetric growth rate, delta-fai times rho-dot-sub-acc equals sigma-sub-diss times the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-sub-g, by the growth-rate relation above, identifies the dissipative stress conjugate to growth as sigma-sub-diss equals rho-naught times delta-fai, the energetic price per unit volume of maintaining the interfacial disequilibrium. Combining the accretion-rate equation and the growth-rate relation then gives delta-fai equals rho-naught over script-K, times the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-sub-g, so the kinetic mobility is mapped to an effective macroscopic bulk viscosity. The viscosity relation reads: sigma-sub-diss equals zeta times the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-sub-g, where zeta is defined as rho-naught squared divided by script-K.

Because script-K greater than zero implies zeta greater than zero, the associated dissipation rate, zeta times the square of the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-sub-g, is non-negative, so the kinetic law is consistent with the second law of thermodynamics. We treat the interface kinetics as linear, consistent with the linear-response regime of continuum phase-boundary theory. While phase transitions can exhibit nonlinear drag under high interface velocities, the present steady-state expansion of the universe is sufficiently rate-bottlenecked for the linear approximation to describe its macroscopic behavior.

These relations complete the internal-state-variable structure of the growth model promised above, and the roles are worth assigning explicitly. The kinematic, strain-like, internal state variable is the accumulated growth, the natural logarithm of J-sub-g: an inelastic, history-carrying variable, the counterpart of the plastic strain of viscoplasticity, and the quantity that carries the growth-rate relation, with the flux law of the accretion-rate equation as its source. Its conjugate thermodynamic force is the dissipative stress, sigma-sub-diss equals rho-naught times delta-fai, identified above. The mobility script-K, equivalently the viscosity zeta, is the kinetic coefficient of the evolution law connecting the force to the rate, and is therefore neither a state variable nor a force. A relation of force to state does also hold in the model, but its coefficient is elastic rather than kinetic: the quasi-static balance of Section 2.3 sets sigma-sub-diss equal to the absolute value of K-naught times epsilon-sub-res, so the driving force is proportional to the residual elastic strain with the stiffness, the absolute value of K-naught, not one over script-K, as the constant of proportionality. The growth element is thus of Maxwell type: an anti-restoring elastic spring in series with a viscous dashpot, with the spring sustaining, rather than relaxing, the steady flow through the dashpot.

Accretion is not the only dissipative channel available at the open interface. The dilating fabric also does work against the acoustic impedance of the precursor fluid, radiating energy into the bulk without any accompanying mass flux; this contributes an additional drag of magnitude zeta-sub-rad, comparable to rho-sub-f times c-sub-f times L, set by the fluid's density rho-sub-f and wave speed c-sub-f. The two channels, however, respond to different quantities: radiation drag responds to the instantaneous dilation rate, whereas the accretion flux responds, through delta-fai, to the accumulated density deficit of the solid. Any shortfall of solidification relative to dilation deepens the deficit, raises delta-fai, and accelerates solidification until the shortfall is erased: with a reservoir of fixed state and any non-zero mobility script-K, full replenishment is the attractor of the coupled kinetics, and the rest-mass density remains pinned at rho-naught regardless of the radiative admixture, the same deficit-driven feedback underlies the relaxation toward epsilon-sub-res-star discussed in Section 3.1. The postulate of Section 2.3 in fact removes this channel from the homogeneous balance altogether: a phase front that consumes static fluid in place displaces no fluid, so the uniform background dilation radiates nothing, and acoustic drag acts only on deviations, the shape perturbations of Sections 3.1 and 3.4 and the fluid-borne signals of Section 3.5. The two-channel family remains instructive as the space of rival variants, and Section 3.3 examines it and its observational discriminants.

Section 2.3. Emergent de Sitter Expansion.

The negative intrinsic bulk modulus, K-naught equals minus the absolute value of K-naught, renders the stress-free state unstable: any infinitesimal dilational perturbation of the nucleated solid generates a destabilizing volumetric stress, K-naught times epsilon-sub-res, negative for dilation and hence pressure-like rather than restoring, that drives further dilation; in a closed system this instability is rate-unbounded. In the open system, dilation of the lattice produces the reduced-density state that draws in the precursor fluid, and the interface kinetics of Section 2.2 convert elastic dilation into stress-free grown volume at a finite rate. Quasi-static stress equilibrium of the fabric, the elastic driving stress balanced by the dissipative interface stress, K-naught times epsilon-sub-res plus sigma-sub-diss equals zero, yields the governing balance: zeta times the time derivative of the natural logarithm of J-sub-g equals the absolute value of K-naught, times epsilon-sub-res.

The first-order form of this balance, stress equilibrium with no inertial term, is not an overdamped approximation but a statement of principle, which we elevate to a postulate of the open-system framework, alongside the three postulates of the original formulation. Intuitively, it extends the first of those postulates, which casts physical space as an elastic hyperplate and thereby sets the expectation that gravity is an emergent property of the fabric: the present postulate extends the same emergence to inertia, which attaches to the fabric's deviations rather than to its bulk.

Postulate, the Background Datum. The homogeneous state of the fabric, its uniform density rho-naught, its uniform residual strain, and the entire trajectory of its configuration through the embedding space, position, velocity, and acceleration alike, constitutes the dynamical datum of the theory. The datum carries energy but no momentum, and its evolution, however accelerated, is resisted by no inertia: collective motion of the background is evolution of the datum itself, governed entirely by the first-order interface kinetics of Section 2.2. Inertia is an emergent property confined to the fabric: it attaches exclusively to deviations from the datum, wave excitations, shape deformations, and mass-energy inclusions, and deviation dynamics is defined relative to the co-moving, co-accelerating datum frame, with inclusions acquiring their effective inertia from the deformation fields they carry.

Corollary, Configurational Thermodynamics. Outside the fabric, only energy and strain have dynamical standing: the background's bulk trajectory has no inertial consequence for anything the fabric carries. And since momentum and kinetic energy are inertia's own currency, the kinetic reading of thermal behavior goes with the postulate: temperature as mean microscopic kinetic energy, heat as its exchange, and any bath defined by either are alike undefined for the precursor, the interface, and the fabric's bulk motion through both. What remains meaningful there is the configurational sector alone: geometry, configurational, or strain, energy and its conjugate stresses, and entropy in its state-counting rather than kinetic sense, the same split ordinary thermodynamics already draws between configurational and vibrational contributions.

The term datum is used in its engineering and geodesy sense: a reference relative to which position and deviation are defined, and which is not itself an object of measurement; it carries no connotation of, quote, data, end quote. The postulate designates the homogeneous background as the reference entity of the dynamics, not a participant in it.

The postulate obligates a reading of the transverse wave-speed relation, c equals the square root of myu-naught over rho-naught, in which rho-naught plays no bulk-inertial role, and that reading is the one relativistic continuum mechanics prefers in any case: in a relativistic medium, wave speeds are ratios of elastic moduli to the energy density per c squared, not to a Newtonian rest-mass density. Writing u-naught, defined as rho-naught times c squared, for the fabric's rest energy density, the symbol u is reserved for energy density, the kinetic coefficient of the deviation wave equation is the intrinsic quantity u-naught over c squared, a property of in-fabric dynamics carrying no reference to motion through the bulk, and the wave-speed relation becomes the constitutive statement myu-naught equals u-naught: the shear modulus equals the energy density. This is a principled condition rather than a tuning. For any relativistic medium, causality bounds the rigidity by myu less than or equal to u, shear-wave speed at most c, so the condition states that the fabric is maximally rigid, as stiff as causality permits, and transverse waves propagate at exactly c as a theorem. A membrane whose tension equals its energy density carries transverse waves at exactly c for the same reason, and a restoring modulus equal to the energy density is the elastic expression of the w equals minus one equation of state to which the framework is independently committed, discussed in Section 3.3. The wave dynamics of the original formulation is preserved verbatim, with rho-naught, defined as u-naught over c squared, retained as bookkeeping.

Maximal rigidity also constrains the fabric's own microstructure. Saturating the causal bound myu less than or equal to u requires every unit of the fabric's cohesive energy to bear shear affinely, and architecturally open networks cannot do so: their openness is stabilized by directional, angular, constraints holding volume that carries no shear load, and they generically support soft collective, rigid-unit, modes that depress the shear modulus below the energy bound. The fabric's cohesion is therefore of the dense, central-force-dominated class, for which the maximally coordinated packing is also the densest geometrically accessible one, the classical result that disordered sphere packings are strictly less dense than close-packed lattices. This constrains, from the postulate alone, what kind of solid the fabric structurally is, a constraint the future microphysical determination of epsilon-sub-res-star deferred below must respect.

Five consequences follow.

First, the growth-rate balance is exact as a first-order law rather than an approximation awaiting justification, the same status held by the kinetic relations of continuum phase-transformation theory, in which the transformation coordinate carries no inertia. In the original formulation the background was static, so the question of its momentum never arose; the expanding extension forces the choice.

Second, accretion proceeds by transmutation in place: the interface consumes the adjacent static fluid where it stands, and no force of any kind is required to bring accreted mass onto the background's accelerating trajectory, because motion with the datum is not a dynamical state to be maintained but the reference from which dynamics is measured. The background expansion requires no bulk flow of the reservoir.

Third, the background's bulk trajectory is unobservable from within: uniform velocity and acceleration alike leave no dynamical imprint on the fabric's contents. This extends to the accelerated trajectory the operational hiding of the fabric's rest frame derived in the companion Lorentz-invariance paper, and parallels the equivalence principle: the local inertial frames of the theory are, by postulate, the frames riding the datum. The trajectory itself is not arbitrary; the interface kinetics of Section 2.2 determines it, and the postulate states only that inertia references it.

Fourth, the resulting structure mirrors the theory the model reproduces: in General Relativity the homogeneous mode likewise obeys a first-order, Friedmann, constraint, while finite-wavelength perturbations obey second-order wave equations. The postulate extends to momentum and inertia the datum principle that governs gravitation in Section 3.2, where the uniform background defines the zero of curvature and only deviations gravitate.

Fifth, the same accounting closes the reservoir's thermal status: with no momentum or kinetic-energy channel surviving the datum, the precursor could neither receive nor deliver thermal agitation even in principle, so it supports no bath and no temperature, consistent with the treatment of the reservoir in Section 3.2 as an ideal reservoir of unbounded capacity and fixed state, a bath in the configurational sense only. Section 2.4. The Hyperspherical Shell Topology.

An essential kinematic condition of this expansion framework is the preservation of Lorentz invariance. The fabric model already possesses an ontological rest frame, that of the fabric itself, and the companion derivation in the Lorentz-invariance paper shows that the Lorentz transformations and the phenomenological equivalence of moving observers follow from the fabric's material postulates despite this frame, in the Lorentz-Poincaré sense: the rest frame exists but is operationally hidden. What the expansion must therefore avoid is not a preferred frame as such, but observable, position-dependent lateral forcing at the phase boundary, which would break the operational invariance derived there. We must accordingly examine the boundary velocities relative to the four-dimensional absolute space.

The geometric setting is inherited from the original formulation: the fabric is a smooth three-dimensional hypersurface embedded in a flat four-dimensional Euclidean space, the continuum idealization already underlying the elastic constraints developed in Section 2.1. To this we add a single new physical assumption: the precursor fluid is static, at rest in some global frame of the embedding space, with no bulk flow, shear, or rotation. Section 3.4 examines the status of this assumption and the observable consequences of relaxing it.

If the four-dimensional precursor fluid were modeled as a static Euclidean background through which a flat three-dimensional solid plate expands, the scenario would mirror the stretching sheet problem in fluid dynamics. Because the plate expands uniformly in all directions, there must exist a center point where the relative velocity is zero. From this center, the lateral velocity of the expanding plate relative to the surrounding static fluid increases linearly with distance. Lateral slip at an accreting phase boundary is a kinetic and energetic perturbation: it renders the interfacial disequilibrium, and with it the local accretion rate and dissipation, position- and direction-dependent, manifesting observationally as a directional preferred-frame effect.

This flat-plate scenario generalizes to a sharp statement. In the steady state every fabric element creates volume at the common specific rate three H-naught, discussed in Section 2.3, so the configuration expands self-similarly about some fixed center in the reservoir frame: a material point at position x relative to that center moves with velocity v equals H-naught times x. A superposed rigid drift or rotation of the configuration relative to the static fluid would itself constitute lateral slip and is excluded by the same requirement invoked below, so the center is at rest in the reservoir frame. For this motion to produce no lateral slip against the static fluid, v must be normal to the fabric at every point; since v is parallel to x, this requires x dotted with t to equal zero for every direction t tangent to the fabric, which holds precisely when the squared magnitude of x is constant along the connected hypersurface. The only such configurations are subsets of round hyperspheres centered on the expansion center. Zero lateral slip therefore forces sphericity outright: no symmetry of the embedding is assumed, and no non-spherical shape, however regular, survives the requirement. What the argument does not by itself supply is closure: the interior of a bounded spherical cap, a partial shell terminating in a free rim, expands with purely normal velocity exactly as the complete hypersphere does. Section 3.4 excludes such bounded configurations in the current epoch on independent observational and kinetic grounds, completing the argument: the fabric must be a closed hypersurface, the complete round hypersphere.

The current-epoch configuration is therefore a centrosymmetric hyperspherical shell, hereafter the hypershell: three-dimensional space is a closed expanding spherical shell within the radially symmetric fluid reservoir, with the geometric center of expansion shifted off the fabric entirely and into the geometric center of the shell within the four-dimensional bulk. The full O-four symmetry of this configuration is an output of the slip requirement, not an input, discussed in Section 3.4.

This topology requires defining the thermodynamic state of the enclosed four-dimensional volume. What the linear constitutive balance requires of the interior is a constant boundary condition on the inner face: a time-varying inward traction would break the steady de Sitter profile derived in the expansion-rate equation. An interior held at the reservoir state cannot satisfy this: the internal volume grows as R to the fourth, so an enclosed body of precursor fluid at that state suffers a continuing drop in pressure and exerts precisely the time-dependent traction the balance forbids. Two interior states do satisfy it. The first is a four-dimensional geometric void, exerting no traction at any epoch. The second is a dilute remnant of the fluid at the saturation state, the state whose specific free energy equals that of the solid, fai-sub-int equals fai-sub-solid, which the two-way interface exchange of Section 2.2 makes the natural fixed point of any enclosed fluid: an interior denser than saturation solidifies net onto the inner face, one rarer than saturation is replenished by net detachment, and at the fixed point the inner face carries gross exchange with zero net flux. A saturated remnant exerts a small constant traction that shifts the operating strain epsilon-sub-res-star without disturbing the constancy of the expansion rate; departures from the fixed point relax by the same interface kinetics and, while they last, enter the balance as a slowly decaying perturbation whose observational signature is a correspondingly decaying deviation from w equals minus one, discussed in Section 3.6. In what follows we adopt the void as the interior idealization; every conclusion below holds for either inert interior, the saturated remnant mattering only as the natural endpoint of a formation history that encloses fluid at closure, discussed in Section 3.4.

Consequently, net accretion occurs exclusively across the outer three-dimensional hypersurface facing the fluid reservoir, trivially for the void interior, and because the inner face carries zero net flux for the saturated remnant. This is consistent with the interstitial accretion of Section 2.2: because the hyperplate is thin along the fourth dimension, the outer hypersurface is adjacent to every point of the three-dimensional continuum, so the accretion is surface-mediated with respect to the transverse dimension while remaining volumetrically uniform with respect to the three ordinary dimensions. Because the inert interior exerts zero, for the void, or constant, for the saturated remnant, traction, the mechanical boundary conditions remain constant regardless of the expansion scale. From the perspective of a localized observer residing within the hyperplate, there is zero lateral relative velocity between the three-dimensional hyperplate points and the adjacent four-dimensional precursor fluid, ensuring local kinematics remain isotropic and the operational Lorentz invariance derived in the companion paper is preserved.

The scope of the present work is the late-time acceleration mechanism: we derive the existence and rate of a steady expansion from the vacuum's material properties, treating the matter content as a perturbation carried by the expanding fabric rather than as a driver of the background dynamics. The derived expansion is exactly de Sitter and the observed expansion is not, the deceleration parameter q-naught is approximately minus zero point five five; the two are compatible in precisely the way they are compatible in the standard model, where the capital lambda component is itself exactly de Sitter and the observed deceleration parameter is the imprint of the matter riding on it. The mechanism derived here supplies the counterpart of the capital lambda component: the non-diluting, w equals minus one, sector of the expansion. Section 3.1. Evaluation Against the Standard Cosmological Model.

The Standard Cosmological Model, known in the literature as Lambda C D M, relies on the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric, an open architecture capable of sustaining various expansion profiles depending on input density parameters, and on the Cosmological Principle, which mandates that the universe be homogeneous and isotropic on large scales. If the Cosmological Principle were violated by sufficiently massive localized structures, the symmetry assumptions underlying that machinery would be undermined, subject to the caveat, familiar from the homogenization of heterogeneous solids, that localized structure threatens the framework only if it fails to average out at the largest scales.

The fabric model predicts a steady de Sitter expansion from material elastodynamics, and it accommodates large-scale structural anomalies that challenge the Cosmological Principle: an expanding matrix does not cease to obey macroscopic thermodynamics when it contains localized defects. Reported phenomena such as non-random parity violations in galaxy spin directions, observations still under active debate, or gigaparsec-scale structures such as the Giant Arc and the Big Ring, would, if confirmed, emerge as localized stress concentrations within the global continuum, coexisting with rather than modifying the uniform drive of the expansion.

The model's relation to the Cosmological Principle differs structurally from that of the Standard Model. The fabric model does not require the matter content of the universe to be homogeneous or isotropic at any scale: the expansion law of the governing balance is a local constitutive statement, obeyed pointwise by every fabric element, rather than a global metric solution whose validity depends on a symmetric source distribution. What the derivation does assume is uniformity of the fabric itself, in two distinct senses. The first is material homogeneity, uniform constitutive properties, rho-naught, myu-naught, K-naught, and script-K, the standard idealization of any continuum theory. The second is a uniform initial state: the solid is taken to nucleate in a maximally symmetric deformation state, so that a single scalar balance governs every element. Homogeneity of the expansion state therefore enters as an initial condition, not as a prediction of the model.

The model does, however, supply a dynamical statement about how such uniformity, once present, evolves, and the two state variables behave differently. Perturbations of the residual strain, delta-epsilon-sub-res, are self-correcting: a region strained above epsilon-sub-res-star maintains a larger interfacial driving potential, delta-fai, so accretion consumes its excess dilation faster and returns it toward the balance point, while a region below epsilon-sub-res-star is re-dilated by the dominant instability. The local expansion rate therefore relaxes toward the common value, three H-naught equals the absolute value of K-naught times epsilon-sub-res-star, divided by zeta, everywhere. This restoring behavior follows from the signs of the kinetics; its formal demonstration belongs with the microphysical determination of epsilon-sub-res-star deferred in Section 2.3. Perturbations of the accumulated growth, delta of the natural logarithm of J-sub-g, are by contrast exactly frozen: because the P-wave modulus vanishes, M equals zero, a local dilational disturbance generates no in-plane gradient stresses and does not propagate to neighboring elements, and because the growth rate is independent of accumulated growth, a region that has created fractionally more volume retains that fraction indefinitely. Inhomogeneities in the fabric's expansion state are thus neither amplified nor erased: a marginal stability weaker than the active smoothing of inflationary dynamics but stronger than instability, and precisely the property that lets the global expansion proceed unmodified amid localized structure. Matter inclusions couple to the balance only through the modulus dependence of the shear-softening relation; this second-order back-reaction is deferred to future work.

State homogeneity is consequently not necessary for the mechanism itself: a quasi-uniform state breaks nothing, since every element still executes the same steady growth and the energy ledger of Section 3.2, being local, closes regardless. Gross non-uniformity would degrade only the phenomenological layer built on the mechanism. First, non-uniform accumulated growth renders the shell aspherical, and by the slip argument of Section 2.4 the resulting lateral velocity against the reservoir reintroduces the anisotropic phase-boundary drag that the round shell eliminates. This qualifies the frozen-growth statement in one respect: growth perturbations are exactly frozen with respect to the fabric's internal elastic coupling, which vanishes at M equals zero, but growth patterns that deform the shell's shape couple to the reservoir through the slip they induce, and the fluid, not the fabric, damps shape modes toward the zero-slip spherical configuration. The observed isotropy of the universe therefore bounds the permissible asphericity, an observational constraint on the nucleation state that the drag channel relaxes over time rather than amplifies. Second, the single scale factor, round geometry, and single curvature parameter are exact only in the uniform limit; a quasi-uniform state yields a perturbed closed geometry, in parallel with the perturbed Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metrics of standard practice.

Because the stability of the homogeneous mode is only marginal, however, the model cannot manufacture smoothness: whatever large-scale uniformity is observed today must already have been present in the nucleated solid. Where inflationary dynamics actively smooths generic initial conditions, the fabric model inherits its smoothness from the nucleation event, which the theory does not model. The Cosmological-Principle-like input is thereby confined to a single initial event and preserved by the dynamics thereafter, rather than imposed as a standing assumption about the matter distribution; it remains an input, as stated above.

The selection of a hyperspherical manifold, derived herein rather than assumed, likewise invokes no Cosmological Principle. Where the standard model's closed geometry is a metric consequence of an assumed uniform matter distribution, the fabric model's hyperspherical topology is a kinematic boundary condition, required to preserve local Lorentz covariance by eliminating lateral phase-boundary drag; it therefore remains valid in the presence of localized heterogeneities, defects, or anisotropic stress concentrations, because the expansion is a pointwise material process rather than a solution predicated on global symmetry.

The open-system framework also provides a distinct thermodynamic perspective relative to standard Dark Energy models. In Lambda C D M, Dark Energy maintains a constant energy density as the universe expands, yet because General Relativity lacks a global time-translation Killing vector in an expanding metric, global energy is not conserved. The fabric model instead replaces the isolated cosmological-constant source term with a mass-energy flux across an open boundary, the four-dimensional precursor fluid serving as the reservoir that restores global energy conservation; Section 3.2 exhibits the ledger explicitly.

Because it maintains a constant vacuum density by continuous creation, the model invites comparison with the steady-state cosmology of Bondi, Gold, and Hoyle, and the comparison is instructive precisely because the classical falsifiers do not transfer. Steady-state cosmology held the matter density constant through continuous matter creation, predicting a universe without cosmic evolution; it was refuted by the cosmic microwave background and by radio source counts, which demonstrated that the matter content evolves. The fabric model creates fabric, space itself, at the constant specific rate three H-naught rho-naught, discussed in Section 2.3, while the matter content dilutes and evolves exactly as in standard cosmology: galaxies age, structure grows, and a hot early phase is not precluded. The observations that eliminated steady-state cosmology constrain the matter content, which the present framework leaves conventional.

Section 3.3. The Necessity of the Open-System Resolution.

The claim that the negative bulk modulus necessitates an open-system resolution, discussed in Section 2.1, deserves a precise logical statement, because its premises differ in kind from adjustable modeling choices. The elastic character of the vacuum is fixed entirely by the fabric model's defining requirements, inherited from the original formulation: the invisibility of the fourth dimension at the continuum scale, and the isomorphism with General Relativity. Invisibility under uniform scaling requires the P-wave modulus to vanish and the cross-dimensional coupling to vanish; the propagation of transverse disturbances, light and gravitational waves, requires myu greater than zero. The negative bulk modulus then follows algebraically. At this level there are no alternatives to consider: relaxing any premise abandons the model's identity rather than adjusting a parameter.

Given these premises, the instability cannot be arrested within elasticity, and the demonstration consists of exhausting the known stabilization mechanisms. First, constraint stabilization, a negative-stiffness phase held stable by a stiff surrounding structure, as established by Lakes and Drugan, is unavailable: the only stiff structure in the model is the transverse one, gamma and beta, which the invisibility postulate, alpha equals zero, decouples from three-dimensional dilation; conversely, any cross-coupling strong enough to stabilize would render the thickness response observable and shift the effective in-plane moduli away from nu equals one, so either horn violates a defining requirement. Second, strain-gradient and nonlocal regularizations stabilize only finite-wavelength perturbations; the homogeneous dilational mode carries no gradients and is untouched. Third, nonlinear stiffening of K as a function of epsilon would arrest the instability at a static post-buckled equilibrium whose moduli no longer satisfy nu equals one, contradicting both the isomorphism and the observed ongoing expansion. Fourth, inertia alone selects no rate and no timescale, and the fabric's collective motion carries no momentum and is resisted by no inertia, by the Background Datum postulate, discussed in Section 2.3. The conclusion is forced: some rate-limiting dissipative mechanism must supplement the elastic description.

The choice of dissipative channel requires a finer analysis, because dissipation mechanisms other than accretion exist and regularize the instability equally well at the level of the governing balance. Two are available. An intrinsic bulk viscosity of the fabric itself, a bare material constant, in contrast to the derived interface coefficient identified earlier, would regularize the instability with no reservoir at all; but with nothing replenishing the density, dilution weakens the moduli by order one per e-fold of expansion, via the shear-softening relation, the drive consumes itself, and the dissipated energy has no sink outside the fabric. The variant sustains neither a steady rate nor a steady material state, and it forfeits the reservoir on which the nucleation origin, the energy ledger, and the geometric selection of Section 2.4 all rely. Radiation damping into the precursor fluid, discussed in Section 2.2, is the serious rival: it retains the reservoir ontology, but by exerting drag on the homogeneous mode it grants the fluid the dynamical purchase on the background, momentum transfer without mass, that the Background Datum postulate denies; as a variant it relaxes that postulate.

The general model therefore carries both channels, and their competition can be parametrized by the replenishment fraction kai, ranging from zero to one: the share of newly created volume whose rest mass is supplied by solidification. The density then evolves as rho-dot over rho equals minus three times the quantity one minus kai, times H, the moduli drift with it, and the effective dark-energy equation of state deviates from minus one by an amount of order one minus kai. The two pure variants are the endpoints: kai equals one is the open-system model of Section 2, with w equals minus one exactly, while kai equals zero is the purely radiative closed variant, with a strongly drifting quasi-de Sitter expansion. As argued in Section 2.2, however, kai is not a free parameter of the ideal-reservoir model: because the accretion flux responds to the accumulated density deficit while radiation drag responds only to the instantaneous rate, any shortfall in replenishment self-corrects, and kai equals one is the attractor of the coupled kinetics. Sustaining kai less than one would require suppressing mass transfer altogether, script-K equals zero, a fine-tuned prohibition at an interface that is in contact, out of equilibrium, and freely exchanging mass and energy, or a reservoir of drifting state: finite capacity, or a phase free energy evolving under the dissipation it absorbs.

This converts the channel selection from a modeling preference into a prediction with a diagnostic. Should late-time observations favor an evolving dark-energy equation of state, the drifting variant is the framework's own natural extension rather than a rival construction. Both statements rest on assumptions this paper makes explicitly, the ideal reservoir of Section 3.2 and the deficit-driven kinetics of the accretion-rate equation, and both are exposed to observation.

A final alternative concerns the drive rather than the dissipation: a conventional material with a positive bulk modulus, K greater than zero, under a uniform external tensile traction would also expand. Such a model, however, postulates an independent, unobserved energetic mechanism operating entirely outside our universe in the four-dimensional bulk, and a static external pull cannot organically generate the rate-dependent viscosity required for a steady de Sitter profile without specialized tuning. The open-system ontology instead treats the negative bulk modulus as intrinsic to the energetic fabric: the expansion is driven internally by the vacuum's native elastodynamics and balanced by the phase-boundary kinetics, and the model remains self-contained.

Section 3.4. The Necessity of Closure and the Reservoir Frame.

Section 2.4 derived the sphericity of the fabric from the zero-slip requirement and deferred closure to this section, because the slip argument cannot supply it: a bounded spherical cap, a partial shell with a free rim, expands with purely normal velocity on its interior exactly as the complete hypersphere does. Closure in the current epoch rests instead on three independent exclusions of a persistent bounded configuration. First, only a closed hypersurface protects the inert interior of Section 2.4, void or saturated remnant, topologically. A cap's concave side is connected to the reservoir around its rim: it floods at the reservoir state, and the flooded region must then be fed continuously through the mouth as the cap expands, a position-dependent filling flow whose tangential slip near the rim is of order the radial expansion speed, R-dot greater than roughly thirty-one point six c, discussed in Section 3.5, in catastrophic conflict with the roughly ten to the minus five isotropy tolerance of Section 3.6. Second, a free material edge is directly observable in its own right: waves reflect from it, edge-localized modes exist, and its direction on the sky is distinguished. Escaping these signatures requires the rim to lie beyond the causal horizon, an empirical accident rather than a structural protection. Third, and most telling on naturalness grounds, a permanent rim would have to be kinetically passivated. Edge sites are the most favorable attachment sites of a solidifying phase, so a configuration whose faces accrete while its rim does not requires the interface mobility to vanish exactly there, script-K equals zero on the rim, the geometric mirror image of the fine-tuned pure-radiation channel excluded in Section 3.3. With sphericity already forced and a bounded shell excluded, the current-epoch fabric must be the complete, closed hypersphere.

These exclusions are statements about the current, observationally constrained epoch; none of them binds a formation epoch that predates all observation. During formation a bounded shell is not only admissible but plausibly instrumental. A first-order phase transition nucleates finite solid patches, not complete closed surfaces, and the rim of a growing patch, being its most kinetically favorable attachment site, is precisely where accretion advances fastest. Rim growth therefore carries a bounded shell naturally toward closure, and once the advance of the rim outpaces the stretching of its mouth by the background expansion, closure completes in a self-reinforcing manner. On this picture the closed topology of the current epoch is not an initial condition imposed by hand but the natural endpoint of the nucleation dynamics, with the pre-closure inhomogeneities, the rim and its feeding flow, confined to the pre-observational transient, in the same epistemic position as the nucleation-state smoothness of Section 3.1. Closure necessarily encloses a body of reservoir-state fluid; its disposition, relaxation to the saturation state of Section 2.4 or consumption to a void, is a transient of the same epoch, with the decaying w-signature of Section 3.6 its only potentially surviving trace. The quantitative development of this formation epoch, patch growth and buckling, the edge kinetics that set the closure rate, the relaxation of the enclosed fluid, and the selection of a single dominant nucleus over many, is deferred to a dedicated future paper; the present work is confined to the post-closure epoch.

The one genuinely new physical assumption of Section 2.4 is the static reservoir, and its status should be stated with equal candor: it is exposed to observation, not protected by construction. A reservoir with a bulk velocity relative to the shell's center adds a coherent slip component across the fabric, maximal where the fabric's normal motion opposes the stream, so the drag, and with it the local expansion rate, acquires a dipole-type modulation on the sky, distinct in origin from the kinematic dipole of observer motion. Internal velocity structure of the reservoir, shear, rotation, inflow, would imprint correspondingly higher multipoles. Section 3.6 records the resulting bound. The remaining logical alternative, an entrained reservoir whose velocity field co-moves with a non-spherical expanding fabric so that slip vanishes despite the shape, is mathematically available but requires fine-tuning the fluid's motion to the fabric's configuration; we set it aside on naturalness grounds rather than necessity. Relaxing the static-reservoir assumption in a controlled way, a uniform reservoir wind, is the natural first extension of the framework should future anisotropy measurements demand one.

Finally, we record what is not assumed. No symmetry of the embedding is imposed anywhere in the construction: sphericity follows from the slip requirement alone, discussed in Section 2.4, and the full O-four symmetry of the final configuration, the round S-three is the only compact three-manifold admitting a fully O-four-symmetric embedding in flat E-four, is an output of the analysis. The smoothness of the fabric and the flatness of the embedding space are not additional cosmological hypotheses but the continuum idealization and geometric setting already adopted in the original formulation, retained here unchanged.

Section 3.6. Falsifiability.

The framework makes several commitments that observation can refute or measure, which we collect here.

First, the spatial geometry is strictly closed. The hypershell topology requires Omega-sub-k less than zero, with magnitude fixed by the shell radius: the absolute value of Omega-sub-k equals the square of the quantity R-sub-H over R. The flatness bound, the absolute value of Omega-sub-k less than roughly ten to the minus three, currently enters as a constraint on R, discussed in Section 3.5, but it becomes a discriminant as precision improves: a definitive measurement of Omega-sub-k greater than or equal to zero, an open or exactly flat geometry, falsifies the topology outright, while a detection of negative curvature near the ten-to-the-minus-three level, within reach of forthcoming surveys, would corroborate the closed geometry and measure the shell radius directly.

Second, the dark-energy equation of state is exactly w equals minus one, without evolution, in the ideal-reservoir limit, discussed in Section 3.3. Section 4. Conclusion and Avenues for Future Work.

In this work, we advanced the Cosmic Fabric Model into an open-system thermodynamic framework to resolve the instability, K-naught less than zero, required by the vacuum's vanishing P-wave modulus. By modeling the four-dimensional embedding space as a reservoir of a precursor fluid, we demonstrated that the hyperplate's tendency to expand is regulated by the continuous accretion of this fluid. The interface kinetics of this phase transition act as an effective bulk viscosity that rate-limits the instability: accretion maintains a small, steady residual elastic strain, and the resulting constant growth rate yields an exact de Sitter expansion. This thermodynamic reframing provides a mechanical explanation for macroscopic accelerated expansion, restores a well-posed framework for global energy conservation, and mandates a hypershell topology to preserve Lorentz covariance. Furthermore, reconciling this geometry with empirical flat-space observations defines a high-velocity, high-energy baseline for the four-dimensional embedding environment relative to the three-dimensional continuum.

This open-system baseline grounds a broader research program focused on the dynamics of the deviations the background carries: the damping of shape perturbations by reservoir drag, and especially the response of the interface kinetics to the strain fields of mass-energy inclusions. Under the Background Datum postulate the background's own acceleration exerts no force on inclusions, so any galactic-scale phenomenology must be carried by intrinsic geometry rather than inertial coupling, and the interface supplies a natural channel. A companion kinetic analysis, in preparation, models the interface's step-mediated kinetics explicitly, in the spirit of Burton-Cabrera-Frank crystal-growth theory, and derives the resulting rotation-curve phenomenology; it preliminarily brackets the residual strain epsilon-sub-res-star left undetermined in Section 2.3 to between three times ten to the minus eight and two times ten to the minus six, an unpublished estimate quoted as a consistency target for the present framework's own future microphysical treatment.
